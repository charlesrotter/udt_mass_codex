# G212 lay report — did the whiteboard find the missing bridge?

Yes, but it found two different things hiding under the word “history.”

If we possess enough complete observer-pair measurements, they already describe the metric. We do
not need to choose a second universe afterward. That part of the missing-history problem was a
category error already anticipated by G129--G130.

What we do **not** yet have is a rule that generates every measurement at every separation from
two or three constants. Reciprocity makes the measurements reverse and compose correctly, but it
does not determine the melody. Infinitely many smooth melodies obey those rules.

G211 nevertheless gives a strong new local result: two observers/clocks that cut through the
spatial sector differently can determine both scalar volume controls at that point. In other
words, a sufficiently rich chord can reveal the local instrument settings. Repeating this over
distance reconstructs the changing score, although it uses information all along the score.

The attractive shortcut “the same law for every observer must force one universal curved
spacetime” was tested. It works only if we assume the **entire local geometry**, in every direction,
is identical around every observer. That would force constant curvature and leave roughly one
curvature scale. UDT currently does not make that strong assumption, and real angular/mixing
structure gives us good reason not to sneak it in.

So the next step remains native and concrete: finish counting the determinant-preserving spatial
instruments, then test whether a finite set of completed observer-pair chords reconstructs all ten
local metric components. If it does, the local kernel-to-metric bridge is complete. The later
question of predicting the universe from only a few anchors remains separate and honestly open.
