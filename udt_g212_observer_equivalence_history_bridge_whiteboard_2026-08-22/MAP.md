# G212 map — from a chord to an evolving score

## The exact gap

G176 fixes how a supplied complete pair pullback is reciprocally calibrated. G211 proves that the
local diagonal scalar sector has two independent modes. Neither result says how those modes vary
from one separation to another.

The bridge question is not “which arbitrary metric should be selected?” It is whether requiring
the *same physical pair law for every observer*, together with exact composition and global metric
realizability, removes the arbitrary functions.

## Three levels

```text
one pair germ
    -> one completed reciprocal chord

separation-labelled pair family
    -> an ordered sequence of chords

global observer network
    -> every pair family agrees on overlaps and middle observers
```

Composition may make the sequence a potential difference. Global realizability may make the
network reconstruct a metric. Neither fact alone proves that the potential or metric is unique.

## Candidate bridge hiding in plain sight

Spherical symmetry about one selected observer leaves arbitrary radial profiles. Isotropy about
*every* observer is much stronger. Under standard regularity and connectedness hypotheses, local
isotropy at every point can force constant sectional curvature, reducing an arbitrary profile to a
finite-modulus family.

G212 will test both the theorem and ownership of its hypotheses. A beautiful theorem built on an
unowned premise remains conditional; an arbitrary-function counterfamily passing the current
premises ends the claim that further local bookkeeping will make the history emerge automatically.
