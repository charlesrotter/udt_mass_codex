# Lay report

We answered a very specific question: can the UDT clock/distance depth and the metric's curvature be
parts of the **same little patch of geometry**, or had our previous calculations accidentally put
them in two unrelated mathematical worlds?

They can be parts of the same patch. In the two richest registered metric families, you can hold the
local clock/distance slope fixed and still build every locally possible kind of curvature by using
the remaining angular and cross-coupling second derivatives. That works whether the depth slope is
ordinary, time-like, space-like, light-like, or zero.

The important catch is about labels. Our current way of writing the metric splits it into a
"depth part" and a "reference frame part." If the reference part is allowed to change, some depth
can be moved from one factor to the other without changing the finished metric at all. It is like
describing the same finished wall by shifting inches between the stud measurement and the spacer
measurement: the wall stays put, but the bookkeeping changes.

So we learned two things at once:

1. **Good news:** depth and curvature are locally compatible in one geometry. The earlier work was
   not combining mutually impossible pieces.
2. **Honest limitation:** the finished metric still does not tell us, by itself, which bookkeeping
   split is the physical UDT depth field. We need a native rule tying observer comparison to a
   complete coframe across spacetime.

The angular sector really matters. A reciprocal-only metric reaches only 8 of the 20 curvature
directions. Locking one angular or shift response to it raises that to 10. Giving the angular and
mixing sectors independent room reaches all 20. That is evidence of an "orchestra" interaction, but
not yet the score that tells the orchestra what to play.

The next justified question is therefore not another local rank search. It is whether UDT's founded
observer comparison supplies a differential/global ownership rule for this depth assignment—one
that survives chart changes and joins consistently around finite cells. That work must remain
metric-led and must not invent an action or bootstrap filter.
