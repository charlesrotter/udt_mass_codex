| Exploration | What we learned | Why it advances the roadmap |
|---|---|---|
| Repeated signals | We can keep the emitter and receiver fixed and calculate changing arrival clocks, directions and beam shapes together. In the tested family, identical metric values at the first instant can lead to different signal records because the prescribed rates of change differ. | Moves from snapshots to an actual evolving geometry and a repeated observing protocol. |

Supplied initial metric rates can calibrate this chosen family before later signal records are calculated. | Gives a joint comparison with shared calibration rather than a separate adjustment for each effect. |

One finite example makes the duration distinction tangible. Its first pulse
spacing suggests a stretch of about2.19, but the complete event stretches by
about1.67 because the ratio changes during emission. Using only that first
pulse would overestimate this event's duration by about31.47%. These numbers
are from the declared hypothetical geometry, not the sky or a universal trend.
All three cases and frozen controls are in NUMERICAL_RESULTS.md.
