# G69 in plain language

We asked whether changing the cosmic landscape and changing how far we look through it leave the
same fingerprint.

Inside our test map, they do not. The complete instrument reads three things: how the image area
changes, how it stretches, and how it is carried sideways. Those three readings let us tell apart
small changes in endpoint, background profile, and mixing strength throughout the tested region.

But there is a second layer: we do not yet know the original pattern being viewed. If that source
pattern is allowed to be anything, it can be adjusted to exactly cancel any of these geometric
maps. It is like identifying a lens from the final photograph while allowing the original picture
behind the lens to be redrawn freely. The lens contains real information, but the photograph alone
cannot identify it.

This tells us why observational anchors will eventually help and why they cannot simply be fitted
yet. First we must restrict or derive enough of the source pattern—or find independent channels
that read the geometry differently. Then a few fixed coefficients may be estimated honestly, with
other observations held back as tests.
