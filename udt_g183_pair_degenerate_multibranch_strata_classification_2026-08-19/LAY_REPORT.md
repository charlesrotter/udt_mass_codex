# G183 lay report

There are three very different ways a comparison can look “singular.”

First, light can travel along a null direction inside a perfectly healthy observer-pair geometry.
Nothing breaks.

Second, the particular direction chosen as the clock can become null. The two-dimensional geometry
may still be healthy, but that clock is no longer a valid observer clock. A different clock can be
chosen only by changing or extending the measurement instructions.

Third, the two directions defining the pair can collapse into one. That is a real loss of geometry:
the completed ruler and scalar kernel are undefined at the collapse.

There is also a middle case: two independent directions can span a null sheet whose internal metric
is degenerate, or a purely spatial sheet with no clock direction. The map still has two directions,
but it is not a valid clock-and-ruler observer plane.

Cut points and windings are different again. They can give several healthy routes to the same
endpoint. The kernel works on each route; it simply does not choose one. In the tape-measure analogy,
the tape still reads correctly on every route, but the tape does not decide which route you meant.

So this step narrows the remaining problem cleanly: singularities are domain failures, while many
apparent “ambiguities” are regular branch multiplicity and must stay branch-labelled.
