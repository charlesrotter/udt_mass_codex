# G151 lay report

G149 showed that one real piece of geometry can play the first chord. G150 showed that smooth local
geometry does not force the relative loudness of its four named notes. G151 asks what can govern how
that chord changes.

The answer is curvature—but only after the observer question supplies a real family of neighboring
relations. Think of marks painted on neighboring strings. If the marks are carried together as the
strings evolve, curvature tells us how their separation bends and focuses. If we merely read a new
distance independently at every moment, there is no continuous string for curvature to act on.

Mathematically, the query must first supply a smooth two-parameter family and identify its variation
with `xi`; terminal endpoints alone are insufficient. “The marks are carried together” is then
`[u,xi]=0`. Without it, the exact identity has extra terms describing how labels are reassigned.
With it, the metric derives a clean generalized deviation equation. A geodesic family reduces to
ordinary Jacobi deviation; an accelerated family retains the transverse change of its acceleration.

This carried-family condition is a genuine restriction: away from the zero chord it forces the
radial acceleration note to vanish and ties depth change and screen turn to spatial gradients of
the observer flow. It is sufficient, not uniquely necessary; special non-carried labels can have
canceling correction terms.

This is linear progress: we found the precise joint between first-order pair geometry and
curvature. It does not yet tell us which observer family Nature realizes or select the universe's
history.
