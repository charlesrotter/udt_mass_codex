# G290 map — metric-native topology/history bridge whiteboard

Date: 2026-08-28
Mode: metric-led, outcome-blind, whiteboard only

## Whole question

G289 establishes that a historical unit-vector Hopf texture can be embedded locally in the
projectivized null cone after an observer and frame are supplied. It also establishes that the raw
component Hopf class does not descend through full local frame gauge and that conformally inequivalent
metric histories can carry the same null texture.

G290 asks whether the requested bridge is one object or several:

1. a metric-native, diffeomorphism- and frame-gauge-invariant sector label;
2. a quantity that can distinguish regular metric histories, including G289's conformal twins;
3. a persistence or propagation statement meaningful on a supplied time-live history; and
4. a nonidentity history restriction rather than another evaluator.

## Full candidate space

- intrinsic topology of the tangent, null-direction, screen, or curvature-eigen bundles;
- relative/framed section invariants;
- Levi-Civita holonomy and loop conjugacy data;
- Chern-Weil characteristic classes and boundary transgressions;
- connection-dependent Chern-Simons-type quantities;
- curvature/Jacobi spectral projectors and their eigenbundle topology;
- exact obstructions showing that topology and history discrimination cannot be the same job.

## Excluded shortcuts

- fixing a spatial frame or round target to preserve the historical raw Hopf number;
- importing the historical `L2+L4` action, constant exterior, finite box, or static equation;
- adding a source, field equation, matter model, observation, scale, Planck cutoff, or `X_max`;
- calling a quantity history-selective merely because it evaluates differently on two supplied
  histories;
- treating kinematic persistence under a supplied smooth deformation as an owned evolution law.

## Maximum whiteboard conclusion

G290 may classify the simplest viable native construction, prove a bounded obstruction, or separate
the requested bridge into distinct mathematical jobs. It cannot derive a physical history, action,
carrier, boundary, dynamics, mass, or particle.
