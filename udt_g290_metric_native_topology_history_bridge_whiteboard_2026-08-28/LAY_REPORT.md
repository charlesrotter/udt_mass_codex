# G290 lay report

The metric does more than attach a topological label to an observer pair. Once a complete observer
pair supplies its two-dimensional viewing screen and a route, the metric tells us how that screen
rotates as it is carried along the route.

That rotation has now been derived exactly. Changing the screen's coordinate axes does not change
the rotation measured after a closed trip. If no screen orientation is chosen, the sign of the
rotation is ambiguous, but its inverse-pair or cosine remains invariant on an orientable screen.

A genuinely nonorientable route may return the screen reflected rather than merely rotated; that
global case has not been classified here. Also, without orientation, opposite local curvature signs
cannot always be told apart, although flat and nonflat members of the registered witness still can.

This quantity distinguishes the previously troublesome conformal twins. They have the same light
cones and the same null texture, but their screens rotate differently because their curvatures are
different. Shrinking the loop recovers the local curvature and avoids the ambiguity that one full
rotation can look like no rotation.

The time-dependent result is equally clean: the change in closed-loop screen rotation equals the
screen curvature passing through the loop's swept-out surface.

The important boundary remains. This tells us how to read any supplied evolving metric; it does not
say which evolving metric Nature chooses. So G290 closes a native history-discrimination gap, not
the physical-history-selection gap.
