## Independent analytic reconstruction

signature (-+++). Supply a smooth scalar theta on U with nonzero future null k=grad(theta) throughout

    nabla_k k = (1/2) grad(g(k,k)) = 0.

k(lambda^A)=0. Such coordinates follow locally from a transversal to the nonzero vector field,
using theta as one coordinate on the transversal. The remaining two coordinates implement the
supplied cross-phase identification. Parameter r is the affine flow parameter of this normalized
k; it is not observer proper time. Shrink U to a product flowbox if necessary.

Because k-flat=dtheta, the coordinate metric is

    g = [[0, 1, 0, 0],
         [1, a, b1, b2],

         [0, b2, q12, q22]].

J=sqrt(det(q))>0. Expansion of the determinant in the first row and column gives det(g)=-det(q),

## Exact converse and free data

For any smooth aligned C=rho k on this flowbox,

    div C=0 iff partial_r(J rho)=0 iff J rho=F(theta,lambda),          (A5)

where F is arbitrary smooth initial data on a three-dimensional section transverse to k.
Nonnegative currents require F>=0. F is neither metric-selected nor fixed by the transport equation.
There are two transverse label variables AND one independent phase variable. A single null phase
sheet theta=constant is characteristic/tangent to k and cannot supply data on the other phase
sheets. A proper local initial section intersects each ray once, e.g. r=r0.

For each fixed theta, dmu_theta=Delta F(theta,lambda)|d^2lambda| is unchanged along the ray cuts.
Thus local divergence conservation implies cut independence within each phase sheet. It does not
imply the same measure on different phase sheets under the supplied identification. The specific
G352 product requires exactly

    F(theta,lambda)=s(lambda)/Delta,

or equivalently partial_theta F=0 in coordinates adapted to that fixed identification. Merely
writing F=h(theta)s(lambda) is insufficient for the fixed uniform |dtheta|/Delta phase factor
unless h is constant and absorbed into the supplied label normalization. A general F need not even
have this weaker separated form. This is an exact local converse within the smooth aligned class,
not a classification of all conserved spacetime vector fields.

Decisive flat counterexample: in Minkowski (t,z,x,y), let theta=z-t, k=partial_t+partial_z,
lambda=(x,y), r=t, J=1, and

    F(theta,x,y)=2+theta^2+x^2+y^2,       C=F k.

On finite bounded coordinate ranges this is smooth, positive and integrable, with div C=0 because
(partial_t+partial_z)F(z-t,x,y)=0. Its measures at theta=0 and theta=1 differ under the fixed labels.
This refutes any claim that current conservation alone recovers G352's phase-independent product.
It does not refute the existence of the product representation (A1).

## Coordinate changes, normalization, and limits

Passive relabeling changes both the label density and J by the same absolute coordinate Jacobian;
rho and C are invariant. A passive relabeling depending on theta can make the coordinate density
look phase-dependent while leaving the geometric current unchanged. The supplied cross-phase
identification must then also be transformed: holding the new numerical labels fixed would in
general specify a different identification. For example x'=exp(theta)x transforms a constant

change of which labels are identified across phases changes the supplied data and is not a
covariance test. Smooth ray-constant labels are needed; an r-dependent relabeling introduces
transverse components in k and invalidates the simple partial_r formula until transformed correctly.

For theta'=b theta+c with b>0 and Delta'=b Delta, k'=b k and rho'=rho/b, so C, Xi and Gamma are
unchanged (with corresponding flow parameter r'=r/b). A negative rescaling violates the stipulated
future gradient. A general increasing nonlinear phase change retains nullness but does not preserve
the fixed common spacing model. Keeping the same measure would require a varying transformed
phase weight; choosing another constant spacing changes the supplied realization. Conservation
still permits such phase-dependent weights, because k(theta)=0; it does not enforce affine phase
normalization.

The theorem is local and regular. It uses a full-rank injective flowbox and J>0. It proves no
smooth-current continuation through caustics, global quotient existence, global phase, global
transversal, completeness, boundary matching, or unique global sum across overlapping labels.
Finite label measure gives finite Xi on finite phase intervals. Pointwise finite observers need
not give a uniformly bounded or integrable rate over a noncompact patch; G352's frequency

On relatively compact regular patches with smooth bounded observer fields this is automatic.
Atomic/singular label content and discrete phase crossings are outside the smooth-density theorem;
