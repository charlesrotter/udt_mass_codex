We constructed the first astronomical comparison around CGCG 074-064. It now
states what one candidate geometry must predict together: where its maser spots
appear, their received spectral shifts, and how those shifts change over time.
The same geometry supplies all three. This is a concrete test specification;
no native astronomical metric has yet been selected or fitted.

The audit found useful restrictions. A single distance number does not describe
every possible image distortion, so we must use the full angular map or justify
the published scalar-distance approximation. A fixed overall redshift cancels
from one optical-velocity slope conversion because both the velocity convention
and the received timing already contain it. Adding another slowdown factor
could count the same effect twice. The redshift itself remains.

We also corrected the source interpretation: its fitted systemic redshift is
not automatically a directly observed reference clock; its statistical distance
interval is not its full uncertainty; its monitored slopes use finite windows.
The underlying source dynamics and measurement model remain explicit conditional
inputs. No new physics was adopted to complete the calculation.

The available source summaries give concrete conditional constraints, but a full
spot-level empirical test still needs the complete data/reduction and a supplied
metric with consistent source histories. The complete spot table was not
retrieved in the bounded access attempts. We performed no likelihood fit and
made no claim that the source's published distance is wrong.
