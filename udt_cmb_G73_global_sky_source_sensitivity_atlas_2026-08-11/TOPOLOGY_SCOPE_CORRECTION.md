## Correct general statement

A robust repeated-image kaleidoscope requires a global noninjective/branching relation, a singular
or critical map in the whole-sky `S^2` self-map case, or restrictions on the source family.

Singularity is not a universal requirement for every repeated-image geometry.

## Exact whole-sky theorem

Let `f:S^2 -> S^2` be smooth and everywhere a local diffeomorphism. Since the source is compact,
`f` is proper, hence a covering map. The target `S^2` is connected and simply connected, so every
connected covering is one-sheeted. Therefore `f` is a diffeomorphism and cannot supply a nontrivial
repeated whole-sky self-image. A nontrivial repeated `S^2` self-image must leave the everywhere-
regular class through critical/branch points.

## Cases outside that theorem

- With different source/target topology, a regular covering of degree greater than one can exist.
- On a partial-sky or noncompact domain, the compact connected `S^2` covering argument does not
  apply; a regular noninjective map may exist.
- A branch-labelled relation is not necessarily one single-valued map. Several regular paths may
  coexist without a caustic, while their selection or combination remains unowned.
- Strong shear can dominate normalized direction while remaining invertible and producing no
  repeated image.

No current UDT source selects any one of these global cases. The next gate remains a complete
symbolic-scale sky-relation topology/critical atlas.
