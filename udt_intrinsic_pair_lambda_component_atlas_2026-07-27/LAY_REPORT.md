# Lay report

We turned one knob in the complete metric: how strongly the angular part responds to the clock-depth
field. We then followed that knob over its entire possible number line.

The result looks like eight adjoining rooms separated by seven doorways. Inside every room, our
three curvature fingerprints clearly identify the clock direction. At each doorway, those three
fingerprints temporarily line up and stop giving a unique answer.

The six examples we already had sit in three rooms:

- the examples at `-2`, `-1`, and `0` share one room;
- the examples at `0.5` and `1` share another;
- the example at `2` sits in the next room.

The important caution is that a doorway may be a limitation of our fingerprinting method, not a
break in the geometry. The clock, twist, and complete metric remain present there. Our next job is to
look at each doorway from another point or with other intrinsic fingerprints. If the answer returns,
the doorway was only a blind spot. If every intrinsic test fails, then we may have found a genuine
change in the metric's symmetry.

This is useful forward motion because the previously vague “where do the neighborhoods end?” question
has become seven exact, finite questions.
