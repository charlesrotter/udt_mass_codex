## 1. What the matched endpoint multiplier actually proves

Let g0=exp(2u)g and let the two observer curves and the smooth selected
emission-to-reception correspondence be IDENTICAL as event curves in both
metrics. Positive conformal changes preserve unparameterized null geodesics,
so a prescribed regular null branch gives the same incidence map. They do not
preserve null affine parameters or general timelike geodesics.

For a timelike curve, d tau0=exp(u)d tau. If Z denotes reception proper
interval divided by emission proper interval for the derivative of this
same correspondence, then

    Z0 = exp(u(o)-u(e)) Zg,
    Zg/Z0 = exp(-u(o)+u(e)).                              (1)

This derives the formula directly from the proper-time line element. It needs
neither stationarity nor Ricci-flatness; those would be extra hypotheses for
identifying or interpreting the reference. Constant additive shifts in u cancel
from (1), so u(p)=0 is not needed to remove a purely constant ambiguity in this
ratio. Nonconstant reference ambiguities do affect (1).

The formula is exact for differential received intervals along a smooth
one-parameter family of signals, or an infinitesimal ticking limit. For two
finite ticks, proper durations are integrals of exp(u) along each clock segment;
endpoint values alone do not generally give the finite ratio. A finite-duration
claim requires constant conformal rate on the segments, an integral formula, or
a controlled small-interval approximation with its error stated.

Same-history has substantive meaning: reference clocks generally require
different forces to follow the same physical observer curves. If instead one
releases clocks to reference geodesics, their histories and incidence maps change
and (1) is not the resulting total comparison formula. Conversely, requiring
the same unparameterized timelike geodesics in sufficiently many directions
forces u constant by R8GRL T1; it would erase any nontrivial extra ratio. Thus
null-path agreement and ordinary clocks do not by themselves operationally
prepare a nontrivial matched reference experiment.

## 2. Ricci-flat reference existence and one-point normalization

The connection change for g0=exp(2u)g gives, in four dimensions,

    Ric0_ab = Ric_ab - 2 Hess(u)_ab - g_ab Box(u)
              + 2 du_a du_b - 2 g_ab |du|^2.            (2)

The equation Ric0=0 is an overdetermined tensor condition, not a consequence
of agreeing null cones. Its trace and trace-free pieces are

    Box(u)+|du|^2 = R/6,
    [Hess(u)-du tensor du]_TF = Ric_TF/2.               (3)

A conditional assignment must establish a positive smooth solution on the
entire comparison domain, with compatible data, and must state local versus
global scope. u(p)=0 supplies no existence theorem. Singularities, boundaries,
topology, positive extension and complete observer histories are separate issues.

Here is a direct adverse uniqueness control requiring no classification
theorem. Take g=eta=-dt^2+dx^2+dy^2+dz^2, p=(0,0,0,0), v=t-x,
f=1+bv>0 and u_b=-log(f), with real free b. Both eta and

    g0_b = f^-2 eta

are Ricci-flat on any chosen positive neighborhood and satisfy u_b(p)=0.
Indeed the inverse-factor transformation reads

    Ric(f^-2 h) = Ric(h) + 2 f^-1 Hess_h(f)
                   + [f^-1 Box_h(f)-3 f^-2 |df|_h^2]h, (4)

and all added terms vanish because f is affine with null gradient. The
same-history multiplier from (1) is f(o)/f(e), which varies with b for events
with different v. For example e=p and the future null endpoint
o=(L,-L,0,0), L>0, give 1+2bL. Ordinary static timelike emitter and receiver
curves can contain these endpoints; the selected physical Minkowski null ray
is regular. Point calibration plus Ricci-flatness therefore does not define a
universal unique positional multiplier, even locally.

The relevant survivor is restricted uniqueness. Given one Ricci-flat h, any
other Ricci-flat f^-2 h obeys

    Hess_h(f)=c h,    c constant,    |df|_h^2=2cf,       (5)

on a connected domain. The trace of (4) gives the Hessian condition; commuting
derivatives and contracting with Ric(h)=0 gives dc=0. Commutation also yields
W(h)(...,grad f)=0. If the Weyl contraction has zero vector kernel throughout
the domain, df=0, so the remaining constant is fixed by calibration. G375
gives a stronger source-owned local classification: nonflat Ricci-flat bases
without a parallel null covector have only constant positive factors; the
parallel-null sector admits f=a+bv. Its fixed-domain, smooth, contractible-local,
positivity and target-scalar qualifications must remain attached.

The scalar I=C_abcd C^abcd is not positive definite in Lorentz signature.
I nonzero is a useful sufficient rigidity diagnostic, not a necessary one:
a nonzero Weyl tensor annihilating a non-null vector is impossible in dimension
four by the three-dimensional algebraic curvature argument in G375. If it
annihilates a nonzero null vector k, a null basis (k,l,e1,e2), Weyl symmetries
and trace freedom leave only trace-free components W(l,ei,l,ej). Raising the
l indices pairs them with k components, which vanish, hence I=0. Thus I nonzero
implies the vector contraction kernel is zero at that point. To propagate a
single-point rigidity conclusion over the whole connected domain one must also
use (5)/its path-ODE uniqueness, not claim pointwise algebra alone constructs a
reference. Existence is still missing even where this uniqueness test works.

An explicit nonexistence family is already covered by the inspected G375
argument: a four-dimensional Einstein metric with Lambda nonzero and Weyl not
identically zero admits only constant conformal Einstein factors on the stated
domain. Constant scaling cannot make its nonzero Ricci tensor vanish. This is
a fixed-base local exclusion, not a general finite recognition algorithm.

## 3. Exact held-orbit echo identity

Let g=Omega^2 h with Omega>0 and h stationary under a Killing field K that is
timelike on the emitter and relay histories. Both are prescribed K orbits. Use
the K-flow parameter t consistently. Require the selected outgoing and return
null branches and zero-delay relay rule to be equivariant under this stationary
flow. Both branches must exist and vary smoothly with emission time; horizons,
caustics, branch switching and causal failures are excluded from the domain.

Stationarity then gives t_return=t_emit+T with a fixed round-trip flow duration
T. This requires stationarity, not staticity; rotation/Sagnac asymmetry between
the two legs does not change the translation-equivariance argument. Along the
emitter orbit, N=sqrt(-h(K,K)) is constant. Therefore

    d tau_emit = Omega(e) N dt_emit,
    d tau_return = Omega(r) N dt_return,
    Q = d tau_return/d tau_emit = Omega(r)/Omega(e).     (6)

If h is the proposed reference g0=exp(2u)g, then Omega=exp(-u) and
Q=exp(-u(r)+u(e)). This compares two events on the SAME emitting orbit. It
does not by itself determine a between-position one-way multiplier on two
different orbits. A spatially varying but K-stationary Omega has Q=1 for every
such echo. Thus this echo is blind to stationary spatial conformal profiles.

Q is an operational derivative using one physical proper clock; no distant
clock synchronization is required. Its interpretation as a conformal scale
ratio nevertheless requires the held family, a stationary representative,
branch equivariance and the zero-delay rule. A physical relay delay measured
by the conformally varying relay clock need not be invariant in flow time.
Unspecified observer motion or delay can therefore produce different Q.
These hypotheses are physical-preparation input, not supplied by the raw number.

## 4. What physical Weyl measurements add, and the false converse

In four dimensions, C^a_bcd is conformally invariant, so lowering and raising
indices gives

    I_g = Omega^-4 I_h.                                (7)

Stationarity gives the same I_h at echo emission and return on one K orbit.
Combining (6) and (7) yields the division-free relation

    Q^4 I_g(r) = I_g(e).                               (8)

When I_h is nonzero, its sign stays the same along the orbit and the positive
root Q=(I_g(e)/I_g(r))^(1/4) is defined. This is an exact consistency test of
the stated conformally stationary protocol. It neither needs nor tests
Ricci-flatness of h: every stationary h satisfies the same relation. Comparing
different spatial orbits instead would require the generally different
reference values I_h at those locations.

At I=0 the relation is vacuous. Zero I does not establish zero Weyl curvature:
the G375 explicit Ricci-flat metric

    h=2 dr dv+dx^2+dy^2+r(x^2-y^2)dr^2

has R_rxrx=-r, nonzero off r=0, but all its curvature contractions vanish in
this quadratic scalar. Raising an r index requires a v index, and the
curvature has no v components. This is also the reference-ambiguity sector
with parallel null dr. A scalar criterion must therefore retain its zero,
sign and degeneracy limits and cannot reconstruct the full Weyl tensor.

A direct false-converse control makes the missing vacuum implication concrete.
On a static patch |rho|<1, set

    h=-(1-rho^2)dt^2+(1-rho^2)^-1 d rho^2
        +d theta^2+sin(theta)^2 d phi^2,
    0<theta<pi.

The first two and last two dimensions each have sectional curvature +1. The
product therefore has Ric(h)=h, R=4, Riem^2=8, Ric^2=4, and

    I_h=Riem^2-2 Ric^2+R^2/3=16/3.

K=partial_t is timelike and stationary in this patch. For sufficiently short
held-orbit regular echoes Q=1 and (8) holds with I=16/3, yet this metric has no
local Ricci-flat conformal representative on the G375 domain: Lambda=1,
W nonzero force every conformal Einstein factor constant. Thus even nonzero
Weyl measurements plus exact echo consistency cannot certify the proposed
vacuum reference. Any Omega^2 h gives the corresponding nontrivial conformal
echo identity and retains the same conformal-class obstruction.

This product is a diagnostic supplied geometry, not a UDT countermodel or
