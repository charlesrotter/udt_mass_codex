# G304 lay report

We found a real clue, but not the final law.

The previous work left two locally healthy possibilities: a zero curvature constant and a family
with one constant that could be positive or negative. Ordinary local causality could not choose
among them.

We now asked a global question already present in the UDT program: must an observer's static region
end at a finite, regular causal horizon?

Inside the exact smooth-centered spherical family, the answer separates the choices cleanly:

- a positive constant produces a finite regular horizon;
- zero produces unlimited flat radial space;
- a negative constant produces no finite outer horizon.

So the working finite-cell condition favors the positive constant. It still does not tell us the
constant's magnitude (size) or select the complete evolving history. The corresponding radius falls out of the
metric once that constant is supplied; we did not insert `X_max` into the reciprocal kernel. We
have not yet proved that this radius is the physical all-frame, all-observer `X_max`.

Equally important, the older WR-L result used a straight-line profile in radius. The newly tested
constant-curvature profile is quadratic. Both can have a regular finite horizon, but they do not
satisfy the same exact equation. They are alternative global closures, not two descriptions that
can be quietly mixed.

The reciprocal metric and kernel remain unchanged. No observation, mass model, source, action, or
fit was used. The next scientific question is whether the positive constant-curvature static patch
extends into the complete observer-relative, time-live UDT geometry while retaining the required
reciprocal and angular structure. That is where this clue either becomes a genuine bridge or fails.
