# G119 lay report

The simplification survived the harder test.

For any smooth time-changing spherical UDT geometry, a central observer does not need a separate
two-dimensional “lens setting” to describe how a tiny patch of sky grows. Rotate one radial light
ray by a tiny angle and spherical symmetry creates the neighboring ray. At the other end, their
physical separation is exactly the sphere's areal radius times that angle.

So the angular part of this central spherical orchestra is not an independently tuned instrument:
it is the same geometry's areal radius playing in two transverse directions. Changing screen bases
can rotate or reflect the written matrix, but cannot change its area, which is exactly `R^2`.

This remains true for a time-dependent metric and at arbitrary finite distance on each regular
branch. At a caustic the visible two-dimensional map collapses, but its position-plus-direction
phase information remains finite; the branch must be retained rather than discarded.

What this removes is the independent P1 angular-screen matrix. What it does **not** yet remove is
the remaining scalar ambiguity: supernova brightness sees the combination `R/sqrt(T)`, where `T`
is radiative transfer. The metric now owns `R`; it still does not derive the carrier/transfer law or
which radius-frequency history Nature realizes.

The next honest step is therefore no longer “fit another angular matrix.” It is to regrade the SNe
interface with this exact theorem and isolate whether the remaining transfer/history split can be
reduced by the existing metric-query network, without assuming transparency or adding coefficients.
