# G83 in lay language

The old G75 map stopped too early to contain the proposed far-distance limit. Its 591 test universes all remain ordinary and finite through their declared endpoint.

We then did one carefully labelled experiment: continue the same mathematical shapes farther without pretending that nature selected them. One of the three basic background shapes has a point where its observer-to-observer clock slowdown grows without bound. That is the right *kind* of behavior for `X_max`, but it is not yet proof that this point is the universe's physical maximum separation.

The angular “orchestra” remained active. Of 591 complete light-path tests aimed progressively closer to that candidate limit, 516 reached their target cleanly, 18 turned around, and 57 were still travelling when the fixed computation window ended. So there are two layers:

1. a simple common clock-dilation asymptote supplied by the background lapse; and
2. richer angular/mixing structure that controls how particular observer-ray comparisons approach it.

That is useful progress. It says the orchestra need not define the basic endpoint divergence, but it cannot be ignored when predicting what observers actually see.

The remaining problem is conceptual and global: different receivers assign different one-sided chart distances to the same lapse-zero point. We still need the metric's observer-pair re-centering/global-completion rule before this candidate can be called the frame-shared `X_max` or given a physical size.
