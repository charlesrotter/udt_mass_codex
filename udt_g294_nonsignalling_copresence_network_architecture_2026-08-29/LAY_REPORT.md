# G294 lay report

The original intuition can be made mathematically coherent, but “infinite speed” is the wrong name
for it.

A speed describes something travelling through space over time. Co-presence can instead be a rule
saying that distant observer states belong to one complete relational state. Such a rule does not
travel and therefore has no speed at all.

UDT already has the right two kinds of pair information to express your mutual-clock intuition:

- distance magnitude can remain positive and the same in both directions;
- directed reciprocal depth changes sign when the pair is reversed;
- the directed position channel is odd;
- the provisional planar mutual-clock channel is even, so both directions have the same slowing
  magnitude;
- in the complete metric, screen mismatch modifies that mutual readout rather than being removed.

The causality condition is separate. Distant states may be related or correlated, but a freely
controlled change at one location must not immediately change a local observable outside its metric
light cone. The metric tells us what the light cones are. UDT does not yet have the response or light
law needed to prove that support condition.

There are two versions of “right now.” A pair-relative co-presence graph is coherent and needs no
universal clock, but it does not create one global present. A universal global present is also
mathematically possible, but it requires an owned integrable time structure. Current premises do not
derive or choose one.

Most importantly, merely adding co-presence does not select the universe. G294 constructs regular
inequivalent UDT metrics with the same co-presence slicing and lawful metric cones. The missing
physical object is narrower:

> a nontrivial rule restricting the complete co-present pair/screen network, together with an update
> rule whose observable effects remain inside the metric light cones.

That is a plausible bridge between G293's augmented-state and global-law lanes. G294 identifies the
architecture but does not invent its formula or claim that a distant image already shows the star's
co-present state.
