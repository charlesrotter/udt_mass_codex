# Lay report

The zoom-out found that we had compressed two different missing pieces into one phrase.

One missing piece tells us how much clock/ruler dilation belongs to a comparison between two
observers. It lives on the route between those observers.

The other tells us how the entire universe reacts when its metric, matter, or boundary is varied.
It lives on the space of possible universes.

Both can be described with the phrase “one-form,” but that is like calling both a road map and a
wiring diagram “a diagram.” They take different inputs and do different jobs. A final self-consistent
universe may connect them, but we have not derived that connection.

The good news is that UDT's relational skeleton is stronger than an absolute-field picture. We do
not need one ruler arrow painted everywhere in the universe. Each observer comparison can carry its
own direction, and the comparisons compose correctly when changes of observer and direction are
recorded honestly. The angular behavior appears naturally when different directions are composed.

What is still missing is a full loop. We can travel from a supplied metric to observer-path
geometry, and from supplied depth to a complete comparison. We cannot yet travel back from all
those comparisons to the metric that must support them. So “bootstrap fixed point” is presently an
excellent architectural description, not yet a calculable equation.

This is convergence: we now know which joints are separate, which arrows already exist, and exactly
where every possible return loop is broken.
