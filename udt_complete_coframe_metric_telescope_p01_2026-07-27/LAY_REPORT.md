# P01 in lay language

We finally used the GPU as a telescope instead of as a theory-making machine.

Think of the complete UDT metric as an eight-instrument orchestra.  We wrote
1,024 different scores in which every instrument could play, then played every
score at five volume levels.  That produced 5,120 geometric configurations.
We did not ask any score to look like a particle, the cosmos, GR, or a desired
answer.

Three useful things appeared.

First, the orchestra is not random noise.  Turning up any one of the 1,024
complete scores increased curvature and the mixing between the reciprocal and
angular parts at every successive level.  The configuration space has a clear
outward organization.

Second, the full orchestra naturally makes rich geometry: curved regions,
clock-like and space-like `phi` behavior in the same configuration, coupling
between sectors, and nontrivial transport around loops.  We do not have to add
a mechanism merely to make the metric do something interesting.

Third, two special ideas did *not* happen automatically.  The `phi` behavior
did not settle everywhere onto a lightlike condition, and the two angular
directions did not automatically become an exactly repeated pair.  That does
not kill those ideas.  Perfect unison is a very thin set in the space of all
possible music, so random full-orchestra scores almost never land on it.  It
tells us that the next map must deliberately chart the special chords and
rank-changing seams, not just throw more random scores at the GPU.

At the loudest level, local geometry was still computable, but carrying a frame
around the entire loop became too enormous for reliable ordinary float64
arithmetic in 1,015 of 1,024 cases.  We label those loop results unresolved and
do not pretend they reveal new physics.

What we have is a trustworthy map of a bounded part of the metric's raw
configuration space.  It is not yet a map of physical solutions, because UDT
still has not supplied the native equation that says which configurations the
universe can actually occupy.
