## 2. Reconstructing the derivative needed by a response test

Curvature at one event does not determine its spacetime derivatives. Repeat the
scalar reconstruction in a neighborhood. Independently establish geodesic
placements exp_o(±h e_a), a=0..3, with timelike e0=u and spatial e_i. For a smooth
scalar r, derivative along each geodesic gives the appropriate covariant Hessian
contraction at o. Thus define

    D_h r = [-r(+he0)-r(-he0)+sum_i(r(+he_i)+r(-he_i))−4r(o)]/h².

This converges to Box_g r at o without a field equation. It requires nine event
values, including earlier/later timelike samples and spacelike preparations,
and metric/ruler/transport information to establish the placements. There is
no acausal readout: results can be collected later. No coordinate separations
are substituted for proper geodesic displacements.

With |r_hat−r|≤ε_R at all nine events and each directional fourth derivative
bounded by B_a on its geodesic segment,

    |D_h r_hat−Box r| ≤ 12 ε_R/h² + h² sum_a B_a/12 + E_place. (3)

The center is shared and has weight −4, explaining 12 rather than an assumed
independence gain. No stochastic independence is needed. The same bound applies
to measured scalar R or to a separately defined perturbation r=δR with its own
background/matching errors. L and h are two distinct scales. Finite data without
independent derivative bounds cannot certify either limit. Smooth scalar
functions can agree at all sampled events while differing in their second jet.
Nor does reconstruction of R identify its positional contribution.
