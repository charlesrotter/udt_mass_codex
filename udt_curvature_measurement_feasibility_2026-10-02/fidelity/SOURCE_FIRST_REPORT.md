## Independent geometric reconstruction and its null space

Write A=Ric(e0,e0), B_i=Ric(e0,ei), D_i=Ric(ei,ei) in an independently calibrated
orthonormal tetrad. PSW1 supplies Q(U)=Ric(U,U) as the limiting triad contraction
`-2 sum_i log p_L(e_i)/L^2`; it does not supply a finite-L Ricci reading.
For c^2-s^2=1 and U_i±=c e0±s ei,

    Q_i±=c^2 A ± 2cs B_i+s^2 D_i,
    R=sum_i (Q_i+ + Q_i-)/(2s^2) -(1+3c^2/s^2) Q(e0).

Choosing the rational control c=5/4,s=3/4 (speed 3/5 of calibrated c_E) gives

    R=(8/9) sum_(six boosted frames) Q(U) -(28/3)Q(e0).

This needs seven differently moving primary geodesics through the same event,
each with its own orthonormal spatial triad and parallel-prepared secondary
clocks. A boost's spatial leg along ei is ±s e0+c ei; the two transverse axes
are unchanged. These are supplied test arrangements, not a preferred population.
The independent exact script verifies the ten symmetric basis tensors, the
seven-row rank and a second rational change of orthonormal frame. It finds rank7:
the scalar and the seven A/B_i/D_i combinations are identifiable, but the three
spatial off-diagonal Ricci components are not. A tensor with only Ric_12=Ric_21
nonzero is invisible to all seven records. Thus the formula recovers R, not full
Ricci or Riemann, and cannot alone test the original tensor field equation.
An all-unit-timelike ideal family could recover Ricci by PSW1 polarization;
that is a stronger data requirement. Even full Ricci is not all Riemann/Weyl.

Tidal geodesic-deviation observations offer another geometric route: relative
acceleration of an independently prepared infinitesimal geodesic separation
gives the electric tidal form, with sign fixed by the stated convention. Taking
its spatial trace gives Q(U). This does not infer Ricci from Einstein's equation
or a presumed source model. It still needs a free-fall/relative-velocity protocol,
spatial calibration and a limit with error control. One observer's tidal trace
alone supplies only Ric(U,U), not scalar R.

## Preparation and finite errors are load-bearing

Fixed clocks, arbitrary accelerated clocks, arbitrary comoving clocks and
parallel-prepared free clocks are different experiments. PSW1 already gives a
fixed-receiver sign reversal and a flat-space kinematic redshift control. In flat
space a small uncorrected relative radial rapidity contributes at order itself
to log p, independently of curvature. A preparation bias of order L^2 therefore
can counterfeit the quadratic curvature coefficient; a fixed bias divided by
L^2 diverges. It must be measured/subtracted with a certified residual or vanish
faster than L^2 for an exact limiting estimator. This is not a demand that a real
laboratory literally reach zero error. Finite-error inference instead needs a
full bounded error interval. Acceleration, finite relay delay and pulse-emission
derivatives must also be calibrated rather than absorbed into candidate R.

If each outgoing log record has absolute error at most epsilon(L), and each
PSW1 remainder has a supplied bound C L^3 over all seven preparations, then each
Q estimate has error at most 6C L+6 epsilon(L)/L^2. The rational scalar formula
has absolute weight sum44/3, giving the conservative total

    |R_est-R| <= 88 C L+88 epsilon(L)/L^2,

before separation, frame and preparation errors. The coefficients assume the
same worst-case per-record bounds, no statistical independence. Smoothness gives
local existence of bounds, not numerical values. A usable finite experiment
must supply them independently, specify uncertainty correlations and choose a
scale window where signal exceeds both truncation and noise. Smaller L alone
does not improve an experiment with fixed absolute noise. Poorly separated boost
frames amplify errors; the displayed speed is an illustrative control, not an
optimal engineering choice. Relativistic boosts change remainder/preparation
costs, so algebraic weights alone cannot optimize them.

Lengths, boosts, c_E and proper time must be calibrated without fitting the
target response. A redshift-derived distance under the target equation cannot
then validate that equation's distance response. Independent scale calibration
remains observational, not a native derivation of c_E or alpha.

## Scalar-mode pole: measurable conditions versus absent experiment

Given an independently identified constant-scalar-curvature background, delta R
is invariant under first-order coordinate-gauge change because its background
gradient vanishes. For a varying background, the perturbation changes by the
Lie derivative of R0; relational event/background matching or a controlled gauge
choice is required. R at an event is a scalar, but coordinate labeling alone
does not provide matching between two geometries or separate a background from
a perturbation. No candidate field equation may be used to perform the purported
independent reconstruction or subtraction.

On the stipulated flat source-free linearized background, a resolved scalar
mode's invariant dispersion is omega^2/c_E^2-|k|^2=M^2. Frequency at one worldline
without independently measured k is insufficient: many (M,k) pairs produce it,
and moving instruments alter frequency and wavevector together. A homogeneous
oscillation is a special k=0 preparation, not something inferred from frequency
alone. A weak signal must be separated from background drift, nonlinear terms,
other modes and finite-window leakage with specified controls. Amplitude can be
unknown for a frequency estimate, but a nonzero resolved excitation must exist.

For an independently established static spherically symmetric source-free
exterior in flat background, the decaying solution has delta R=A exp(-Mr)/r.
The slope of log|r delta R| can identify M on a nonzero branch without knowing A.
Source-free exterior, static symmetry, radial metric distance, absence of a
growing mode and boundary selection must be independently established. They
cannot be inferred merely because an exponential fit works. More general
spatial profiles can test the differential equation but need spatial derivative
error bounds and a known geometry. Unknown forcing can imitate a different mass.
These flat dispersion/Yukawa formulas do not automatically hold globally on
nonflat constant-curvature backgrounds; there the covariant background operator
and its domain/boundaries must be used, or a controlled flat approximation proved.

Nothing inspected establishes a laboratory source/excitation, its coupling,
background preparation, amplitude, sensitivity, derivative bound, source-free
certificate or existing-instrument precision. The correct output is a conditional
protocol with these inputs open. Reconstructing a nonzero R does not by itself
identify an additional independent scalar degree of freedom or its causal law.

PSC1's strong positive-pole interval proposal has a further obstruction to being
treated as an available experimental knob: its solution backgrounds generally
vary Lambda between solutions. Holding one Lambda fixed, an open vacuum interval
requires R F_R-F=0 and therefore zero M^2 wherever defined. Real matter environments
cannot substitute without an independently justified source law and background
analysis. Finitely many environment measurements cannot establish exact equality
throughout an open interval. Weak space/time agreement does not distinguish the
quadratic and cubic-curvature controls, which share the flat first variation.

supplied comparison E_f confirms this: F=1+2alpha R, FR-2f=-R and 3Box F=6alpha

Two point pairs with different X identify alpha=(R2-R1)/(6(X2-X1)) and
Lambda=(R1-6alpha X1)/4, subject to any stipulated alpha sign/nonzero domain.
Different R with equal X instead excludes every finite alpha; different R alone
is not an identifiability condition. Constant X and R leave a one-parameter line.
Two fitted points supply no independent confirmation; additional held-out points
or an independently specified parameter test the same affine relation. Finite
agreement with uncertainty cannot establish the equation throughout a region.

X needs more than the pointwise scalar: it is a covariant second derivative of
R (hence generally fourth metric derivatives). At an event with unit tetrad,
use affinely parameterized geodesics through that event and independent R values
on them. Hess R(ea,ea) is the ordinary second derivative along that geodesic;
subtract the timelike contribution and add the three spacelike contributions.
Arbitrarily accelerated laboratory worldlines instead include a·grad R, which
must be removed using independently known acceleration/gradient. Coordinate
partial derivatives without the connection do not reconstruct Box R.
