# Lay report

We found the exact missing gear—not its final shape, but its empty socket.

UDT already has a strong machine for reading a universe once a complete metric history is supplied.
It can separate reciprocal depth from common scale, include the full angular/mixing orchestra,
compare observers, compose consistent comparisons, and calculate how an assumed history behaves
near \(X_{\max}\).

But none of the active equations currently tells the common scale how to change. Some equations
contain its derivatives, but only in the same sense that a speedometer contains the car's speed:
they report what the supplied motion is doing. They do not act like the engine or transmission that
determines the motion.

That is why the work kept narrowing without closing. We were repeatedly asking an excellent
evaluator to double as an evolution law.

This does not mean UDT must pick one unique universe. Ordinary physics also allows many lawful
solutions. The sharper issue is that UDT does not yet own a rule that turns finite common-scale
initial data into a history. Without such a rule, the entire common-scale history must still be
supplied. Reciprocal, scale-free relations can remain meaningful; dimensionful rates and volumes
cannot yet become predictions.

The next clean question is whether UDT's observer network already carries scale consistently among
three observers. That will tell us whether the remaining object is merely an evolution law for an
already well-defined scale field, or whether even cross-observer scale comparison needs one more
piece of structure.
