# G266 lay report

The reciprocal kernel already contains both parts we were looking for.

One part remembers direction: A-to-B and B-to-A are inverses. The other part measures the common
size of the difference and is identical in both directions. They are not separate additions to the
theory; they are two faces of the same reciprocal comparison. Importantly, the common part cannot
be detached and made to compose on its own. The directional part has to remain in the calculation.

The simple candidate that says both clocks appear slow by the same amount is `sech(delta)`. Its
formula now comes directly from the reciprocal kernel's common magnitude, with no fitted
coefficient. What is not yet derived is the sentence saying that this particular inverse magnitude
is the physical clock reading.

The remaining ambiguity is mostly the word “distance.” Areal radius, ruler distance on a static
slice, and optical distance are all legitimate measurements made from the same metric. If each is
separately declared to drive reciprocal depth at a constant local rate, all three produce the same
first local behavior. They diverge at the next order, where curvature and angular response appear.
That local agreement is probably why the distinction stayed hidden.

So this is progress, not another free-function census. The kernel's mutual channel is now explicit
and time-live. The narrow unfinished bridge is deciding—or deriving—which complete observer-pair
distance protocol Nature uses to feed that channel.
