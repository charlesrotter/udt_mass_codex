# G115 lay report

## What changed

We finally let the center of the spherical UDT geometry move in time without importing a fitted
cosmology. The metric then plays three related but different notes:

1. the observer-pair clock/ruler imbalance;
2. the angular beam carried across the sky; and
3. the frequency a specified moving source clock actually measures.

They come from one metric/query construction, but they are not the same number.

## The clean result

Smoothness forces the pair-depth and areal-depth notes to begin very quietly near the center: they
grow like distance squared. They cannot have a linear central slope in this regular point-observer
class.

The source-clock note is different. It can grow linearly because it compares the live radial
threading of the geometry with the motion of the source family. At leading order it is controlled
by one difference, `b-q`:

- `b` says how the metric's radial threading is changing;
- `q` says how the supplied source family moves relative to that threading.

Either coefficient changes if we tilt the allowed central time slices slightly, but their
difference does not. The measurable candidate is the relationship `b-q`, not either coordinate
coefficient alone.

This means time dependence did not magically turn the terminal `phi_pair` into the old linear P1
curve. Instead, it located a lawful place where a linear observed redshift can live: the complete
observer–source relation. That is a simplification and a type correction.

## What happened to the orchestra

It stayed inside the construction. An actively carried, time-changing instrument direction produces
a mixed angular term before the pair readout, and its squared magnitude modifies that instrument's
fixed-label pair depth. Merely renaming sky coordinates cannot manufacture this term; passive
relabeling is gauge. The angular
beam itself has position and direction/momentum parts and remains well-defined even when its
position image focuses to zero.

Exact spherical symmetry makes the central beam isotropic, so shear and the rank-one match are
silent on this particular chord. They were silenced by the symmetry of the question, not deleted
from the full theory.

## Why the kind of source matters

- A point source gives one endpoint, not a resolved picture.
- A resolved source screen gives allowed image positions, but not a rule for the rays' outgoing
  directions.
- A moving spherical source worldtube plus a chosen null direction supplies a phase boundary, but a
  ray must first land on that same direction. If it does, spherical symmetry makes the tangent match
  automatically two-dimensional; if it does not, it fails the boundary before a rank is assigned.

Once a complete source boundary is supplied, the matching calculation is exact: two angular modes,
one mode, or no modes can match. In the exact spherical central class the answer is usually two or
zero; one-mode matching needs anisotropy.

## Honest limit

We have derived how a regular time-live local UDT metric plays this chord. We have not derived which
history Nature uses, how real source clocks populate it, or how the local series continues to SNe,
`X_max`, or the CMB. The next useful question is whether the founded observer/source calibration
identifies the linear `b-q` frequency channel with the observed redshift while retaining the
quadratic terminal and angular channels—before either SNe dataset is exposed.
