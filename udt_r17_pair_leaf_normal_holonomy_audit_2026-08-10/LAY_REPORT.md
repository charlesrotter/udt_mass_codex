# Lay report

The last audit found that the complete metric naturally divides the regular stationary geometry
into two-dimensional observer-pair sheets—cylinders made from time and the Hopf-fiber direction.
This audit asked what happens to the other two angular directions when one moves around inside one
of those sheets.

The answer is useful and concrete.  The metric itself supplies a rule for how that transverse
two-direction “compass” rotates.  Most angular-weight branches have local twist: carrying the
compass around a small closed loop returns it rotated by an amount fixed by the metric.  One branch,
`lambda=-1`, has no such local twist anywhere on a stationary sheet, although a full trip around the
cylinder can still retain a global rotation.  A different branch, `lambda=0`, makes the transverse
metric descend cleanly to the two-sphere beneath the Hopf fibers.

So two instruments in the orchestra have acquired distinct jobs:

- `lambda=-1` is the universally leaf-flat normal-transport branch;
- `lambda=0` is the universally Hopf-base-compatible transverse-metric branch.

That is genuine structure emerging from the supplied metric, not a desired answer inserted into
it.  It is also not yet a selector: the geometry has shown two special roles, but has not said that
one role wins, that both must coincide, or that either branch is physical.

The remaining joint is now narrower.  We need to determine whether the complete global relation
family supplies a lawful way to combine these leafwise transports across different Hopf-base
leaves.  A base path can be carried once someone supplies it; the metric has not yet selected the
path or promoted this normal connection into the complete physical observer-pair map.
