# Lay readout

The elegance idea says that matter might be the stable valleys of UDT's own landscape. This audit
asked a more basic question: **do we actually have that landscape yet?**

The answer is: **we have the terrain, but not yet one native rule for how to walk on it.**

The metric gives us a real common geometric stage. It includes the reciprocal clock/ruler structure
and leaves room for the complete angular and mixing geometry. We even know that nontrivial complete
examples of this stage exist. That is meaningful progress; the candidate structures are not merely
a pile of unrelated equations.

But a terrain map by itself does not tell you which directions a physical configuration is allowed
to move, which slopes count as restoring forces, what boundary it must obey, or which paths are
actual solutions. Those are the pieces needed before “stable valley” has one native meaning.

The existing families currently use different rule books:

- the constant and field versions of P4 are related, but passing the constant test does not prove
  the stronger field test;
- the time-live and angular-live modules are well-typed possibilities, not yet one shared physical
  motion;
- the Hopfion has a real conditional stability result, but only after supplying its carrier,
  functional, and finite box; and
- bootstrap describes the kind of global/local self-consistency we seek, but not yet the operation
  that performs it.

So the stability hypothesis is **not blocked as an idea**, and it is now better framed. It may be
tested honestly inside each declared conditional model. What we cannot yet do is claim that all of
those valleys belong to one metric-derived landscape or that their count is a native particle
taxonomy.

In the orchestra metaphor: the metric has supplied the concert hall and the instrument families.
Some instruments have conditional sheet music. We are still missing the native score that says
which instruments play together and what counts as a self-sustaining chord.
