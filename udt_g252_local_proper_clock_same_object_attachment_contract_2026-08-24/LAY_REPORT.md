# G252 in lay language

We now have a clean way to put one real unit onto the otherwise scale-free metric picture.

The metric says how much dimensionless proper time lies between two marked events on one observer's
path. An independent clock measures those exact same two events. Dividing the clock reading by the
metric's dimensionless reading fixes the one remaining overall scale.

Nothing was added to the reciprocal kernel. No angular effect was fitted. `c_E` then converts that
clock duration into a length, but does not provide a second knob.

A second clock interval is more valuable than a second fitting parameter: it checks the proposed
dimensionless history. If it gives a different overall scale, that history fails the common-scale
test.

What remains open is choosing and applying a real clock attachment, and then testing whether one
supplied complete history passes multiple independent attachments. G252 did not choose the
universe; it made the first absolute calibration test precise and noncircular.
