## Findings and smallest repairs

### ROD-M1 — changed inputs must flag review, not automatically invalidate assumptions

Plan, Team/continuity paragraph (reviewed lines 163–167), says a changed input
"invalidates the relevant assumptions until checked." This is stronger than
AGENTS' rule that a changed source flags review without automatically refuting,
regrading, or accepting a result. A source change can be editorial, or a result
can survive under unchanged hypotheses. Automatic invalidation could incorrectly
erase valid conditional work and redirect research.

Smallest repair: "A changed input flags the affected assumptions and positive
and negative descendants for review; applicability under the changed input stays
unverified until checked. It does not automatically refute or regrade a result."
The persistent record and disk-authority principle survive unchanged.

### ROD-M2 — distinguish a newly derived equation from an added physical equation

Plan step 1, reviewed lines 63–64, groups "new equation" with prohibited
automatic premise adoption. The earlier permission for ordinary derivation
largely resolves the context, but the unqualified phrase could discourage the
very extraction of consequences the pilot seeks.

Smallest precision repair: refer to "adoption of an additional governing
physical equation" or state that derived equations within the mandate remain
permitted. The boundary is new physical authority, not mathematical novelty.

### ROD-M3 — make the pilot's resource launch gate explicit

The proposal appropriately postpones the exact scientific question and its
charter. Its three-probe/two-repair workload is a useful bound, not yet an
execution budget. Reviewed lines 193–195 nevertheless refer to "existing
CPU/checkpoint limits" without a specific owner, while AGENTS requires actual
work-order and measured-environment limits rather than stale inheritance.

Smallest precision repair: before launch, the approved charter must state the
current CPU/process, memory, output, checkpoint/manual-stop and total workload
limits, plus the remaining discovery/review allowance. Remove the generic
inheritance phrase. This does not require a countdown that kills a valid running
job, does not authorize multi-hour production, and does not require choosing a
scientific question in this process-design turn. The proposal is discussable
now but becomes executable only once that launch information and authority exist.

### ROD-M4 — central integration should preserve epistemic status

Plan step 6, reviewed lines 121–123, says to integrate "accepted scientific
content." The rest of the plan correctly allows reviewed conditional or
unresolved work to be saved. Using "reviewed content at its actual epistemic
status" would make clear that integration and review do not scientifically
promote an unadopted proposal. This is a clarification, not a finding that the
current draft actually adopts a premise.

## Substantive assessment

**Autonomy:** The cycle grants approach changes, counterexamples, unexpected
connections, milestone decisions, review, and bounded repair inside one mandate.
Its question family can accommodate several connected attempts. It does not
require a return after every derivation or use an unresolved result as permission
to invent physics. The precise permission boundary remains the charter.

**Difference from existing practice:** TC0 already had source comparison and a
stop; PCW1 already had independent proposals and review. The draft states this
directly. Its defensible proposed change is prospective allocation, continuity,
and deciding which uncertainty each extension can reduce. It does not establish
that these practices were absent or that they will improve productivity. The
retrospective's then-known/later-result distinction prevents the inspected
process argument from using future outcomes as an original launch test.

**Speculative discovery:** Initial proposals need a plausible cheap probe, not
a proof, desired answer, or novelty quota. The plan preserves numerical and
empirical discovery, permits conditional tools with concrete uses, and allows
unadopted hypothesis families when expressly authorized. A negative outcome can
change a decision without becoming a universal nonexistence theorem. The chosen
first pilot is intentionally narrower than the whole operating method; failure
to obtain a native implication in that pilot must not disqualify other methods.

**Anti-consensus and coordinator bias:** Proposals precede peer/coordinator
solution exposure; alternatives and objections remain recoverable. No vote can
resolve physics, and the fresh review covers the recommended continuation as
well as the candidate. A shared source packet and shared model still create
correlated blind spots; these safeguards reduce some exposure pathways but do
not establish intellectual or model independence. Keep that limitation in the
pilot assessment rather than adding another guaranteed reviewer layer now.

**Overhead:** One work order and notebook, milestone returns, and existing
evidence utilities are proportionate. Two fresh reviewers are a proposed pilot
cost, not evidence of higher scientific quality by themselves. If screening
returns before a candidate exists, any review should inspect the stop decision
and sources actually made load-bearing; it should not manufacture a candidate
certification stage. The effort/administration comparison at pilot return can
test whether the chosen staffing burden is worthwhile.

**Direction decisions and continuity:** The draft asks whether each extension
can change an inference, settle an uncertainty, or enable a use, and records
reopening conditions. It separates conditional tool value from the native
geometry objective. LIVE/HANDOFF/ROADMAP defer the old proposed successor without
rewriting the scientific source, launch no pilot, and retain PIA1 as the
scientific checkpoint. README is a pointer. This is appropriate operational
routing; it is not a scientific descendant regrade.
