# G173 lay report — what happens at a radial turn

G172 measured progress by watching the radius. That works while the pair moves inward or outward.
At a turn, the radius briefly stops changing even if the pair keeps moving sideways around the
sphere.

G173 finds that the geometry itself remains healthy. The pair surface still has one clock
direction and one spatial direction, and its metric stays nondegenerate. The breakdown belongs to
the radial ruler, not to the pair relation.

However, replacing that ruler is not unique. Two rulers made only from the same metric and pair
tangent both behave correctly, both reproduce the old radial answer when motion is purely radial,
and both remain finite at the turn—but they give different scalar readings there. The full metric
tensor is unique; the single-number reading depends on which lawful ruler calibration is used.

There is also a sharp no-go: no finite new ruler can exactly equal the old radial reading at every
point right up to the turn and then remain nonzero at the turn. A change-of-ruler term is
unavoidable.

So the next missing item is smaller than a new physical mechanism. It is whether the native UDT
observer relation supplies a particular ruler calibration through turning motion. G173 does not
choose one.
