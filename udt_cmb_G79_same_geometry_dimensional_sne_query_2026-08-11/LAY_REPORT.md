# G79 in lay language

We finally made one of the trial universes answer two questions at the same time.

First, it told us how much the source clock is redshifted relative to the receiver clock.  Second,
it told us how a small bundle of light is focused or stretched on its way between those same two
observers.  Earlier work often had one of those pieces without the other, or used the old SNe curve
as an input.  Here both numbers came from one complete metric geometry before the old SNe formula
was consulted.

For this particular trial geometry, the redshift factor is about `1.14564` and the angular distance
is `0.755985` times the still-unknown physical size `R`.  A separately written ray calculation got
the same answer to roughly eleven decimal places.

This also records the old CMB-temperature picture for later.  The geometry can act like both a lens
and a redshift map.  The lens says which part of a source sky lands in each observed direction.  The
redshift says how its frequency—and conditionally its temperature scale—is changed.  We will not
apply that idea until after the SNe and/or `X_max` distance curve is better established.  For now we
have built and checked one piece of the optical machine, not generated the observed CMB map.
