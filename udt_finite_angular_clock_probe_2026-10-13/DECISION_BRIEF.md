# FAC1 discussion packet

We found a mathematical restriction worth keeping. For a precisely prepared
small sphere of freely falling clocks, average received slowing depends on a
particular balance between two parts of spacetime curvature. Some supplied GR
geometries give average slowing; a regular rotating Kerr example gives average
speeding. Thus an average-slowing law would exclude specific curvature patterns
while still allowing ordinary tidal effects.

That is a conditional step toward selection, not yet UDT selecting geometry.
The clocks measure the total effect of their geometry and motion. We have not
derived why this laboratory average must represent UDT's additional positional
effect, or where such a law must apply. Both physical claims remain unadopted.

The study also corrected a possible inference without changing an old result:
averaging every direction in the Kasner example gives a positive answer; the
older negative result averaged only the principal axes and said so. Keeping
the two independently emitted signal averages separate also matters: combining
them can hide speeding in one of the comparisons.

Two fresh reviewers checked the mathematics, source meanings and counterexamples.
One independently reproduced the plane-wave result before seeing the answer;
the other independently constructed the recovery argument. Both accepted the
conditional result with caveats. They share the model family; this is not human,
different-model or experimental confirmation. Required repository checks and
publication have their own receipts.

The recommendation is to retain this tool and discuss the physical connection
next: does reciprocal positional slowing constrain an explicitly prepared
angular average of total ticking, or a different relation among actual readings?
The founding interpretation of c_E, ordinary proper clocks and the expectation
of a distant positional effect remain central. A local sign test has not ruled
out that distant effect or required a new postulate.

If AMS were provisionally adopted, its local vacuum consequence would be
4|E|²>=3|B|² for each required observer. Strict inequality ensures short-range
average slowing; equality needs more analysis. E/B are curvature components,
not new physical fields. This would narrow possibilities, not supply a full
field equation, source law or physical scale. Adoption has not been requested
or performed. One alternative is to keep the theorem only as a diagnostic
while pursuing a better justified finite-distance relation.

An exact GR conflict cannot be claimed without specifying the recovery regime.
An actual smooth family can have a positive interval that shrinks to zero while
approaching the negative Kerr example. Distant onset and finite-tolerance
recovery are likewise not excluded. The next proposal should address physical
attribution and regime before spending resources on another geometry survey.

no two-hour ceiling. No GPU, long solve or new premise is needed for this return.
Future investigation needs its own concrete question and bounded work order;
