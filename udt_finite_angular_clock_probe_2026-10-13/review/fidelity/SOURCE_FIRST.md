## Independent recovery argument: a common finite query matters

Fix a compact regular tube containing the prepared clocks and both null
incidences for one positive L and the whole preparation sphere. Suppose a family
of smooth metrics tends smoothly to a control metric there, with smoothly
matched initial event, orthonormal frame, proper L, units and branch. Assume the
limiting incidences remain transverse and the relevant histories stay in the
tube. Geodesic and parallel-transport ODE dependence carries the preparations
and histories continuously. The null incidence implicit equation then carries
the actual arrival events and null tangents continuously. Positive endpoint
frequency contractions stay bounded away from zero on this compact angular
family. Consequently p_+, p_- and their logarithms converge uniformly in n,
and their means converge.

Hence a strictly negative control mean at this fixed L excludes a sequence of
nonnegative means at that *same* query. Either one of the two negative means
already contradicts AMS. Smooth convergence is a sufficient topology for this
argument; I do not claim to have optimized its differentiability threshold.
Holding coordinate curves fixed without preserving the preparation would prove
a different statement. Loss of branch regularity or escape from the common tube
would also remove a stated hypothesis.

This is a continuity argument, not a no-go theorem for all GR recovery. If each
correction parameter permits positivity only for `0<L<r(epsilon)` and
`r(epsilon)->0`, it never promises positivity at the common adverse L. Likewise
a law applying only after a distant onset, a bounded family of tolerances, or a
different permitted query does not inherit this contradiction.

## Actual supplied-metric test of the shrinking-domain escape

This construction was developed here before exposure to FAC1's proposed
recovery argument. Its purpose is to test the quantifier seam, not to introduce
a physical correction, field equation or selected profile. All metric choices
are **free-and-explored supplied controls**. No empirical fitting is involved.

Let g0 be any smooth Ricci-flat metric near o. Choose normal coordinates with
`g0(o)=diag(-1,1,1,1)`, `t(o)=0`, `dt(U)=1`, and define

    u_epsilon=epsilon t^2/2,
    g_epsilon=exp(2u_epsilon) g0=exp(epsilon t^2) g0, epsilon>0.

This is a Lorentz metric on the same fixed local patch, has the same tangent
metric and connection at o, and tends smoothly to g0 on fixed compact subpatches.
Preparations are performed separately in each actual metric, with the same
tangent event/frame and proper preparation length, not with frozen coordinate
receiver histories.

Here is a direct local derivation of its Ricci jet. The conformal connection
difference is

    C^a_bc=delta^a_b u_c+delta^a_c u_b-g0_bc u^a.

At o, du=0, so quadratic C terms vanish. Contracting
`nabla_a C^a_bd-nabla_d C^a_ba` in four dimensions gives
`Delta Ric_bd=-2 Hess(u)_bd-g0_bd Box(u)`. Since
`Hess(u)=epsilon dt tensor dt` and `Box(u)=-epsilon` at o,

    Ric_epsilon(o)=-2 epsilon dt tensor dt+epsilon g0,
    Ric_epsilon(U,U)=-3 epsilon.

Therefore the actual PSW/PRT first-signal sphere means satisfy

    M_+(U,L)=epsilon L^2/2+O_epsilon(L^3),
    M_-(U,L)=epsilon L^2/2+O_epsilon(L^3).

For each fixed epsilon>0, both are strictly positive for sufficiently small
positive L. Indeed for any fixed unit timelike V at o,
`Ric_epsilon(V,V)=-epsilon[2(dt(V))^2+1]<0`, so the same conclusion holds in
each such laboratory, with no uniform radius over unbounded boosts claimed.
No result throughout a whole spacetime region is inferred from this one-event
calculation, and no distant asymptote is constructed.

If the original g0 has a strictly negative mean at some fixed regular L, the
continuity argument proves that the above positive neighborhoods cannot retain
