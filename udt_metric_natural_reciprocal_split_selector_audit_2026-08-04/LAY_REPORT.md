# Lay report — what the metric can and cannot choose

The result is simpler than the long search made it look.

The metric can sometimes point out a natural “clock-and-ruler sheet.” If the geometry is uneven
enough—like the squashed angular geometry—its curvature singles out one spatial direction. Pair
that with the clock direction and the desired two-dimensional sheet appears without us choosing it.
The separate intrinsic-form work finds another such sheet wherever its geometric two-form is
nonzero.

But the perfectly round branch has no distinguished spatial direction. Asking the metric alone to
choose one there is like asking a perfect sphere to mark a unique longitude. Every answer is
equally good, so no unique answer can be natural. We proved that exactly: the symmetry permits
projectors onto nothing, time alone, all of space, or everything—but not time plus one spatial
direction.

That means UDT does not presently have one smooth universal clock/ruler sheet covering every
retained branch. It has a more structured atlas:

- on broken-symmetry regions, the metric can choose a sheet;
- at round, zero, or tied regions, it can at most return a family of equally valid sheets;
- across some defect or collision surfaces, a chosen sheet cannot be continued uniquely; and
- on the observer-query bundle, every queried observer/ruler pair has its own tautological sheet;
  smooth spacetime sheets may exist, but the bundle projection does not naturally choose one.

This is progress rather than another generic “underdetermined.” We have identified a real positive
mechanism, a real obstruction, and the exact seam between them. What remains is an ontology choice
that must come from UDT: does the theory need one physical sheet everywhere, allow branch-derived
stratified sheets, or formulate its comparison law directly on the full query bundle?

No option has been selected, and none of this yet supplies an action, matter source, carrier, or
physical universe branch.
