# Lay report

We tested the main ways the geometry might naturally choose how the original
clock/ruler relationship extends into all four dimensions.

Several candidates really do find useful pieces. Curvature can mark preferred
directions. The angular geometry can identify preferred axes or small sets of
axes. A finite-cell seam can constrain how a choice is joined. None of them,
however, chooses the whole four-dimensional rule everywhere without first
being handed an extra direction, path, boundary, or global branch.

Holonomy—the accumulated turning produced by carrying a frame around curved
space—came closest. Two supplied kinds of global turning each force one
pointwise generator answer: all three spatial directions behave alike, or the three
directions complementary to a chosen ruler behave alike. A different
reciprocal flip forces zero only after we first restrict the problem to a
diagonal form; in the full family it leaves two freedoms, and it is not an
ordinary Lorentz-frame turning. UDT has not yet selected any of these global
structures, so they constrain an answer after that choice; they do not make
the choice. They also do not yet prove that these pointwise answers join into
a global section.

The useful next question is narrower: do the founding postulates themselves
define a consistent global family of four-dimensional extensions and the
rules for joining local descriptions? Only after that could we ask which
members are physically variable. Calling all seven freedoms physical fields
now would assume the answer. This audit does not permit that assumption or an
invented action.

This was a deliberately bounded test of twelve named candidate families, not
every construction mathematics could form from the metric.
