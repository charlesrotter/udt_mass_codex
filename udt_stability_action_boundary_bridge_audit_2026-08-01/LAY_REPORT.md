# Lay report — what the bridge audit learned

The short version is: the missing stability piece cannot simply be borrowed from either of our two
candidate gravity-style action routes.

Think of F01 as a precisely tested table with one unstable leg. C2/Bach and EH tell us broad things
about how a complete table *might* flex. C2/Bach is like a design with more complicated joints; EH
is like a simpler joint design. But neither drawing tells us how its joints connect to this exact
table, nor what shape the missing leg must have.

There is an additional guard: C2/Bach is not currently an active candidate at all. Its old
conditional uniqueness depended on the strong local scale-neutrality premise, which later audits
challenged and made inactive unless Charles explicitly reauthorizes that counterfactual branch.

We proved why resemblance is not enough. You can change the edge construction of a mathematical
system without changing its interior equation at all. That edge change can make the stability test
more positive or more negative. So an interior C2/Bach or EH equation cannot secretly determine the
missing F01 edge response.

This leaves the stability hypothesis alive, but more honest and focused. We now know not to keep
testing isolated families while quietly hoping a familiar action will fill their missing boundary
rules. The next real task is to derive the universe's rule for which variations and boundary
responses are physically allowed. Once that rule exists, the family stability tests can resume on
a common foundation.
