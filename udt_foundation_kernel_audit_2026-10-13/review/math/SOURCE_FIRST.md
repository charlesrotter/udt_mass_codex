## Operational objects before algebra

For a physical received-tick comparison, specify an emitter timelike worldline,
a receiver timelike worldline, emission and reception events, a chosen causal
signal family/path, orientation/time orientation and clock/ruler calibration.
If successive emitter proper-time values map smoothly to receiver proper-time
values by `tau_R = F(tau_E)`, the received emitter ticking per receiver tick is
`q = d tau_E / d tau_R = 1/F'`, where the relevant map must be regular and
future-monotone. Geometric-optics frequency comparison equivalently measures
`(-g(u_R,k_R))/(-g(u_E,k_E))` on a transported null wave covector; that equivalence
presupposes signals actually follow the specified metric null propagation and
their phase/tick identification, rather than deriving signal physics from a
metric's mere existence. A signal/worldline specification is not optional data.

Three different reversals must stay distinct:

1. Invert the same regular event correspondence mathematically. Its derivative
   is reciprocal. This inverse runs back through that correspondence.
2. Send another future-directed signal from the other worldline. This uses new
   events and potentially another path; it need not be the inverse map.
3. Compare assigned coordinate clock rates using a simultaneity convention.
   This need not equal received ticking at all.

The original wording alone does not fix these event assignments. The September
clarification does choose received ticking as intended meaning and thus rules
out substituting (3) as the entire explanation. It does not identify (1) and
(2). Ordinary proper clocks plus one shared geometry can support mutual signal
redshift on suitable worldlines; that fact does not determine a positional law.

## What difference-dependence and composition can prove

The June quotation is substantial and must be included. A strong, mathematically
precise *conditional* interpretation is a positive scalar factor
`D(A,B)=f(phi_B-phi_A)` with `f(0)=1`, continuous at zero, on a signed additive
domain containing the required sums, and composition
`f(x+y)=f(x)f(y)` whenever the terms belong to that domain. Taking logarithms
gives an additive continuous function, hence `f(x)=exp(k x)`. On an open connected
interval the same local result follows with composition restricted to admissible
sums and continued by subdivision. The real constant `k`, its sign and the
normalization/units of `phi` remain unspecified. Without appropriate regularity,
additive pathological exponents are another omission; a physical smoothness
assumption can supply regularity but should be stated.

On a signed group-like domain, `f(x)f(-x)=1`. Requiring both factors strictly below
one for a nonzero pair is impossible. Requiring exchange symmetry `f(x)=f(-x)`
forces `f=1` on that domain. This is a no-go only for identifying mutual received
slowdown with this single invertible endpoint factor; it is not a no-go for
two distinct future signal experiments or the whole founding interpretation.

Strongest favorable alternative: a nonnegative accumulated depth `s` on a causal
or ordered path semigroup can have `f(s)=exp(-k s)`, `k>0`, and additive positive
depth along concatenated paths. Both opposite future paths can then have factors
below one; concatenating them has depth `s_1+s_2`, not zero. But endpoint exchange
is not the group inverse. If `s` is an unsigned spatial separation, additivity
holds only for suitable ordered collinear segments, not arbitrary triples in a
metric space. Composition of actual causal transfers requires matched events and
intermediate clock state. The words do not determine which domain is intended.

An exponential scalar factor alone does not select staticity, spherical symmetry,
areal coordinates, zero shift, a radial profile, a field equation or a reciprocal
relation between two independent metric coefficients. In particular, the
assertion in the quotation that these words uniquely determine the metric is a
claim to test, not an additional proof of uniqueness.

## Supplied metric: what follows and what does not

Write the branch as `ds^2 = -N(r)^2 c_E^2 dt^2 + L(r)^2 dr^2 + r^2 dOmega^2`,
with supplied `N=exp(-phi)`, `L=exp(phi)`, so `N L=1`. On a regular finite-real
smooth-phi interval with `r>0`, it is Lorentzian and static. These metric choices
are pinned to the supplied benchmark, not independently derived from the words.

For static clocks, `d tau=N dt`. A radial null path obeys
`dt/dr = +/- L/(c_E N)`, so `|dr/dt|=c_E exp(-2 phi)`. Static orthonormal ruler and
clock elements give `d ell=L |dr|`, hence `d ell/d tau=c_E` along a radial null ray.
The coordinate speed is not itself a local observed signal speed. Finite positive
N and L give finite coordinate travel time across a compact regular interval.
Neither this local calculation nor the existence of metric cones assigns an
operational value to an underlying infinite speed or derives a global causal
structure without a global completion.

For static endpoints connected through a stationary fixed signal path, travel
time is independent of emission time. Thus `dt_R/dt_E=1` and
`q(A->B)=N_A/N_B=exp(phi_B-phi_A)`. The opposite future experiment between the same
static endpoints has `q(B->A)=N_B/N_A`. Their product is one. Nontrivial mutual
received slowing therefore does not arise for these clocks and paths. This
calculation already holds with **arbitrary positive L(r)**: lapse ratios cannot
select `L=1/N`. Failure of this static control cannot reject dynamic geometries,
moving observers or additional admitted event/path data.

A useful counterexample family for metric uniqueness is fixed positive N with
different smooth positive L in areal radius. Every member has the same static
endpoint clock ratios, difference-dependent exponential N if imposed, and
composition of endpoint lapse factors; their radial spatial geometry and travel
times differ. These are controls against that implication only, not full UDT
models satisfying every founding claim.

## Coordinate and calibration tests required of the candidate

1. `N L=1` is a relation in the declared coordinates and normalization. For a
   general static radial metric, define a new radial coordinate by
   `dx/dr=N L`; then the t-x block has reciprocal coefficients. The angular term
   becomes `r(x)^2 dOmega^2`. Imposing reciprocal coefficients **and** areal radius
   is generally a restriction, not something proved merely by this coordinate
   change. Under constant `t'=alpha t` with numerical c_E held fixed,
   `N'=N/alpha`; any claim of invariant unit determinant must retain calibration.
2. Difference-dependence of an endpoint factor is not invariance of the full
   areal metric under `phi -> phi+a`. For constant phi=a, after rescaling t the
   spatial metric is `exp(2a) dr^2+r^2 dOmega^2`. Its orthonormal angular sectional
   curvature is `(1-exp(-2a))/r^2`, whereas phi=0 is flat. One can obtain this
   directly from proper radial distance `rho=exp(a)r` and the sphere-radius
   derivative `dr/d rho=exp(-a)`. Thus the common shift generally changes geometry
   in the supplied areal branch. No preferred observer, no preferred phi zero,
   homogeneous geometry and coordinate relabeling are different requirements.
3. For any positive calibrated dimensionless pair `(a,b)`, defining
   `chi=(a-b)/(a+b)` implies `chi=tanh((log a-log b)/2)`. If additionally `ab=1`,
   let `a=exp(s)`, `b=exp(-s)` and obtain `chi=tanh(s)`. This identity is exact,
   but its physical force depends on why these are the supplied pair and why
   this ratio is the measured readout. Many other functions of s are available;
   the appearance of tanh alone does not select the readout or pair.
4. The normalized difference cancels common multiplication `(a,b)->(lambda a,
   lambda b)` but not independent recalibrations. Adding an actual time/length
   dimensional pair requires units or normalization before taking its sum.
   The quoted reciprocal identity of c can motivate conversion factors; a unit
   conversion and its reciprocal do not alone produce a nontrivial geometry.

One favorable formalization worth preserving is a continuously composed diagonal
frame map `T(s)=diag(exp(-k s),exp(k s))`. It follows if one independently supplies
diagonality, a one-parameter homomorphism and reciprocal/determinant-one structure
(or a precise length-time exchange involution). Its normalized reciprocal-pair
readout is the stated tanh identity. Those additional restrictions are exactly
where the physical interpretation must be made explicit. Neither determinant-one
structure nor an actual received-tick identification follows merely from calling
the two endpoint directions mutual.
