Charles's founding postulate is the starting point: what we observe as c_E is
mutual slowing of separated clocks, currently interpreted as light travel time.
The audit's earlier emphasis risked reversing that order. The corrected research
begins with clock relations and asks how the metric and kernel represent them.

The reciprocal kernel's algebra survives. Given the full metric, a specified
calibrated comparison and the later adopted completed-pair rule, its value
follows exactly. A local clock interval ratio Z is encoded as
chi=(1-Z²)/(1+Z²), with the source clock used to label the comparison. No
separate light-propagation mechanism is needed for that encoding.

What is still unproved is the shorter historical claim that mutual clock slowing
itself forces the particular inverse time/radial pairing. The earlier argument
inserted an analogy at that point; later work explicitly recorded stronger
pairing premises. Keeping those premises visible preserves their conditional
results. This does not justify discarding hundreds of results or declaring the
full UDT postulates insufficient.

We also tested the constructive direction. Specified mutual clock records can
be represented by one geometry with ordinary freely falling clocks and an
exchange symmetry. In the supplied control, flat and curved geometries give
the same records. Their normalized ruler tapes vary while the chosen
timing-based ruler stays fixed; the flat member's tape equals it. Thus the representation is possible, but those
records alone do not select the whole metric or the physical ruler assignment.
This is a mathematical control, not a claimed UDT solution or new physical law.

the mutual-clock postulate to the physical metric/comparison/ruler assignment?
