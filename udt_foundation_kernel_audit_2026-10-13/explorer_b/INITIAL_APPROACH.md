## Question and first probe

Can one distinguish what the supplied static spherical metric fixes from what
is required to predict two observers' received tick rates, and then locate the
extra assumptions needed to identify a reciprocal scalar contrast with that
comparison?

Start operationally with two future-directed timelike worldlines, their proper
clocks, and two separately specified families of future-directed null signals.
The mathematical inverse of one emission-to-reception map need not be the
opposite signal experiment. Derive both maps on the benchmark for stationary
radial clocks, directly from null propagation and proper-time increments.
Compare this result with the benchmark's reciprocal lapse/radial scale factors.

The first probe is exact and analytic. Favorable: the same metric-derived
quantity controls the intended mutual received slowdown after every comparison
choice is explicit. Adverse: a concrete admitted benchmark preparation yields
an inverse pair, a one-sided redshift or no redshift rather than mutual slowdown.
Ambiguous: the founding words do not select observer motion, clock pairing or
the required comparison, leaving multiple valid preparations with different
results. None of these alone settles all realizations of the founding words.
