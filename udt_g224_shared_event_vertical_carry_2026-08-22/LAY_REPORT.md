# G224 lay report

## What changed

The missing local handoff between two observer comparisons is simpler than it looked.

Imagine one lightlike ruler arriving at observer `B` and another leaving `B`. They may point in
different directions and may use completely different affine scales. Observer `B`'s own
metric-normalized clock measures the scale of each one. There is exactly one way to rescale the
outgoing ruler so that `B` measures it the same way as the incoming ruler.

That is the missing local coupling. It is not fitted and it is not an extra mechanism.

## What it buys us

Along each supplied observer relation, the clock changes by a factor `r`. The reciprocal ruler
amplitude changes by exactly `1/r`. At a real shared middle event, the handoff adds no new free
number. Therefore a chain `A -> B -> C` carries the reciprocal ruler by

\[
\frac1{r_{AB}r_{BC}},
\]

exactly opposite to the clock product.

## What it does not buy us

It does not decide which lightlike relations Nature realizes. It does not align angular screens,
choose a path, or force a separately constructed direct `A -> C` relation to agree with the broken
route. Those are different questions.

One preregistered sentence was too strict: two null lines at different events can still be
abstractly normalized by their local observer clocks. But without a shared event they are not a
physical composition vertex. That correction narrows the remaining gap rather than reopening it.
