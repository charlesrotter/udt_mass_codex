## 3. The pointwise receiver limit does not imply a fixed-emission population limit

The following is an actual free-geodesic family, rather than CPW's arbitrary
unit-vector illustration or FCL's accelerated-clock control. In the same
g=x^-2 eta, let the emitter remain at y=0, with the reference emission e=(1,0).
The rightward ray has conformal affine tangent kbar=(-1,1,0,0); its physical
emission frequency is 1. For each epsilon in (0,1), select reception

    q_epsilon=(x=epsilon,y=1-epsilon),
    T=u/x=(-cosh rho,sinh rho,0,0), rho=log epsilon

at that event, and take the unique physical geodesic with that tangent.
Along any one such geodesic the conserved spatial momentum is

    P=sinh rho/x,    sinh rho(x)=P x,
    dx/dtau=-x sqrt(1+P^2 x^2),
    dy/dx=-P x/sqrt(1+P^2 x^2).

These follow from y-translation symmetry and unit normalization, with no
field equation. At the selected event

    P_epsilon=(1-epsilon^-2)/2,
    omega_o=epsilon(cosh rho-sinh rho)
           =epsilon exp(-log epsilon)=1,
    Z_epsilon=1.

Each P_epsilon is finite separately. Each receiver extends back to the interior
slice x=1 with finite initial velocity and position; explicitly, for P!=0,

    y(x)-y(1)=[sqrt(1+P^2)-sqrt(1+P^2 x^2)]/P,
    y(1)=1-epsilon-[sqrt(1+P^2)-sqrt(1+P^2 epsilon^2)]/P.

Its late endpoint is finite and its own rescaled velocity tends to (-1,0,0,0),
since P is fixed along that receiver and P x->0. FCL holds for every receiver.
Across the chosen population, however, P_epsilon tends to negative infinity.
The reception events approach the same boundary point (0,1), yet Z=1.
The initial positions even remain bounded and tend to y=2; the missing
uniform control is the initial momentum. No one worldline is being switched
between velocities or asserted to violate geodesic motion.

There is also an independent proper-clock check. For nearby emitter events
on y=0, null incidence gives x_e=x+y(x) and tau_e=-log x_e. On a fixed receiver,

    Z=(d tau_o/dx)/(d tau_e/dx)
      =x_e/[x(sqrt(1+P^2 x^2)-P x)].

At q_epsilon, x_e=1 and the denominator equals 1, again giving Z=1.
This confirms the actual received-tick derivative, not only a chosen null
normalization. It is exact analytic checking with a second identity, not an
independent implementation or sampled numerical certification.

Defective inference: exchange “for every fixed finite-data receiver, as its
own endpoint is approached” with “uniformly over receivers at late events.”

bounds and a common regular branch. This control does not refute an intended
