## Lead 1: a finite last-emission time with an invariant clock residue

Nearest previous attempt: FCW1 gives a pole in an initial-distance/conformal
label in a supplied homogeneous sector. CPW1 separates the general conformal

The following consequence expresses that result directly through two proper
clocks and avoids identifying Omega with distance.

Let τ be FCL1's receiver proper time, s the emitter proper time, s_* the
regular interior emitter limit, and x=Omega_o on the receiver. Normalize each
complete ray to physical ω_e=1 as in FCL1. Its exact collar equations give

    Z = dτ/ds = 1/(x B(x)),
    dx/dτ = -x γ(x)/N(x),
    B(x) -> B_* > 0,  γ(x) -> 1,  N(x) -> N_* > 0.

Consequently

    ds/dx = -N(x)B(x)/γ(x),
    s_* - s(x) = integral_0^x [N(v)B(v)/γ(v)] dv,
    (s_* - s)/x -> N_* B_*,
    (s_* - s) Z -> N_*.

The integral identity uses the supplied regular emitter limit and the actual
monotone null correspondence. The positive finite integrand limit proves the
ratio limit by elementary averaging, not by differentiating an uncontrolled
asymptotic remainder. FCL1's proper-time result then gives

    τ = -N_* log((s_* - s)/s_ref) + C + o(1),

where s_ref>0 is only a dimensional reference for the logarithm and C is a
finite origin-dependent constant. The new residue N_* is geometric:

    N_* = [-gbar^{-1}(dx,dx)|_p]^(-1/2).

Under x'=a x, gbar'=a² gbar with smooth finite positive a, dx'=a dx at the
boundary; the inverse-metric factors cancel. N_* and the proper-clock product
above are invariant under these regular conformal gauges. Its value is not
selected. Proper times here use c_E=1 length units; a seconds convention makes
the corresponding unit conversion. This is not an X_max assignment.
