# Lay report — did stitching the universe together identify `phi`?

Not yet. But the reason is now much clearer.

Imagine every local map of the same landscape has two layers: the actual landscape and a transparent
measuring grid laid over it. You can slide the measuring grids and update the instructions for how
one map overlaps the next, while the landscape itself stays exactly the same.

That is what the exact calculation found. We changed the local `phi` labels differently in three
overlapping regions. The reference grids and their overlap instructions changed in compensation.
The complete spacetime geometry did not change, and all three regions still stitched together
perfectly.

Global structure is not empty. The stitched object can remember things such as whether a loop flips
the reciprocal orientation or carries a nonzero period. A finite-cell seam can also restrict the
actual geometry on its two sides. But these are like facts about how the atlas is bound; they do not
tell us where to place the zero mark on every measuring grid.

If we independently declare one reference atlas physical, or independently know the actual
observer-to-observer depth comparisons, most of the freedom disappears. But UDT has not yet derived
either declaration in the current record. A branch of the geometry may also provide its own natural
depth marker, but known versions work only away from collisions and degeneracies.

So this was productive rather than circular: it closes one tempting route. Ordinary global gluing
cannot be the missing selector. The missing joint is a rule saying how the derived reciprocal
comparison is **owned by the physical geometry and observer query**, not another local curvature
calculation.
