# Lay readout

The old work did not secretly contain valid lepton or quark predictions. It had too many selectable
ingredients: familiar particle labels, imported physics, chosen small numbers and powers of `pi`,
and missing calculation files. Close numerical matches produced that way cannot tell us that the
metric predicted the particles.

But it did contain one worthwhile way of looking.

Imagine the angular part of the metric as a small flexible picture frame. We had recently been
asking mostly how the whole frame expands or contracts. That is one motion. The old work reminded us
to inventory **all** of the frame's elementary motions before naming any of them as physics:

- the whole frame can change area;
- it can rotate;
- it can stretch one way while squeezing the perpendicular way; and
- that stretch direction can be turned by 45 degrees.

Those are exactly four independent angular responses: one area, one rotation, and two shape modes.
This is not speculative—the current metric audits already contain those four kinds of object, and
their algebra closes exactly.

So the useful discovery is not “the old particle formulas were right.” It is: **we may have been
listening too hard to one angular instrument.** The scalar called `lambda` is only the equal-in-all-
directions area response. The full angular orchestra also contains rotation and two shears.

That gives a clean next step: carry this four-part response through the already mapped complete
finite-cell branches and watch what the metric itself permits, mixes, preserves, or degenerates.
Only afterward should we ask whether any resulting structure resembles a carrier, force, or matter.
