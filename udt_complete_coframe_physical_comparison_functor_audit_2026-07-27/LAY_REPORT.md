# Lay report

We tested every known way the basic clock/ruler reciprocity could be extended into the other two
directions of the four-dimensional metric.

The result has three parts.

First, in the registered way of writing the complete frame, there are seven infinitesimal choices
left: three describe how the angular sector changes with depth, and four describe how it mixes with
the clock/ruler sector. All seven change the metric to first order in this chart; they are not merely
seven coordinate labels. This is not yet a count of physical degrees of freedom.

Second, mathematical consistency does not choose among them. Every choice can be carried along a
specified path and composes correctly with the next path, provided the middle observer uses the
transported version of that choice. This explains why repeated composition audits have not selected
the missing answer: composition is permissive here, not selective.

Third, when any of these generators is inserted into the full-twist test geometry, it cannot be
compressed into one path-independent endpoint generator. Its curvature rotates the complete frame
too richly. The path-labelled version still works perfectly. Another, more special whole solution
could have reduced curvature/holonomy and allow endpoint collapse, so this is not a universal
rejection and does not say that all transport itself is path-independent.

In plain language, we now know the shape of the missing joint. UDT needs an active, authoritative
rule—not merely a mathematically available construction—that looks at the complete metric and
founded observer pair and chooses how reciprocity acts on the angular and mixing sectors. That rule
must also say how choices are transported and whether the result belongs to a path or only to its
endpoints. None of the current active principles supplies it.

The all-pairs clock network still has enough information to support a future field-level law once
the observer/event variation domain is selected. What is missing is the UDT-selected complete
extension, its global typing, and the target that network must match—not a lack of mathematical
room.
