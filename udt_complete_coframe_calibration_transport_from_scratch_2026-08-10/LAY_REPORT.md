# Lay report

The old survey was asking, “Do we already have an equation somewhere that resets the tape between
observers?” The clean derivation shows that this was one layer too low.

Ordinary geometric transport is like carrying a perfectly rigid ruler through curved space. It can
rotate the ruler and bring it back twisted, but it does not stretch it. That is why the metric's
canonical connections kept returning zero dilation.

The real positional dilation lives in the complete two-observer tape itself. Once that tape is
specified, its clock width and ruler width give one exact imbalance. As the tape changes, the rate
of change of that imbalance is fixed. Time dependence, angular structure, and mixing all change the
tape before this reading is made; none was switched off.

If A-to-B, B-to-C, and A-to-C are pieces of one common calibrated tape system, the middle reading
cancels automatically. There is no extra scalar reset mechanism to discover. If each tape is built
independently, their calibrations need a transition rule.

So the missing piece is now simpler: what in the completed UDT geometry chooses or constructs one
common observer-pair tape family? A global bootstrap or on-shell law could eventually own it, but
this calculation does not assume which one.
