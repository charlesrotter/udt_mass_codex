# Lay report

We tried the four steps in the required order and stopped at the safety gate.

The first question was: does the UDT metric already contain a rule that says which complete
universe configuration is the real one? The answer, in the current record, is no. The familiar
Cartan and curvature relations are more like instructions for measuring any assembled machine.
They tell us its connections and curvature, but they do not choose which machine nature builds.

Reciprocity and the finite-cell work do more: they specify important parts and restrict how those
parts can fit. But several complete assemblies still satisfy those rules. Bootstrap has the right
big-picture shape—a universe whose global state and local matter/geometry tune each other—but the
two actual tuning rules have not yet been derived.

The second question was whether the finite-cell seal supplies the missing boundary rule. It does
not. Knowing that `phi` is zero and odd at the seal still leaves its slope, the other fields, and
the curvature free. More importantly, the right boundary conditions depend on the missing bulk
equation. A two-derivative equation and a four-derivative equation require different boundary data.

So we did **not** invent an equation and run a solve. That would have manufactured the desired
certificate. The live time-and-angular solve and the final certificate were correctly withheld.

The gain is a clean diagnosis: the missing piece is a genuine whole-universe response map, most
plausibly of the already identified two-way bootstrap type. It may later yield an action, or the
action may remain downstream. But the current premises do not yet supply that map.
