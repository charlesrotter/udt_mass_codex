# G326 lay report

G325 tested changes that stretch or shrink the three main directions without tilting them. G326
tested every spatially uniform way those directions can tilt into one another.

There are six such mathematical changes. Five do not change the local spacetime geometry: on an
infinite cover they are merely changes of coordinates. But those coordinate changes cannot be made
smoothly and periodically on the fixed three-dimensional torus. They therefore change how the
torus is globally joined—real global shape/orientation choices, not local gravity and not gauge.

The sixth change is different. It changes the local tidal curvature, providing the second component
of the same Kasner shear whose first component appeared in G325. The two shear components combine
like the two entries of one direction-independent shear magnitude.

Together G325 and G326 have now found every spatially uniform first-order change in this chosen
spacetime: one time-coordinate freedom, eight global torus-shape/orientation choices, two local
shear components, and one curvature-scale change. That is a substantial completion of the
homogeneous perturbation base.

It still does not show that the spacetime is stable. Disturbances that vary from place to place—the
nonzero Fourier modes—have not yet been classified, and neither have nonlinear interactions. No
metric, reciprocal kernel, angular behavior, physical scale, or universe-selection premise changed.
