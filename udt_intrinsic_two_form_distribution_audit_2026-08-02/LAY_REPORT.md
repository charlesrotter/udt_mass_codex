# Lay report — what the metric's new two-form actually does

The previous step found that six complete test geometries contain two independently changing
metric quantities: reciprocal depth and angular area. Their joint change makes a geometric
two-form. This audit asked what direction structure that two-form creates, without pretending it is
a particle or force.

The answer is surprisingly concrete.

Where the two-form is nonzero, it leaves exactly two directions untouched: the clock direction and
one additional spatial line. That spatial line is genuinely made by the metric. It is not put in by
hand.

But it is not a single globally smooth arrow through the whole finite cell:

- on one exact set, the line lies entirely inside the angular screen;
- on a larger open set, it mixes the reciprocal ruler and angular screen;
- it never lines up exactly with the reciprocal ruler in this ensemble;
- on an equatorial sheet the two-form vanishes, but the signless line can usually be continued
  smoothly through it;
- on three intersecting great circles, different approaches give different limiting lines, so no
  unique continuation exists there.

A useful picture is a grain pattern in a transparent sphere. Most places have a definite grain
direction. Across most of one thin sheet the grain temporarily fades but resumes with the same
orientation on the other side. Along three special circular seams, however, the grain can approach
in more than one direction. Those seams are genuine geometric defects of this chosen profile, not
numerical noise.

This is steady progress because the metric has supplied a new building block and its complete
failure set. It is not closure: the line is not yet a carrier, a Hopf field, matter, or a law of
motion, and no tested branch is selected as the universe.
