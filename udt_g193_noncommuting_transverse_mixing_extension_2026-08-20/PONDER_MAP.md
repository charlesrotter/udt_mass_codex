# G193 ponder map — one independent transverse-mixing extension

Date: 2026-08-20

## Lay frame

G192 allowed one transverse mixing voice.  A fixed screen rotation isolated that voice from a
passive direction, and the two resulting scalar equations factorized exactly.  G193 asks whether
that cleanliness came from the metric architecture or from having only one active direction.

The least-imposed next tile adds one new arbitrary smooth voice that couples the two screen
directions.  Its relative strength may change with time.  Therefore the preferred screen axes may
rotate and the mixing matrices at different times need not commute.  No function is fitted and no
desired sign, loud/quiet/loud pattern, asymptote, or caustic behavior is imposed.

## Whole versus bounded slice

The whole complete-coframe problem permits a general time- and space-dependent four-dimensional
coframe, arbitrary pair germs, screen anisotropy and rotation, singular strata, and global branch
structure.  G193 is one analytic tile inside that arena: arbitrary time dependence in one common
scale and two symmetric transverse-mixing functions along one supplied central pair.

This tile is stronger than G192 because it includes genuinely noncommuting screen histories.  It
is not the whole complete metric.  In particular, a third independent symmetric screen component,
an antisymmetric/rotational connection channel, spatial dependence, other pair germs, and global
topology remain outside the calculation.

## Observing, not targeting

The question is:

> What frequency, full matrix tide, Jacobi map, and caustic classes does this enlarged metric
> family contain?

It is not:

> Can we preserve G192's factorization or no-caustic result?

Either preservation or failure is an acceptable result.  Turns and caustics are characterized,
not filtered.
