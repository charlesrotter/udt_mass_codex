# G181 lay report

G180 gave us a reliable measuring tape wherever the observer-pair geometry is smooth. G181 asked
what happens when that tape appears to stop or bunch up at an endpoint.

The key result is simple: a zero tape rate does not necessarily mean spacetime or the pair relation
has broken. Sometimes the temporary coordinate has merely paused—like tracing a straight line with
a pen whose hand slows to zero. Rewriting the relation in the completed ruler coordinate removes
that pause exactly.

Other endpoints are genuine boundaries of the present calculation: the completed clock/ruler
coefficients may diverge, the endpoint may lie infinitely far along the completed tape, or two
branches may meet in a cusp that the metric alone cannot glue smoothly.

The amount of tape accumulated and the reciprocal depth are also separate questions at this
boundary. Finite tape can end at finite, infinite, or oscillating depth; infinite tape can do the
same. The current metric identities classify all of these possibilities but do not choose one.

For the primary UDT metric, angular motion protects a radial turning point from becoming singular.
The first zero occurs only when the entire spatial pair tangent vanishes, not merely when radial
motion pauses.
