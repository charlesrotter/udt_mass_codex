# G182 lay report

Two completed pieces of the observer-pair tape can meet in three increasingly demanding ways.

1. Their reciprocal clock reading can agree.
2. Their entire intrinsic tape metric—including the sideways clock shift—can agree smoothly.
3. The actual observer-pair surface can continue through the join with the same direction and
   curvature.

Each step is stronger than the one before it. The first is not enough for the second, and the second
is not enough for the third.

The important simplification is that no new scalar or mechanism is missing. The extra information
needed for the third step is the complete pair tangent already supplied before the metric pullback.
The two-dimensional metric remembers its magnitude but can forget its direction, just as a
speedometer can report the same speed while a car turns around or turns a corner.

This explains the G181 stalls. An odd-power stall continues straight after the completed tape
coordinate removes the apparent stop. An even-power stall turns back: the tape metric stays smooth,
but the realized path has a cusp. The distinction is carried by direction, not by reciprocal depth.

The result classifies supplied joins. It does not choose which branches Nature realizes.
