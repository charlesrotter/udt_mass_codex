# G141 lay report

The algebraic direction of a carried calibration difference was hiding in the words “A compared
with B.”

If two observer endpoints have already been admitted to one matched calibration family, each has a
complete clock-versus-ruler reading after the whole metric orchestra has played. The constructed
comparison is the difference between B's reading and A's reading; it is not a new number painted
onto the line afterward.

That is like altitude: the difference from A to B changes sign when you travel from B to A, and
altitude differences add through C automatically. No city has to be the center of the Earth, and
no preferred zero altitude is needed.

Mathematically, the endpoint pair metrics each produce a triangular clock/ruler factor in the
supplied calibrated basis. Comparing those factors gives one calibration matrix. Its diagonal
imbalance is exactly B's endpoint `Phi` minus A's endpoint `Phi`. Its shift remains present as
another channel, and all angular and mixing effects have already modified the endpoint metrics
before the comparison is read.

This removes one algebraic mystery: sign, reversal, and three-observer composition follow inside a
properly typed supplied endpoint family. It does **not** yet prove that this constructed calibration
difference is Nature's physical observer-pair depth. The theory must still derive—or the operational
query must supply—which endpoint states share one carried family and whether this is the physical
inverse. G141 does not choose the universe or determine `X_max`.
