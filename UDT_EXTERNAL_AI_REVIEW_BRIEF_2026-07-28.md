Classify response rank, eigenlines, invariant subspaces, and all degeneracies on `b>0`, then connect
them to regular cap limits where the orbit rank drops and `G3^-1 X(G3)` is undefined. Determine
which distinctions are coordinate, orbit-type, or genuinely metric-invariant.

### P3. Selection may be the wrong demand

Audit whether UDT actually requires one globally owned reciprocal plane, or whether a set-valued,
symmetry-related, chartwise, or whole-solution object is the correct metric output. A two-plane
countercontrol might expose a missing selector, or it might show that asking for a unique local
plane is a type error.

### P4. Global-local closure and variation domain

Determine the smallest complete off-shell object on which UDT can vary before an action is chosen.
Test whether reciprocal depth, angular transport/topology, finite-cell completion, and global
matter-energy-curvature consistency form one closure problem. Do not invent a bootstrap equation or
use density as an independent tuner before a native matter/source definition exists.

### P5. Hopf geometry versus carrier emergence

or Hopf fiber into a selected section, transport law, target geometry, and deformation space—or
prove that the current metric data cannot do so. Do not identify an `S2` fiber with the `S2` carrier
by topology alone.

Reassess whether the action should select the closure or instead be derived downstream from an
already selected whole-solution/variation domain. Preserve the present `C2`/Bach and EH conditional
statuses and the July 1 provenance firewall.

### P7. Scale, `X_max`, and matter

dimensional datum or mass law. Test whether a complete global matter-curvature closure can jointly
select `X_max`, total proper density, and stable-matter parameters without circular definitions.
