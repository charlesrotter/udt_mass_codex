## What we learned

Angular cancellation is a meaningful geometric discriminator, but it is
not by itself an identification of the law governing geometric response.
The distinction can be made exact, not just illustrated by another example.

In the declared positive one-function static-spherical class, the familiar
trace-free Ricci response S and the already-studied quadratic response Q
share the ENTIRE balanced family f=1+a r²+b/r. Q also admits a second family
f=1+b/r+d/r². The geometric angular-cancellation test excludes its d!=0
members. It leaves both response formulas compatible with all the remaining
backgrounds. No coefficient is identified as charge, mass or physical content.

The first-order test of Q at a common Ricci-flat background is misleading
if interpreted as an exact freedom: its residual has zero first variation,
but only two specified families of profile directions extend to exact nearby
solutions. A direction from either family works on its own; a particular sum
of directions from the two families cannot work, even after higher-order
corrections. This is an integrability constraint, not a physical superposition
law or a time-evolution/stability result.

Applying angular cancellation to those exact solution directions again
leaves precisely the S-family directions. Thus the declared filter really
restricts geometric possibilities, yet still does not select between these
two response formulas on the filtered class.
