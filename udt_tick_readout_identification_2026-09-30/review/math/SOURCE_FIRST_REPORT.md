## Independent geometric argument

Along an affine null geodesic k, a variation J through null geodesics satisfies
nabla_k J=nabla_J k and nabla_k k=0. Therefore

    d g(k,J)/d lambda = g(k,nabla_J k) = (1/2) J[g(k,k)] = 0.

Endpoint variations with source proper time as parameter give J_e=u_e and
J_o=A'(tau_e)u_o (extra multiples of k caused by changing parameter endpoints
have zero contraction with k). Thus

    Z=A'=(-g(k_e,u_e))/(-g(k_o,u_o)) > 0.

This uses no population. Proper clock origins affect additive arrival labels,
not the derivative; the full map is fixed only once corresponding events and
clock origins are supplied. A single isolated null segment gives the contraction
ratio; identifying it with a derivative requires the stated regular family.
The value Z is not an absolute emitted or received tick rate without the source
record. Known emitted intervals map to differences of A, or the integral of Z.

A positive scaling k -> a k that is constant along each ray leaves affineness
and Z unchanged. It may vary smoothly between rays of the family. An arbitrary
factor varying along a ray generally destroys affineness; the cancellation does
not license using the affine transport equation unchanged in that parameter.
A proper clock can normalize -g(u_e,k_e)=1 for convenience. This need not make k
a physical photon momentum or attach any energy scale to it.

At an intermediate event on the same uninterrupted ray choose any future unit
w. Set omega_m=-g(w,k_m)>0. Then

    (omega_e/omega_m)(omega_m/omega_o)=omega_e/omega_o.

Changing w changes both factors reciprocally. It does not change physical
endpoint clocks, the branch, or Z. Changing an endpoint worldline changes the
actual query and can change Z. A physical relay is not this cancellation.
FSL1's carried-direction composition provides the full Lorentz-frame version.
Its orientation repair remains necessary for the claim SO+(1,3); the scalar
contraction argument itself needs no spatial orientation choice.

## Normalization of a ray versus change of a population

An unparametrized future null ray has no distinguished positive magnitude. A
representation k=alpha ell can be rewritten ell'=a ell, alpha'=alpha/a while
holding k fixed. Expressing a fixed population in this new representation must
carry its scalar coordinate and measure consistently. Its tensor M is unchanged;
this is only geometric bookkeeping.

PRI1 instead supplies a measure nu on actual future nonzero null vectors, with
finite second moment M=integral k_flat tensor k_flat dnu. If one maps each vector
k to a(k)k and takes the pushforward measure while retaining its original atomic
weights, then

    M_new = integral a(k)^2 k_flat tensor k_flat dnu.

That is generally a different mathematical population. Per-ray Z values still
cancel a(k), but M and its minimizer can change. A common positive a multiplies
M by a^2 and preserves its minimizer, while changing its magnitude. Direction-
dependent scaling can change the minimizer. Conversely changing the measure
weight by a^-2 can preserve M but generally changes first moments/counting data:
preserving one moment is not proof of preserving a complete population.

Explicit control: equal weights at k+=(1,1,0,0) and k-=(1,-1,0,0) give a rest
second-moment observer. Replacing k+ by 4k+ gives effective moment weights16:1
and minimizer exp(r)=2, hence u_M=(5/4,3/4,0,0). The normalized ray support and
individual Z values are unchanged. This refutes only recovery of this population
rest assignment from those ray ratios alone; it does not refute reconstruction
from sufficiently rich independently supplied population information. Tick
counts are not automatically the measure nu: that identification would itself
need a supplied protocol, not an imported energy or detector interpretation.

PRI1's uniqueness is uniqueness for its specified minimization criterion, not

## Supplied records and several streams

Let mu_e be a supplied locally finite counting measure on an emission proper-
time interval. For the stipulated lossless one-to-one labelled correspondence,
mu_o=A_*mu_e, i.e. mu_o(B)=mu_e(A^-1 B). This covers discrete ticks without a
continuous density. If mu_e has density r_e, the additional density assumption
gives r_o(A(s))=r_e(s)/Z(s), by the change of variables ds=d tau_o/Z. This is
counting transport, with no energy or detector law. Finite windows are handled
by preimages; no constant-Z approximation is needed.

For several streams arriving at the same proper clock, use their disjoint
labelled emission spaces, supplied measures mu_i and maps A_i into that common
receiving time coordinate. The ideal complete count is sum_i (A_i)_*mu_i.
Other weights, acceptance, merging or recording rules are extra supplied data.
If endpoints have different receiving clocks, addition first requires a common
recording coordinate/correspondence; geometric arrival maps alone do not create
one. No universal weighted mean Z is selected by this identity.

A compatible Minkowski control uses the receiver x=0 and two emitting clocks

propagate rightwards and arrive with A_1(s)=10+2s, A_2(s)=10+s/2. Both maps have
