# Lay report — the middle chord is now frozen

We have done something deliberately modest but important.

The supernova data already told us the shape of one observer-to-observer relationship over the
middle, astronomical regime. We have now written that relationship onto a card, sealed the card,
and promised not to rewrite it after looking at BAO, the CMB, or the far endpoint.

What is on the card is the brightness-distance curve and its reciprocal clock reading. What is not
on the card is which member of the full orchestra produced it. Supernova brightness alone cannot
tell us how much came from the angular geometry, mixing, the screen, or the complete time-live
history. It also cannot by itself prove the law by which light carries energy.

So this is a real middle-regime anchor, but not yet a spacetime model. It gives future work a fixed
note that the complete orchestra must hit. BAO and CMB now become honest exams rather than extra
knobs for tuning that note.

One technical limitation matters: we have the best central curve and separate uncertainty ranges,
but the old saved result does not contain the full joint uncertainty map between shape and absolute
scale. We therefore freeze the central curve and refuse to invent a more precise error band.
