## 1. The boost analogy: exact identities, different objects

At one event in 1+1 Minkowski geometry, two future unit timelike velocities have
relative rapidity eta, beta=tanh(eta), gamma=cosh(eta). A longitudinal **receding
received-clock interval factor** is D=exp(eta), hence

    D = gamma(1+beta),       gamma = (D + D^-1)/2.

Gamma describes time dilation with a simultaneity convention; D includes the
changing null travel path. They differ already at small eta:
gamma=1+eta^2/2+O(eta^4), D=1+eta+eta^2/2+O(eta^3). Their identical divergent
limit does not make them the same observable.

For an explicit actual forward/return check, take inertial A at x=0 and B at
x=beta t after a common origin, beta in (0,1). A's signal emitted at t_e>0
intersects B at t_B=t_e/(1-beta). B's proper time is tau_B=t_B/gamma, so
d tau_B/d t_e=D. A return pulse emitted by B at tau_B reaches A at
t_r=gamma(1+beta) tau_B, giving d t_r/d tau_B=D. Thus both directions have
the same receding Doppler redshift, while a prompt echo has derivative D^2.
Future return does not mean inversion of the original event map.

A Lorentz boost B obeys B^T eta B=eta. By contrast the diagonal deformation
diag(exp(-delta),exp(delta)) in an orthonormal time/space basis yields the
nontrivial reciprocal metric diag(-exp(-2delta),exp(2delta)); it is not a
Lorentz boost except at delta=0. A boost *is* diagonal with reciprocal entries
in a null basis, where the metric is off-diagonal. Confusing these bases erases
the distinction between changing observer components and changing a marked
clock/ruler pullback. Similar tanh formulas do not identify beta with chi_pair.

The limit eta→infinity is the boundary of normalized timelike velocity space:
