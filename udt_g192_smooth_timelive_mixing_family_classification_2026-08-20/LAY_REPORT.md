# G192 lay report

G191 used an orchestra whose overall volume rose steadily and whose mixing instrument held one
constant note. G192 let both of those vary freely with separation/time inside the same metric
construction.

The result is simpler than expected:

- The overall volume controls the observed frequency. If that volume reverses direction, the
  frequency reverses too. So G191's smooth one-way redshift was a feature of its chosen exponential
  control, not an automatic law.
- Mixing affects the two image directions through both its current strength and how fast that
  strength is changing. Neither contribution may be switched off.
- Those two mixing contributions fit together in an exact mathematical factorization. Within this
  entire smooth family, the screen can shear and rotate its response, but it never collapses to a
  nonvertex focus. That part of G191 was not an accident of constant coefficients.
- The sideways response can be positive, negative, or zero. The metric determines it from the live
  mixing history; there is no universal extra angular coefficient.

This is real simplification and a wider native result, but it is still one carefully declared
two-function family. It does not yet say that every possible UDT metric history is caustic-free or
tell us which smooth functions Nature realizes.
