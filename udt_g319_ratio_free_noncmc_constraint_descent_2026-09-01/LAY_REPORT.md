# G319 lay report

The new result is that G318's restrictive behavior came from one simplifying assumption, not from
the full constraint equations.

G318 temporarily required two changing parts of the initial geometry to stay in a fixed ratio. That
made the equations choose a special power-law relationship and ruled out some power classes. G319
removed only that fixed-ratio requirement and kept the rest of the same narrow test frame.

Without the fixed ratio, the two constraints interlock into one exact bookkeeping constant. Given
any smooth, positive, repeating shape for `psi`, that constant can be chosen large enough for the
remaining initial-data functions to be reconstructed smoothly, with the requested positive or
negative sign of `tau`. Four different nonconstant shapes, each in both signs, were checked directly.

In plain language: within this test frame, the constraint equations make all the parts agree with
one another, but they do not choose the shape. They are more like consistency requirements on a
snapshot than a rule selecting which snapshot Nature uses.

G318 was not wrong. Its power law, its obstruction, and its periodic tidal family all survive when
the fixed-ratio assumption is deliberately restored. They are now correctly understood as a
special subfamily, not as restrictions on every member of the broader solution space.

There is one unfinished edge case: the exact reconstruction formula divides by a quantity called
`B`. At places where `B` is zero, the original equations remain meaningful and impose special
matching conditions, but this run did not classify every possible global crossing. Those cases were
kept visible rather than discarded.

Most importantly, this run did not change the UDT metric or reciprocal kernel, did not fit data,
and did not choose a universe, history, scale, topology, or `X_max`. It clarified how broad the
allowed initial snapshots are in one deliberately restricted mathematical test.
