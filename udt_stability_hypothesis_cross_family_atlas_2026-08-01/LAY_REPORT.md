# Lay report

The stability idea did not begin with one special Hopfion and then spread outward. It began with a
different set of simplified UDT cells where changing angular conditions changed whether a
configuration had an unstable direction, and where an exact inequality separated a stable sector
from an unstable one.

The Hopfion later gave the picture a much better three-dimensional example. It is like finding a
real, intricate arch after first learning the load-balancing rule from beams and braces. Removing
the arch would not erase the rule, but it would remove our best example that the idea can work in a
fully three-dimensional topological structure.

Other objects play different roles. Massless rings tell us that some architectural layouts cannot
carry mass. Empty cell types tell us that some layouts cannot be built at all. The formal time and
angular modules tell us which pieces fit on paper but have not yet been assembled into one running
machine. None of those is automatically a stable particle.

The shared picture is therefore modest but useful: UDT appears to contain families of possible
structures, and mathematical consistency or stability eliminates some branches while retaining
others under stated assumptions. What we do not yet possess is one UDT-native rule that performs
this sorting across every family, proves persistence through time, or lets the whole universe choose
the surviving catalogue.

Nor do we yet have a row of isolated “notes” corresponding to particle species. The P4 inequality
leaves a continuous range of allowable tunings, while the Hopfion is one conditional example. The
idea that isolated stable basins become a discrete particle table remains the hypothesis still to be
tested.

So the development program should survey and prune the whole catalogue, not optimize one Hopfion
and call it matter. The Hopfion remains one laboratory. P4 cells are another. Bootstrap may later be
the rule that makes their surviving collection globally self-consistent, but that selection rule is
still missing.
