# Lay report

We checked whether the cosmic distance profile used in the supernova work could also be used as the
inside-to-outside shape of a complete spherical resonator.

It cannot be carried over unchanged. Near the center, that profile has a tiny linear corner. A
one-dimensional or observer-to-observer calculation can tolerate that role, but when the angular
sphere collapses to a point, the same corner becomes infinite curvature. Turning on the angular
mixing does not cancel it. The repaired mixing profile behaves properly at leading order; the old
one did not—but the separate curvature corner remains.

The far end behaves differently. With mixing on, it is like a finite end of an instrument whose
end condition still has to be chosen. Dirichlet and Neumann are two possible test fittings, not a
law of nature. With mixing off, the end recedes to infinite mathematical distance and no such wall
fitting is freely available.

So we learned exactly why the next spectrum calculation would have been premature. The angular
wiring diagram remains good, and the supernova relation remains good in its declared role. What is
missing is a complete radial profile that is actually smooth at the center and whose relationship
to the observer-pair profile is derived rather than assumed.
