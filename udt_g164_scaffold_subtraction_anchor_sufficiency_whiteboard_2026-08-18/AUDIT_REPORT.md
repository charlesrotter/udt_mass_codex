## Reconstruction is not selection

Global co-presence and network consistency are load-bearing. They reduce redundant pair
descriptions to one coherent metric field and reduce matched reciprocal edges to an endpoint
potential. A fully valued rank-complete network may reconstruct the complete metric.

But a reconstruction theorem says:

```text
these many pair values are one metric.
```

It does not say:

```text
these are the values Nature must realize.
```

Passive all-observer recentering is covariance, not an active transitive isometry premise. Adding
active homogeneity could reduce functional freedom, but that would be a new nonidentity condition.

## Does UDT need an equation?

Not necessarily. “Missing evolution equation” is too narrow. Any metric-native nonidentity
restriction could close relative scale: a differential equation, a global integrability or
completion theorem, a flat physical scale carry, an active symmetry classification, or a complete
function-valued relational valuation.

The anchor-only architecture becomes sufficient only after such a restriction reduces the metric
family modulo gauge to finite moduli. If only one constant per connected component remains, one
lawfully identified dimensional datum can calibrate it. Before that reduction, finite anchors
cannot determine arbitrary functions.

`c_E` and `G_obs` do not themselves form a length. Their unit constraints retain one simultaneous
length/time/mass rescaling. With an independently owned mass, density, energy density, interval, or
area bridge, one can form a dimensional calibration such as `G M/c_E^2`; that does not by itself
create a metric law.

## Two legitimate theory architectures

1. **Relational reconstruction theory.** The complete globally valued co-presence network is the
   physical state. No separate history selector is missing; the metric is reconstructed from the
   relational values. The theory must still say how those values are obtained or observed.
2. **Finite-data predictive theory.** A finite set of anchors and initial data is expected to
   generate the whole network. Then a nonidentity restriction must first collapse the present
   functional freedom to finite moduli.

The current evidence does not choose between these architectures.

## Maximum conclusion

anchors do not yet do that, but they may calibrate the residue if the next gate finds only finite
moduli.
