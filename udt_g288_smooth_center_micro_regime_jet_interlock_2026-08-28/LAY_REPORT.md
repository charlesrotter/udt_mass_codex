# G288 lay report

We started over from the current UDT metric rather than trusting old audits.

Near a perfectly smooth center, the metric cannot vary arbitrarily.  Its first allowed change has
one very simple shape.  That first change affects clock rate, acceleration, and curvature together,
but it cancels out of the two angular/tidal channels.  Angular distortion only appears in the next
layer of the profile.

So the solution space has given us a useful native pattern:

- the exact center returns to balanced \(\phi=0\);
- just away from it, a negative-\(\phi\) region is possible;
- the leading region is highly symmetric and angularly quiet;
- the first angular departure has a fixed internal ratio, but an unfixed size;
- no Planck length or physical mass has been inserted or derived.

In plain terms, going to a smaller radius does not automatically turn every UDT effect up.  A
smooth center actually forces some effects to fade more quickly than others.  If a loud micro regime
exists, it is more likely to be a finite band away from the exact center, whose location and strength
still require the global metric history or an observational attachment.
