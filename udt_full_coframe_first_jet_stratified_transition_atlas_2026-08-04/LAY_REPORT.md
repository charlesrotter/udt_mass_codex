# Lay report

We stopped holding the metric's moving parts still. At one point, every possible first change of
the four-dimensional metric is available: time changes, spatial changes, reciprocal changes,
angular stretching and shearing, and the ways the two sectors lean into each other. Nothing in the
coframe bookkeeping removes one of them.

The most interesting positive result is the orchestra effect. Whether the depth field `phi` looks
time-directed, lightlike, or space-directed is not controlled by `phi` alone. Hold its coordinate
slope fixed and change only the angular shear or the angular/reciprocal mixing: the causal class can
move through all three possibilities. The complete metric really does make the reciprocal and
angular sectors one joint structure.

We also found two different kinds of transition:

- At a lightlike `dphi`, the complete metric may remain perfectly healthy. The raw depth direction
  survives, but the normalized projector used to split reciprocal from angular directions blows up.
  The split fails, not necessarily the geometry.
- At actual coframe rank loss, the metric itself becomes degenerate. Its inverse and ordinary
  Levi-Civita connection no longer exist. Some polynomial remnants remain, but choosing them as the
  next theory would be an additional premise.

The symmetry of the geometry changes sharply across these strata: rotation-like for timelike
`dphi`, Lorentz-like for spacelike `dphi`, a contracted `ISO(2)` form at nonzero null, and the full
Lorentz algebra when `dphi` vanishes.

What we did not find is an instruction telling the geometry which transition to follow. First jets
describe every possible initial lean of the orchestra; they do not provide the score. A genuine
time-live solve still needs the native law that relates changes at neighboring events.
