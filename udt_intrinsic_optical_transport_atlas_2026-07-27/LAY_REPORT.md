# Lay report

We followed actual lightlike paths through the complete test metric instead of merely inspecting
the local geometry.

The result is consistent across all 36 tests: as a path moves, its transported viewing screen does
not stay perfectly aligned with the new local clock-and-ruler screen it encounters.  The metric
continuously mixes them.  At the same time, the expected clock dilation remains exact and the
light-image transport behaves cleanly.

So three things that previously looked like separate clues are now demonstrably parts of one
geometric process:

- clock dilation;
- angular/Hopf-direction twisting; and
- the focusing or spreading of an observed image.

That is encouraging.  It means we are no longer inventing a connector between those pieces—the
metric connection already is the connector.

It did not yet reproduce or select the supernova law.  Different paths prefer different ends of
the tested angular-weight range, and none matches exactly.  Because these test metrics are not
on-shell universe solutions, that is neither a failure of UDT nor evidence for choosing a
parameter.

The next clean question is global: after carrying this clock/ruler/screen structure around complete
loops in the finite cell, what returns unchanged, what rotates, and what fails to close?  That can
tell us whether the complete metric itself restricts the allowed angular response or topology.
