# G132 lay report

The “missing scale” was partly a bookkeeping illusion.

UDT's founding clock-ruler operation is like a balanced gearbox: one side shrinks exactly as the
other grows. The transformation itself has no extra “make both gears bigger” knob. That does not
tell us the physical size of the whole machine; it only tells us that size is not another setting
inside the reciprocal gear operation.

Later, we listened only to the **ratio** between the two gears. Ratios cannot tell whether the
whole gearbox is one inch or one mile across. That is what G131 detected. But the complete pair
metric also reports `kappa_pair`, the overall size of that particular gearbox. Keeping
`kappa_pair`, `phi_pair`, and the shift together reconstructs the complete pair measurement.

So G131 did not prove that UDT is scale-free. We temporarily threw away the scale channel by asking
only for `phi_pair`. Keeping `kappa_pair` preserves the scale already present in the supplied pair
metric, although it does not explain where the universe's metric history gets that value.

There is still a real unfinished problem: the metric must supply its complete history and physical
network of observer comparisons. In the spherical case, an independently calibrated physical area
of a sphere gives a natural ruler. Merely naming the metric's own areal coordinate does not select
its size. In the general nonspherical case, the complete metric contains area and volume channels,
but the current kinematics does not yet determine their physical values through the whole universe.

`c_E` and `G_obs` are useful observational anchors. Together they still need a measured mass,
density, or energy density to make a length. That kind of anchor can tune a derived finite family;
it cannot by itself invent the missing history equation or determine an arbitrary function at
every point.
