# Lay readout

Your cross-check was right: we had been staring too hard at one branch.

The metric work has produced two different geometric ways of carrying something that the current
bookkeeping calls mass. One appears when the cell is varied as a whole (`F01`). The other appears
when its angular ingredients are allowed to vary from place to place (`F02`). They are related
mathematically, but they are not known to be the same physical object.

The Hopfion is a third kind of evidence. It is a genuinely three-dimensional, finite-energy stable
shape inside its stated box, but we still supply its carrier and energy rule. So it demonstrates
that the proposed ingredients can support a stable object; it does not yet show that the bare UDT
metric creates that object.

The important surprise is that “more mechanisms on” does not always mean “closer to success.” Some
extra freedoms destroy a branch; some boundary choices only move an instability; angular stiffness
can support another branch; and global cell arrangement can allow or prohibit nonzero mass. The
correct picture is therefore a switchboard, not one ladder.

What remains missing is one complete, naturally selected setup in which the metric tells us which
switches are real and how they belong together. Until then the strongest honest statement is:

> UDT has multiple exact conditional routes to mass-bearing geometry and one conditional stable
> energy-bearing Hopf model, but it has not yet selected one as native stable matter.

One reasonable next test is to see whether all already-derived F02 conditions can coexist in one
stationary member. That checks an overlooked branch without inventing anything new. It is not yet
ranked above the open F01, F04, or F07 gates.
