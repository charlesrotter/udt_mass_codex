# G169 preregistration — bidirectional co-present metric distance

Date: 2026-08-18

## Whole question

Does the proposed statement

> physical distance under co-presence is the complete reciprocal metric response of one observer
> pair in both directions

have a precise mathematical home that is consistent with the founded reciprocal kernel, the G167
complete primary-metric pullback, the G168 supplied-germ plane theorem, reversal, and lawful
three-observer composition?

The test must distinguish:

1. an oriented reciprocal relation from its unoriented distance class;
2. local orientation reversal from full observer reversal;
3. matched one-dimensional reciprocal composition from an arbitrary non-collinear observer
   triangle;
4. a metric evaluation of supplied endpoint germs from a derivation that the physical relation owns
   those germs or their cross-endpoint carry.

- regular timelike clock germs and nonzero separation germs only;

  global completion.

## Candidate object

Let an oriented complete pair response be provisionally typed as

```text
R_AB = (A-side calibrated germ and pullback,
        B-side calibrated germ and pullback,
        reciprocal endpoint identification/carry when supplied).
```

Let reversal `tau` exchange the two endpoints and invert every genuinely arrow-valued channel. The
candidate unoriented relational distance is the reversal orbit

```text
Dist(A,B) = [R_AB] under {identity,tau}.
```

This is not assumed to be a real-valued metric-space distance. A scalar magnitude may descend only
after positivity, separation, symmetry, and triangle properties are independently checked.

## Exact tests

1. `tau^2=id` and the reversal orbit is symmetric by construction.
2. On the founded reciprocal block, prove
   `delta_BA=-delta_AB`, `q_BA=1/q_AB`, and `chi_BA=-chi_AB` when the reverse is the inverse of the
   same supplied reciprocal relation.
3. Determine which scalar magnitudes descend to the orbit and whether they are metrics,
   pseudometrics, or merely readouts.
4. Use G168's exact flat surface family to test whether ordinary endpoint/surface reversal alone
   forces reciprocal scalar inversion or a unique reverse germ.
5. For three matched reciprocal relations prove the G166 additive/Mobius laws and inverse laws.
6. Test whether arbitrary three-observer pair metrics must satisfy those scalar laws. Do not impose
   additivity on a non-collinear triangle merely because it would be convenient.
7. For supplied full carries verify that inverse and composition are exact, while scalar closure is
   weaker than complete matrix closure, as bounded by G156/G160.
8. Type-check whether the current metric derives the physical two-ended relation or only evaluates
   it after its endpoint germs/carry are supplied.

## Preregistered falsifiers

The strongest full-derivation claim fails if any one occurs:

- full observer reversal cannot be constructed from the metric and one endpoint germ;
- two regular endpoint germs for the same boundary observers give terminal depths that are not
  negatives;
- scalar reciprocal closure holds while the complete relation/carry triangle fails;
- an arbitrary non-collinear observer triangle requires additional middle-observer calibration;
- the reversal orbit is well-defined only after adding a two-ended ownership statement not present
  in the founding record.

The candidate definition itself fails if reversal is not an involution on correctly typed supplied
relations, or if its orbit depends on which orientation is chosen after all declared endpoint gauges
are quotiented.
