# G214 lay report

The completed observer-pair result survives changes of local measuring chart without losing any
information.

Think of the completed pair as carrying two coordinated records:

1. a normalized shape of the clock-ruler comparison; and
2. a density recording how the local ruler markings scale.

When two charts overlap, the density absorbs the overall change of scale while the normalized
shape undergoes a reciprocal, determinant-one change. On a third overlapping chart, those changes
compose in exactly the right order. Putting the two records back together recovers the ordinary
metric in every chart.

So the local chords really can be stitched into one supplied score without losing the quiet scale
channel.

There is one important boundary. The `AB` and `BC` comparisons are generally different pair
surfaces, not two charts on the same surface. They do not automatically multiply into `AC`.
Composition works when the `B` calibration used by both comparisons is explicitly the same, and
when the endpoint incidence data match. The local metric algebra does not by itself choose or
identify those different pair germs.

What closed: local chart and overlap consistency of the completed kernel.

What remains open: which pair germs form the physical network and what supplies their actual
values and cross-pair incidence matches.
