The basic reciprocal stretch is naturally quiet when the two observers have nearly zero reciprocal
depth and grows toward either extreme.  That is the underlying rhythm.

The angular instruments do not have fixed volume knobs attached to that rhythm.  Their volume also
depends on how the metric is changing and bending with distance.  They can get louder, quieter,

So “loud, quiet, loud” can describe the overall reciprocal regime without requiring every
instrument to play the same pattern.  The metric naturally permits different ratios of instruments
in different regimes.  What remains is to determine which complete radial history the UDT
foundations and observational anchors support—not to bolt on separate volume controls afterward.
