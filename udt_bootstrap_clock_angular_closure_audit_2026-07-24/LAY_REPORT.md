# Lay report

Yes: some of our trouble may come from studying geometry before the
matter-filled universe has closed on itself.

Imagine looking at how two nearby light rays spread sideways. There are two
sideways directions. In the matter-free examples we tested, those directions
were equivalent, like every compass direction on a perfectly round tabletop.
The geometry had no reason to choose one.

A self-consistent matter-filled universe could make the sideways curvature
unequal. If one of those two curvatures then exactly matches the clock
dilation, the metric automatically points to the correct direction. We found
the exact formula for that direction. It does not need to be added by hand.

That is real progress: two of the earlier missing requirements collapse into
one intrinsic curvature test.

But the density number itself does not cause this. To affect local geometry,
UDT needs a rule saying how the complete matter content changes when the
metric changes. We do not yet have that native matter-response rule.

There are also two remaining transport questions:

- Does the selected direction stay fixed as it is carried through the
  universe?
- If the clock-dilation rate changes along the path, what connection term
  keeps the local clock/curvature matches joined together?

So the matter-free work was not wasted or false. It found the exact lock.
Bootstrap matter may supply the key, but UDT has not yet derived the key.

The safe hiatus checkpoint is:

> A complete matter–geometry bootstrap can plausibly close the seam, and we
> now know the exact invariant conditions it must satisfy. No existing UDT
> source yet supplies the required native response.
