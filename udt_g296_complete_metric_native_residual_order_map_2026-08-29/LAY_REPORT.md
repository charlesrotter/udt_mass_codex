# G296 lay report

The missing rule no longer needs a larger geometric machine. The complete metric already contains
the clock, radial, angular, mixing, and screen information. A rich enough observer network is
another way to record that same metric; it is not automatically an extra ingredient.

The first place a local rule can genuinely constrain the metric is its curvature. The metric's
value and first derivatives can always be made locally featureless by choosing a suitable frame;
curvature is where invariant change first appears.

But “use curvature” still does not select an equation. If we additionally require the familiar
package—second order, symmetric two-index equation, and automatic conservation—the result reduces
to the Einstein vacuum equation. Those additional requirements are sensible candidates, but UDT
has not yet derived all of them.

The screen-wave test explains why this matters. Two universes can have zero scalar curvature and
even satisfy the vacuum Einstein equation while carrying different gravitational-wave tides. A
law does not have to reject one: different lawful initial or boundary data may produce both.

So the gap has narrowed to a real choice of principle, not a missing algebraic manipulation. We
must decide whether UDT adopts the conservative no-new-state second-order lane, or which specific
assumption it relaxes to obtain genuinely different metric dynamics at the extremes.
