## Independent physical expectations

1. **An actual observable comes first.** R6 gives the proper arrival derivative
   for an actual regular null correspondence, `Z=d tau_o/d tau_e=omega_e/omega_o`.
   Slowing means `Z>1`, and redshift `z=Z-1` needs no extra response function.
   Clocks, endpoint events, preparation, first versus return leg, and weighting
   must be specified. The kernel then evaluates supplied comparison data; this
   is not a rule selecting those data or geometry.

2. **Mutual physical slowing is not inversion.** Reversal of one calibrated
   comparison inverts its multiplier. FAC1's two first emissions and an immediate
   future echo use different event correspondences. A physical law for both
   first signals cannot be proved by inverting a single map or by checking only
   their product/combined average. A combined positive statistic can conceal a
   blue first leg, as FAC1 already demonstrates in a recovery control.

3. **Rotational fairness can motivate a measure, not a sign.** A uniform measure
   on one specified preparation sphere is natural if one explicitly requires a
   normalized rotation-invariant linear average. Logarithms are useful for
   multiplicative ratios and matched depth. Neither choice follows as a unique
   physical observable merely from one geometry or no preferred observer. One
   must distinguish coordinate covariance, rotation invariance within a fixed
   laboratory, and agreement between genuinely different boosted laboratories.
   The latter prepare different clocks and need not record equal numbers.
   Universality means the same governing law, not frame-independent values.

4. **The sphere and statistical functional are load-bearing.** FAC1 integrates
   `log p` over original preparation directions. Received-sky angles, an emission
   population and number-/flux-weighted detections are different measures.
   Any change of angular variables must carry its Jacobian; aberration alone
   does not preserve uniformity. Arithmetic, geometric and reciprocal-rate
   averages can have different signs even on the same records. A normalization
   or Jensen inequality can manufacture a nonnegative statistic without removing
   any possible geometry. A useful selection proposal must show a metric/query
   it actually excludes, with the same fixed definitions.

5. **Free preparation does not isolate a cause.** Parallel initial velocity and
   free fall remove imposed initial velocity mismatch and proper acceleration.
   Ordinary gravitational tides still change relative motion, rays and reception.
   Total `M_+` and `M_-` are therefore physical net clock statistics, not an
   identified additional positional part. The owner treats SR, gravitational
   and positional effects as one geometry without specifying a sum/product
   decomposition. A matched-reference contrast may be useful, but its matching
   and attribution are further hypotheses; a reference is not proved necessary
   for every conceivable native relation.

6. **Founding meaning survives a failed candidate.** The positional meaning of
   observed `c_E`, ordinary proper clocks, actual received ticks, one geometry
   and an additional positional contribution are retained owner commitments.
   The sources do not turn tentative total-redshift wording into an every-query
   total-sign law. Nor do they establish that all clarified commitments are
   insufficient to imply a physical relation. A control satisfying only the
   inspected kinematic assumptions is not a full-UDT countermodel.

## Sign and regime requirements

FAC1 already gives a useful, nonautomatic conditional restriction. For fixed
event and finite laboratory U in a Ricci-flat *neighborhood*, the prepared means
have coefficient `(4 E^2-3 B^2)/360` at order L^4. Requiring both means nonnegative
at every sufficiently short L gives `4 E^2 >= 3 B^2`; strict inequality suffices
locally, equality remains open. Pointwise vacuum is insufficient. Outside
regional vacuum the leading term is `-Ric(U,U)L^2/6`; replacing it with the
vacuum quartic theorem silently changes the premise. This source result does
not yet supply all-observer classification, a uniform radius over unbounded
boosts, or a distant-distance law.

Any proposed distance regime should state: which distance; which worldlines
and family remain fixed; whether the law concerns all small L, a specified
finite interval or a distant limit; how null-branch existence is maintained;
and whether thresholds may depend on observer, geometry or a recovery parameter.
The tentative beyond-solar-distance expectation does not select a threshold,
unitless profile, distance attachment or uniform asymptotic domain. `c_E` is a
calibration anchor. `X_max` is WORKING/OPEN global completion and cannot supply
a local cutoff or selected onset scale.

The fixed-query recovery obstruction is valid only when the same finite regular
query belongs to both the sign domain and the required recovery domain. FAC1's
shrinking positive intervals defeat a stronger inference from smooth metric
recovery alone. A local negative Kerr control is neither a distant-effect
refutation nor automatically an empirical GR recovery target. Conversely,
renaming an excluded fixed finite query a distant regime does not cure an
already declared overlap. Approximate empirical recovery needs an actual
tolerance and observable domain, not an exact all-strong-field assumption.

## Promising complementary tests and return criteria

The most economical new test would compare several explicitly defined scalar
means on the *same* actual records and classify which sign restrictions are
automatic consequences of probability normalization and which are additional
physical conditions. A flat moving-clock control can separate average-induced
slowing from curvature, provided it is not mislabeled as FAC1's parallel-free
preparation (that flat preparation has unit ratios). Carrying emitted and
received angular measures through the same Lorentz ray map is a particularly
sharp test of a proposed observer-neutral averaging argument. A small-curvature
expansion can then show exactly which proposed scalar adds a variance term to
FAC1 and whether its sign law still restricts geometry.

A distinct useful route is to test an actual finite protocol or operationally
matched contrast against the owner’s distant-onset meaning. The discriminator
should establish what the proposed matching holds fixed and whether physical
clock effects can be separated from a reference choice; PEA/COC already rule
out several universal scalar/reference shortcuts, but not all restricted uses.
Repeating their absence result without a new protocol is not progress.

A third possible bounded target is an all-observer implication of a genuinely
nonautomatic angular law, keeping the extracted local limit prior to each finite
boost and avoiding a fictitious uniform experimental radius. This could sharpen
FAC1's conditional tool but would not justify adopting AMS by itself.

A return is justified by a reviewed substantive implication, a precise rejected
identification plus a useful surviving discriminator, or a genuine budget/scope
boundary. No present source establishes exhaustive failure of every in-scope
route. Conversely the work order does not require filling the budget with
redundant examples: a concrete result that changes which physical question must
be answered next can be decision-ready. Final review must explain that decision,
not simply report that a physical join is still open.
