# ALP1 — what the angular-clock investigation learned

We have a sharper test of proposed clock laws, but have not derived the physical
angular-slowing law from UDT's existing commitments.

Two tempting justifications fail in precise ways. First, a positive sky average
can arise from how directions are counted. Changing from a uniform emission sky
to a uniform reception sky reverses the mean's sign even for one ordinary Lorentz
comparison. The exact normalization behind that positive sky mean does not carry
over automatically to FAC1's prepared clocks: if imposed throughout the
sufficiently-small-separation regime there, it excludes nonzero Weyl curvature
in a vacuum neighborhood, including examples where
FAC1's weaker slowing condition already holds.

Second, distant slowing does not fix nearby behavior in the supplied examples.
We constructed actual freely prepared clock comparisons that share the same
limiting signal separation and distant divergence power, yet have opposite
nearby signs. These are mathematical controls, not native UDT histories or
empirically established GR limits. Their comparison boundary is not X_max.
They reject that particular inference, not the additional positional effect.

A useful tool survives: under FAC1's regional-vacuum and preparation hypotheses,
two ideal short-distance clock statistics determine the electric and magnetic
Weyl curvature norms for that observer. These are parts of metric curvature,
not new fields. The result does not identify the additional positional effect,
detect vacuum from data or provide a finite-precision measurement protocol.

Both fresh reviewers independently checked complementary arguments and numeric
controls, and challenged whether the campaign should continue. They found no
substantive defect in the frozen candidate and support this bounded return.
They share the inherited model; there is no human or different-model review.
Original drafts and the pre-crash correction remain preserved. Final integration,
repository checks and publication are separate gates recorded by actual receipts.

The next physical question is now more precise: why should specified actual
clock and angle records, or a specified finite-distance comparison, obey a
particular relation? Normalizing a statistic or changing its weights cannot
supply that reason. FAC1 remains useful; its physical sign and regime remain
unadopted. No new postulate is proved necessary and no all-UDT no-go is claimed.

This campaign returns for lay discussion after its authorized banking gates.
It does not automatically start another campaign, adopt physics or restart
deferred/protected work. A next proposal should name the physical connection
and a test that could change the decision, using the surviving tools where useful.
