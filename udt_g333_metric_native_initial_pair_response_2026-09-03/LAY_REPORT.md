# G333 lay report

We stopped asking whether the geometry was Hopf-shaped and asked a more basic question: once the
metric has supplied a lawful starting universe, what does it say happens to a tiny separation at
the very next instant?

The answer is not arbitrary. The metric gives every direction a common expansion or contraction,
plus a directional imbalance. The same local curvature number that enters the spacetime constraint
also fixes that imbalance. A separation aligned with the distinguished local direction generally
changes at a different rate from one crossing it. Directions in between interpolate exactly.

This works for the whole G332 family, including irregular examples whose distinguished paths do
not close into Hopf circles. No topological winding number, fibre length, mass mechanism,
observational fit, or extra response profile was inserted. So the earlier Hopf work may still
matter later, but it is not needed for this result.

The test also clarifies the reciprocal kernel. In the particular diagnostic pair used here, the
single final clock number does not record the changing spatial separation, while the complete pair
metric does. That supports the project's rule that the completed pair pullback comes before its
terminal reciprocal readout. It does not mean clocks never respond; other observer-pair directions
still have to be tested.

What we have is one exact first-instant response. We do not yet know whether the directional line
persists, whether irregular or Hopf-like data are stable, or which lawful starting data Nature
occupies. The clean next extension is to test more general observer-pair germs and then evolve the
data for nonzero time, still without targeting Hopf structure.
