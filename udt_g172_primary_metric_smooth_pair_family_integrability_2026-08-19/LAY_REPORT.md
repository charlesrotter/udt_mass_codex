# G172 lay report

The local formula from G171 was not a one-position trick. For a broad, explicitly declared class
of smoothly separating observer-pair surfaces, the primary UDT metric makes the pieces assemble
continuously by itself.

The radial part supplies the familiar \(\phi\) response. If the pair direction also moves across
the sky, the spherical part of the same metric contributes an exact positive Gram term before the
reciprocal readout. The result is one chord:

\[
\Phi=\phi+\frac14\log\!\left(1+r^2e^{-2\phi}a^2\right).
\]

Here \(a^2\) is simply how quickly the pair direction changes across the unit sky. It is not a new
coefficient and it was not fitted. If the direction is radial, \(a^2=0\) and the formula returns
exactly to \(\Phi=\phi\).

The family can be followed smoothly for as long as the supplied primary metric and angular curve
remain smooth and the areal radius keeps moving monotonically. Reversing the pair reverses the
signed depth, and adjoining two segments of the same family gives the direct endpoint result.

The first unfinished edge is concrete: at a radial turning point, areal radius no longer labels the
pair direction, so another calibration chart is needed. G172 does not say which angular curve
Nature realizes, and it does not claim a global universe or complete transport law. It says the
metric-native kernel itself is coherent across this whole smooth class, without the discarded
scaffolded kernel.
