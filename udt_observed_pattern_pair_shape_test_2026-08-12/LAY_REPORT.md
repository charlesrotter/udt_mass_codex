# Lay report

We have now done more than prepare the data.

The complete UDT geometry tells us how to compare the sideways size of the observed pattern with its
depth-wise size. In the orchestra metaphor, that comparison listens to three things together:

- how the angular screen magnifies or squeezes the image;
- how the observer-pair ruler is stretched;
- how reciprocal depth changes along the pair relation.

That combined measuring rule is now derived and does not depend on how we label positions along the
comparison.

We then tested two deliberately simple stand-ins. The first used only the basic reciprocal curve. It
missed badly. The second used the already frozen SNe curve. It was dramatically better and was close
in four of the six slices, but it still missed too much in two slices to pass the test.

This is useful. It says the SNe scalar curve captures part of the middle-distance shape, but it is not
the whole score. The angular and mixing instruments cannot simply be ignored for this observed
pattern. We have not fitted those instruments or invented a repair. The next honest test is to take a
complete metric history derived independently, let all three ingredients play together, and see
whether its prediction lands on the measured pattern.

It does not yet determine `X_max`, explain the origin of the pattern, or validate UDT as a whole.
