# G76 lay report

G75 built 591 mathematically allowed smooth versions of the axial background profile. G76 asked the
same simple question of every one: if an observer looks in every direction, where does each viewing
direction arrive on the outer comparison sphere, and does the resulting sky map tear, fold, lose a
direction, or squeeze any patch nearly flat?

The answer is strikingly orderly in this bounded test. In 587 cases the entire sampled sky arrives,
keeps the same orientation, and wraps the outer sky exactly once. Four strongest-profile cases did
not meet the deliberately strict time-step agreement threshold, so they remain honestly unresolved.
Even those four showed no tear, fold, lost ray, or near-flat patch; their unresolved label is a
numerical caution, not an observed geometric failure.

The sky is not unchanged. Different profiles stretch and compress different angular patches by
moderate amounts. In the orchestra metaphor, the metric already acts like a real optical instrument:
it reshapes the whole sky coherently rather than scrambling it. But this calculation has not yet put
an image or CMB source behind that instrument, has not transported polarization, and has not chosen
the physical profile or universe scale.

The gain is that the 591 candidate profile shapes no longer look like 591 unrelated machines under
this query. They occupy one robust, degree-one whole-sky relation family at the sampled resolution.
That sharply cleans the next question: determine what source-independent sky structure follows from
this robust family, then add only the observational anchors or source information whose ownership is
explicitly justified.
