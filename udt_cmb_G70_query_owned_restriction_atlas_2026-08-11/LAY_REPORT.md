# G70 lay report

G69 showed that UDT's geometric “telescope” has several real adjustment knobs, but an unknown source
picture can compensate for the telescope's distortion. G70 asked what extra knowledge would make
the knobs distinguishable.

The clearest tested answer is demanding: if we already know the source pattern—including its overall
strength—and can independently observe the angular carry, then all three geometric knobs are
distinguishable throughout the frozen test map. If one knob is independently fixed, less source
information can often distinguish the remaining two.

But UDT does not currently give us those missing facts. It does not yet determine the physical CMB
source pattern, its normalization, the last-scattering endpoint, the physical profile, or a direct
scalar-temperature measurement of angular carry. Giving the calculation those facts makes the
inverse problem work; it does not derive the facts.

Some weaker combinations sit extremely close to losing or gaining a mathematical direction, so the
strict overall result remains numerically unresolved rather than declared solved.

In the telescope metaphor: we now know which labels would have to be printed on the lens, the film,
and the focusing ring to reverse-engineer the settings. The instrument itself has not yet printed
those labels for us.
