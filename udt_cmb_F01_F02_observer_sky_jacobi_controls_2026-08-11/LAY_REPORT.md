# Lay report — the first native observer-sky control

We finally built the geometric equivalent of the first piece of the telescope rather than fitting
a conversion factor afterward.

Imagine launching a tiny round bundle of neighboring viewing directions from one observer. In the
round F01 control, that little bundle stays round and grows linearly along the registered radial
comparison. The unknown radial clock profile `A(r)` does not bend or squeeze it on this special
query.

In the mixing-on F02 control, the bundle generally stops behaving like a featureless circle. One
screen direction still grows normally, while the direction aligned with the angular mixing is
slightly squeezed or stretched. The first correction is quadratic in weak mixing. Its sign and
size depend on the local shape of `A(r)` and `h(r)`, so the geometry gives us the exact instrument
but not yet the universe's setting of that instrument.

This matters because the old CMB exercise used a fitted scale and offset to place a mathematical
comb on the sky. The new result says the sky scale belongs in this Jacobi/telescope map, while the
comb offset belongs to boundary and mode physics. We have located the jobs correctly and exposed a
directional effect a one-number affine fit cannot contain.

We have not predicted the CMB. We still need a physical remote comparison surface, a complete
metric profile, and a source/population rule saying which patterns actually have power. The next
honest step is to have this exact local result adversarially reviewed, then decide whether a bounded
finite-path F01/F02 integration can be posed without inventing the missing global profile.
