# Lay report

Imagine that A, B, and C each have a calibrated measuring tape connecting them to the others.
Earlier we treated “does the tape pass through B correctly?” as one missing law. It was actually
three questions.

First, if the tape arriving at B is the very same calibrated tape that leaves B, joining the two
pieces is automatic and associative. No new law of physics is needed for that bookkeeping.

Second, if B throws away the incoming tape and builds a new one, we need an explicit conversion
between the old B tape and the new B tape. Einsteinian `c_E` calibrates both, but does not prove they
are the same tape.

Third, going directly from A to C need not give the same result as going A to B to C. If all three
are merely overlapping charts of one common geometric surface, they must agree. If they are
different paths through curved or branch-filled geometry, their difference can be real holonomy—
the geometric equivalent of returning with a rotated compass.

So we have not derived the universe's one physical comparison network. We have derived how any
properly supplied network must be joined and exactly how to measure when its paths fail to descend
to one path-independent tape.
