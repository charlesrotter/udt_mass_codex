# G334 lay report

G333 found one real, metric-owned initial change in spatial separation. G334 asks what two locally
boosted observers would see in the pair metric at that same instant.

For the directly inherited pair coordinates, the answer is coherent. A boost does not create a new
effect. It moves the same effect among three entries: the clock entry, the clock--ruler cross entry,
and the ruler entry. One exact combination of those entries gives the original G333 response for
every finite boost.

The one-number terminal readout is less complete. With no boost it sees none of this first spatial
change. With a nonzero known boost it can recover the response, but it becomes a poor measurement
as the boost approaches zero. The full pair matrix does not have that blind spot in the declared
inherited coordinates.

There is an important limit. Saying how much the pair is boosted at one point is not enough to say
how its measuring axes are carried to the next point. If those axes are continuously reset to stay
orthonormal, their raw metric entries can remain unchanged even though the geometry is changing;
the change is then recorded in the resetting of the axes. So an arbitrary pair first jet requires
both the local boost and its transport rule.

This is useful progress, not a new law. It shows exactly how the existing metric response behaves
under finite local boosts and where the supplied-germ boundary lies. It does not yet give what a
boosted observer measures over time, because that also needs spatial derivatives and a nonzero-time
spacetime development. The metric, kernel, angular sector, and topology claims are unchanged.
