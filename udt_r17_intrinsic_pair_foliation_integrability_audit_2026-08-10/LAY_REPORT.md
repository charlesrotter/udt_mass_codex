# Lay report

The metric has now done something concrete and elegant.

Imagine spacetime as a bundle of infinitely many long spiral ribbons. Each ribbon joins UDT's
clock direction to its reciprocal ruler direction. The metric itself lays these ribbons through
the whole supplied R17 universe; we did not draw them in by hand. Every point lies on exactly one
such ribbon, but the metric does not choose one ribbon as special.

The angular sector is not another set of flat ribbons. It twists between them like the horizontal
structure of a woven rope. That twist is why angular information cannot simply be flattened into
the clock/ruler sheet or ignored.

Most importantly, when we measure the full little two-dimensional geometry of any reciprocal
ribbon, its clock contraction and ruler expansion balance exactly. The old reciprocal condition
`clock scale x ruler scale = 1` falls out of the complete metric on these leaves. The same geometry
then reads out `phi` directly, and comparing two endpoints gives the already banked R17 depth
`phi(B)-phi(A)`.

What remains missing is the rule for carrying the entire four-dimensional angular orchestra from
one observer to another. Different routes or windings can rotate/mix the angular part differently.
So we have found the metric's reciprocal backbone and its endpoint dial, but not yet the unique
complete observer-to-observer transport law.
