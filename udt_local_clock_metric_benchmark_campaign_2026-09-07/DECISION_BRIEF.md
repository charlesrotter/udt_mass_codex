# Local clock benchmark — decision brief

The published clock measurement is consistent with the independently calibrated
geometric prediction under both frozen uncertainty treatments. It supplies a
conditional constraint on one local metric gradient—not a unique UDT prediction
or an independent repetition of the experiment. LC2's final review controls
the review status of this brief; no scientific promotion is implied.

Using the source's downward ordering, in units of10^-19 per centimetre:

| Quantity | Result |
|---|---:|
| Published corrected clock gradient | -12.4 ±2.6 |
| Independently calibrated geometric prediction | approximately -10.9 |
| Difference | approximately -1.5 |
| Quadrature comparison standard uncertainty |2.60|
| Upper standard uncertainty allowing any measurement/reference correlation |2.70|

The ± notation denotes standard uncertainty, not a hard bound or automatically
a68%/95% confidence interval. Both displayed difference bands include zero.
The source supplies its measured gradient, total error and independent gravity/
height calibration; conventional atomic, laser, gravimeter and correction
models remain explicit assumptions. [Zheng2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-40629-8)

UDT's working physical-metric premise, with the supplied stationary laboratory
observers, gives a=c_E²d ln N. In the source's effective-linear-gradient regime,
the clock datum therefore corresponds to an upward logarithmic lapse gradient
**(1.24 ±0.26) ×10^-16 m^-1**. Equivalently it gives11.14 ±2.34m/s² of supported
acceleration. That is the same clock measurement in different units, not a second
gravimeter result. No calibration was retuned to make it agree with9.803m/s².

The useful limitation is now quantitative: allowing unknown cross-correlation
between the two reported summaries changes the uncertainty little, but this
does not protect against arbitrary incorrect or omitted instrument bias.
Some predeclared hypothetical correction displacements remove the one-standard-
uncertainty overlap; they are sensitivity scenarios, not evidence of a real
correction error. A central shift of about+1.5 units would erase the mismatch,
but was not fitted or applied. An unrestricted gradient bias makes the geometric
inference unidentifiable.

The constraint is local and conditional on stationarity, near-linearity,
finite-cloud averaging and adequate source uncertainty models. Spatial geometry,
unsampled functions, curvature, initial data and history remain undetermined.
Ratios alone do not fix scale; dimensional anchors can fix it in an identified
family. Neither every legitimate input nor a complete Earth metric must be
uniquely selected to make this small comparison meaningful.

We did not count two analyses of the same experiment as two tests, infer height
from the clock result to predict itself, fit raw data, or adopt an instrument
model as native UDT physics. Published outcome exposure is known; there is no
new blind confirmation. Agreement is shared geometric consistency, including GR.
Neither physical-content identification nor emergent light was a prerequisite.

Recommendation: retain this as a limited practical metric-to-measurement
benchmark. More repetitions of this same comparison would add little. The next
direction decision is whether to seek an observation that constrains a genuinely
different geometric freedom, or return to geometric/emergence research; those
remain parallel. No additional physical premise is demanded by this result,
and no follow-on campaign is launched here.
