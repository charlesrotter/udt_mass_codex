# Lay readout

We took inventory of every plausible “dial” the universe might use to tune
itself: total volume, curvature, angular shape, holonomy, limiting separation,
topology, mass, energy, density, and the existing particle quantities.

The encouraging part is that the metric already gives us many real pieces. It
defines local curvature, the full nonlinear interaction wiring, conditional
volumes and areas, angular shortest-path structures, and global topology
labels. We also calculated exactly how several of those pieces change when the
geometry is nudged. In the orchestra metaphor, we now know many instruments,
their ranges, and which notes can respond to which deformations.

What the metric has not yet given us is the score that says which readings are
the universe's physical tuning variables and how the whole universe feeds
those readings back into the local geometry that permits matter. “Everything
must be self-consistent” tells us the orchestra must tune together, but it does
not yet tell us what note each instrument must match or how to adjust it.

This also explains why putting a guessed cosmic density into a solver now
would be premature. We can write the familiar ratio mass divided by volume,
but UDT has not yet supplied its own total-mass function or the law telling the
metric how to respond to that density. A conventional cosmological estimate
can later be used as a comparison center, but it cannot safely substitute for
the missing UDT rule.

So this was useful forward progress: the obstacle is no longer vaguely “the
action” or “scale.” It is the missing global-to-local closure joint. The exact
geometric instruments are present; the native rule that combines and tunes
them is not yet derived from the registered premises.
