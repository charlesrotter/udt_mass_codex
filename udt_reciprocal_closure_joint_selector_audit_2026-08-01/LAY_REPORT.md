# Lay report — the missing joint now has a precise shape

The useful discovery is that the old particle model's two energy terms may be two views of the same
piece of geometry, rather than two unrelated ingredients.

Imagine the metric creates an unoriented local axis—like a compass needle with no marked north end.
Call that axis `P`.

- As you move along one path, the axis may bend. The cost of that bending is exactly the old `L2`
  term.
- Now move around a tiny square. Bending first in one direction and then another may fail to agree
  with doing it in the opposite order. The size of that loop mismatch is exactly the old `L4`
  area/twist term.

So `L2` and `L4` can be read as “path bending” and “loop mismatch” of one geometric object. That is
a real algebraic result, not a metaphor pasted onto the equations.

It also explains why one-dimensional changes are special. If the axis only changes back and forth
along one groove, there is bending but no enclosed loop area. Two genuinely independent directions
are needed for the twist term to appear.

The missing table leg is now clearer: **what makes the complete UDT metric choose that axis?** The
metric contains several possible local directions and planes, but the current foundations do not
choose one everywhere. Ordinary spacetime curvature cannot do the job by itself because every
curved geometry has some loop mismatch, including geometry with no matter carrier at all.

My best current guess is therefore not “add a new `S2` field.” It is:

> some complete metric branches may select their own reciprocal axis or projector, and matter-like
> structure may be the branches where that projector closes nontrivially and persists globally.

That would make the carrier a feature of a solved metric branch rather than an extra object glued
onto the metric. It also allows different branches to support different sectors; there need not be
one universal winning branch.

What we have not earned:

- the metric has not yet selected the projector;
- UDT has not said that both path bending and loop mismatch must carry physical cost;
- their relative strength is still free;
- the boundary, time law, source, mass, and bootstrap return are still missing.

So this is promising because it turns a vague hunch into one exact bridge and one sharply located
gap. The next honest test is to inspect the existing complete metric families and ask whether any of
them selects such a projector naturally, without asking for a particle or forcing Hopf topology.
