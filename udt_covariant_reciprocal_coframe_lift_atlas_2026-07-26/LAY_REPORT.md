# Lay report

This did become simpler.

UDT begins with a two-part rule: a clock side and a ruler side respond in
opposite ways. The problem was how to install that two-part rule inside a
four-dimensional world without quietly choosing a preferred direction.

The metric and constants alone cannot do it. Even adding `hbar` gives another
number, not a direction.

If a clock direction is supplied, there is one especially simple option:
treat every spatial direction as the ruler side. That gives a unique
clock-versus-all-space `1+3` action. It is elegant, but it changes the original
two-line interpretation and fails wherever the supplied direction becomes
null or disappears.

If an observer and one particular separation direction are supplied, the
answer is almost fixed. Only one physical knob remains: how much the two
directions sideways to the observation participate. We called that knob
`lambda`.

- At `lambda=1`, every spatial direction behaves identically. The observer no
  longer needs a chosen target direction.
- At `lambda=0`, only the clock/ruler plane responds and the sideways screen
  watches as a spectator.
- Other values let the screen participate by different amounts.

The most interesting exact result is what happens when one observer compares
different directions. Except at `lambda=1`, those directional operations do
not commute. Their disagreement automatically produces all three ordinary
spatial rotations. Together they form a nine-dimensional local algebra.

That is an orchestra-like result: angular structure appears from the
relations among different reciprocal observations rather than being bolted
on as a new mechanism. But it is still local frame geometry, not yet force,
matter, curvature, or a Hopfion.

The remaining conceptual choice is now very clear. Is UDT built from one
complete clock-versus-space operation, or from a consistent family of
observer-pair comparisons? The original postulates sound more like the
second. Mathematics has now supplied that family, but not yet the rule that
connects separated observers, chooses `lambda`, and proves that every pair is
viewing one coherent global geometry.
