# Lay report

We learned where the bootstrap rule would live, and we found one real piece of it.

Imagine the universe as a structure assembled from interlocking sections. The geometry already
tells us that different choices for assembling the whole structure demand different shapes at the
joints. A twisted join, a smooth seam, and a capped end do not accept the same local pieces. That is
a genuine global-to-local effect already present in the metric geometry.

But cutting a finished structure into pieces and reassembling it only proves that the pieces fit the
structure they came from. It does not explain why that structure, rather than another one, exists.
Adding a list of measurements made from the finished structure does not change this: every finished
structure can report its own measurements.

The bootstrap hypothesis adds the demand that the whole must constrain its local structures and
that the local structures must rebuild that same whole. Mathematically, this is best represented as
a matching relation, not necessarily an optimization formula. The current metric gives us part of
the matching relation at seams and global joins. It does not yet give the interior physical rule
that chooses which complete structures are realized or persist.

A new exact observation makes the limit especially clear: some different global twists accept the
same nonzero local endpoint data. Therefore even perfect knowledge of that local joint does not
always tell us which global structure surrounds it.

So this was progress, but not closure. We now know the missing object more precisely: a
metric-native, observer-independent relation that connects complete global data to the admissible
interior coframe configurations. It need not be a scalar action or a unique branch. Once such a
relation exists, its joint variation could supply the response needed for dynamics and stability.
