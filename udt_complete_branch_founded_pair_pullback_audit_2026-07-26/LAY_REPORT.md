# Lay report

We asked whether the missing UDT clock law was already present in one of the complete universe
shapes we had mapped.

Most of the twelve recorded shapes are still blueprints: they describe how a universe *could* be
closed, but they do not give a finished metric that can be tested. Only the round and squashed
three-sphere examples are complete enough.

The round sphere is too symmetrical to choose a ruler direction. The squashed sphere does choose an
axis—this is a real and interesting example of structure emerging directly from the metric—but it
does not choose its orientation.

When we calculate everything that axis contributes, its twist produces two geometric signals. One
is not integrable around the fiber. The other integrates perfectly, but it is simply ordinary time,
not UDT's distance-related clock dilation. If we remove the extra orientation we had to choose, both
signals disappear.

So the pieces currently on the shelf still do not make the desired joint. We have complete geometry
with no nontrivial clock, and nontrivial local clock dilation with no complete universe geometry.
Combining them by hand would not be a derivation.

The useful advance is that the missing piece is now sharply visible: a complete metric solution in
which the clock and angular sectors are already coupled, so the metric itself chooses the observer
pair and its normalization.
