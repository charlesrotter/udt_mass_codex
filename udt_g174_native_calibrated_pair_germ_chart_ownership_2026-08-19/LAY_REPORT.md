# G174 lay report

G173 looked as though the metric allowed several different numerical rulers at a turn. G174 shows
that this was mostly a bookkeeping issue.

Imagine drawing the same curved line with tick marks. The bare line tells you its direction, but it
does not tell you how far apart the tick marks are. G173 catalogued several lawful ways to place
those marks. They gave different numbers because they were different rulers.

Once an observer-pair experiment supplies the actual calibrated tick marks, the metric gives one
answer. Re-labeling the curve while carrying the same marks along changes nothing. Uniformly
switching from metres to feet shifts the endpoint labels equally and also leaves the measured
difference unchanged. Stretching the tick spacing differently at different locations is a new
ruler, not another description of the old one.

So the kernel itself is not multiply valued. The remaining open question is upstream: what in the
full physical observer relation supplies and carries the ruler calibration? We have not selected
one of G173's candidate rulers, and we have not added a new mechanism.
