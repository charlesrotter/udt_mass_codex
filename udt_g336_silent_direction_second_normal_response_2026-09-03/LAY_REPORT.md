# G336 lay report

G335 found directions whose initial response was exactly zero. G336 asks the next honest question:
if a direction is silent at the first instant, what does the adopted metric equation make it do at
the next instant of change?

The answer is not one universal behavior. Depending on the lawful starting geometry, the same kind
of silent direction can begin opening, begin closing, or remain silent through the second step.
In the signed mathematics these are positive, zero, and negative second responses, with an exact
example of each. So the equation has not secretly forced every quiet direction
to wake up in the same way.

There is also a direction-carry issue. Imagine marking a direction on the initial spatial slice.
If that mark is carried forward by the same convention used in G333, its second response is fixed
exactly by the local curvature and initial bending. If the direction is allowed to rotate while it
is carried, an interior direction's reported second response can change. The strict horizontal
endpoint is special: its result is always positive and does not depend on that first rotation at
this order. What looked like a second, vertical endpoint is actually where the two algebraic
branches meet, so it is a boundary check rather than a member of the strict family.

The complete boosted two-observer metric records this response. The single terminal clock scalar
can still miss it for an unboosted pair, just as in the first-response work.

This boundary distinction was caught internally before external transmission; it did not change
the main formula or the positive/zero/negative result. This is useful base work, but it is not yet a complete evolving universe.
The exact cases that stay
silent through second order require a third-derivative calculation. No topology, matter, mass,
observation, scale, `X_max`, or physical-history choice was introduced. The UDT metric and
reciprocal kernel were not modified.
