# Lay report

We found a real connection, but it is more like **joint design** than **physics selection**.

Imagine building the same collection of local pieces into different closed objects.  One blueprint
might say the piece must come back unchanged; another says it comes back flipped, rotated, or
sheared.  Those global instructions change which pairs of values at the two ends are allowed to be
glued.  Requiring a smoother seam also makes more derivatives agree, shrinking the set of legal
joints.  In one already conditional two-cap construction, choosing which circle closes fixes the
local cap value to `+1` at one end and `-1` at the other.

So the whole-object design really can constrain the local pieces.  That is the sought shape of a
global-to-local relationship.

What it does **not** yet do is choose the whole-object design, supply the interior law, or say which
local configurations are stable matter.  Most of the global blueprints are still mathematically
precise assembly instructions without a complete metric built for them.

Curvature did not close the missing loop.  At present it tells us what a supplied metric is doing.
Turning it into an instruction would require us to choose a target curvature, an averaging rule, or
a globally parallel object.  UDT has not supplied those choices, so we did not invent them.

In short: **the metric's completion data can shape the local admissibility family, but they do not
yet tune or select the physical family.**
