# Lay report

We asked a simple-sounding question: if observer A compares with B, and B
compares with C, must that give exactly the same result as A comparing
directly with C?

There are two importantly different answers.

If every comparison is forced into one shared, perfectly flat measuring
frame, then yes. Under that extra rule, the metric has one especially simple
extension: all three spatial directions stretch together while the clock
changes reciprocally. That is the `lambda=1` option.

But real geometric comparisons need labels telling us whose ruler and clock
frame is being used at each endpoint. Once those labels are handled
correctly, the three-observer chain works for every value of `lambda`. What
matters is having one consistent full frame at B so that the incoming and
outgoing comparisons join.

UDT has not yet derived that joining rule. If B uses one direction frame when
looking toward A and another when looking toward C, a rotation or transport
instruction is left between them. In curved geometry, going around a loop can
legitimately return a rotated frame; that is holonomy, not necessarily a
contradiction.

So we gained a sharp clue but not closure:

- a completely flat, path-independent universe would force the simple
  all-space-together option;
- a curved or transported universe needs a native rule for joining the
  different observer-pair frames; and
- changing the observer, rather than merely changing direction around one
  observer, remains genuinely noncommutative even in the simple option.

The next missing piece is therefore geometric, not another fitted number: a
metric-derived rule saying how complete clock/ruler/angular frames are
identified or transported from one observer pair to the next.
