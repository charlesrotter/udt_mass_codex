# Xmax correction in plain language

`X_max` is not a wall at the outside of the universe. It is the distance-limit between observers,
in the same structural sense that `c` is the speed-limit between observers.

As relative speed approaches `c` in relativity, dilation grows without bound. In the intended UDT
frame, as observer separation approaches `X_max`, positional dilation grows without bound. The limit
is approached but not crossed by a finite comparison.

What has now been fixed is the meaning of the limit. What has not been solved is the formula telling
us exactly how dilation grows with general observer separation, what numerical value `X_max` has, or
how the complete angular and global geometry affects the comparison.

This gives future work a real test. A proposed distance-dilation law that does not develop the
`X_max` asymptote is not the intended UDT law. But reaching infinity at an endpoint is not enough to
win: many formulas can do that. A successful law must also preserve observer reciprocity, local
physics, angular/path information, and the ordinary `c_E` calibration.

It also simplifies the boundary question. We must stop asking for a boundary condition “at the wall”
until a complete solution proves that its representation of the observer limit is an actual boundary.
The asymptote itself supplies no action boundary term or physical edge.
