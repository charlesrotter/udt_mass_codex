## Finding C2 — a Fermi rate quotient needs an arrival map before it is UDT received ticking

**Exact antecedent.** The owner observable is reception proper interval divided
by emission proper interval on actual signals. Ordinary local clocks remain
ordinary. R6 defines Z=d tau_o/d tau_e=omega_e/omega_o on a specified regular
null branch. W4 gives one causal geometry; it supplies no permission to replace
that event correspondence by coordinate simultaneity.

**Literature bridge.** Neumann--Puetzfeld--Rubilar,
[2006.09716v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.09716v2), §II Eqs.(1)--(6), take the
reference clock at rest in the Fermi frame, unlike the earlier normalization,
and assume time-independent frame acceleration/rotation. Their defining
quantity is Cbar+1=(ds_X/ds_Y)^2, obtained from the local metric rate along
dy^0 with v^i=dy^i/dy^0. For flat inertial coordinates it is 1-v^2.
Section V Eqs.(33)--(57) inverts kinematically corrected quotients using known
positions and velocities. Section II explicitly leaves accurate time-transfer
modeling to a further calculation. Consequently their Eq.(4) terminology
“redshift” is not, by itself, a derivation that an arbitrary uncorrected
received-tick record equals this Fermi quotient. I use neither their simulations
nor apparatus precision claims. This is a protocol distinction, not a verdict
that their inversion is wrong.

**Own chain-rule check.** In any common coordinate t, put r_i=d tau_i/dt.
If the null arrival map is t_o=F(t_e), then

    Z = [r_o(F(t_e))/r_e(t_e)] F'(t_e).

The final factor and evaluation events matter. In flat space take an emitter
A:x=0 and an inertial receiver B:x=L+vt, |v|<1, near emissions for which the
chosen outgoing branch is regular. Both are ordinary proper clocks:

    t_o=(t_e+L)/(1-v),
    (d tau_B/dt)/(d tau_A/dt)=sqrt(1-v^2),
    Z=sqrt(1-v^2)/(1-v)=sqrt((1+v)/(1-v)).

For v=3/5 the simultaneous rate quotient is 4/5 while actual Z=2. Reversing
v gives the same 4/5 but Z=1/2. Curvature is zero throughout. Thus simply
feeding received ratios into the rest-reference Fermi master equation without
the transfer/protocol conversion can manufacture a false curvature inference.
This counterexample concerns object substitution, not any actual reported
compass experiment. A legitimate measurement strategy may model/correct the
transfer or choose a protocol making the conversion explicit.
