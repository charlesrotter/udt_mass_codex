# Lay report

We found something genuinely clarifying.

We had been asking whether one number—`phi`, the reciprocal clock depth—could somehow carry the
whole comparison between observers, including how their complete spatial frames turn and mix.
Mathematically, it cannot.

Think of `phi` as the odometer on a vehicle. It can keep a perfectly consistent running total along
its own channel. But if the vehicle turns, the odometer does not tell you its direction. Successive
turns in different directions cannot be replaced by adding one number; the turning information has
to be kept too.

That is what the Lorentz-frame algebra says. Comparisons in different directions do not commute.
Their mismatch is an angular rotation. No nontrivial rule can compress the entire connected frame
comparison into one additive real number.

So UDT's structure now looks cleaner:

```text
phi / reciprocal depth       = the additive clock-ruler channel;
angular coframe transport    = the unavoidable directional channel;
complete comparison         = both channels together.
```

This does not weaken `phi`. It stops asking `phi` to do a job it mathematically cannot do.

It also explains why `lambda`, the sideways response, kept refusing to emerge from observer
reciprocity. Once an observer direction is supplied and all spatial directions are treated equally,
`lambda=+1` follows conditionally. Once a particular ruler is also supplied, the remaining screen
still admits every real `lambda`. Observer-frame composition alone therefore does not decide how
the physical metric's angular sector responds. That belongs to the realization of the metric, not
automatically to the comparison rule.

We did **not** prove that every possible metric-wide selector is impossible. A whole solution could
derive a clock potential, observer reduction, or global groupoid using curvature, higher
derivatives, topology, or nonlocal consistency. The stationary Killing-norm clock is already a
bounded example. The honest result is narrower: the scalar-only route and pointwise metric-only
route are dead; the reduced reciprocal-plus-angular route is the correct surviving type.

The most useful next question is whether we have been assigning the transverse metric response to
the wrong layer. Before deriving anything else, we should audit whether the founding postulates
require `lambda` inside observer-comparison kinematics at all, or whether comparison is already the
reciprocal cocycle plus angular transport while `lambda` belongs downstream to whole-metric
realization.
