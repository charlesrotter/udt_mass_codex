# Lay report — what GR's long experience teaches us

Imagine two people in different rooms of a large, twisted building, each holding a compass and a
clock. The building's geometry tells each person what lengths and times mean where they stand. It
does **not** automatically tell them how to line up their compasses across the building.

GR handles that comparison in layers:

- choose the two observers;
- specify the corridor or light path connecting them;
- carry directions or clock information along that path using the geometry's connection;
- keep track of any twist accumulated around loops; and
- only then interpret the resulting comparison physically.

Different corridors can produce different rotations in a curved building. That is not a flaw or
an ambiguity to be patched. The path dependence itself is geometric information—holonomy.

The useful clue for UDT is therefore simple. We may have been asking for one globally preferred
“master instrument” before deriving the rules for how the orchestra's local instruments are
recognized as equivalent and carried from one observer to another. UDT already has a founded
reciprocal clock/ruler pair and a conditional composition law. What it still lacks is the native
rule saying:

> These are the legitimate local versions of the same relational object; this is how one version
> is transported along an observer path; and this is what remains physically different after that
> equivalence is removed.

That could be the missing table leg. It is not yet a discovered leg: GR only shows us the correct
kind of question. UDT must still derive the rule from its own metric. We also did **not** erase the
seven open extension directions by calling them gauge, and none of the open matter/action/bootstrap
questions changed status.
