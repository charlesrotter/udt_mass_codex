# G228 ponder — when do neighboring notes become one chord progression?

Date: 2026-08-23

A curvature reading at one event is a chord. G227 showed that the directional instruments in that
chord cannot be tuned independently.

Moving along one ray adds a next chord, but a single line may still be too thin to test whether the
music comes from one surrounding geometry. Any lawful-looking change along that line might simply
be called its derivative.

The metric becomes more demanding when several neighboring directions are heard together. The
differential Bianchi identity is the rule saying their changes must interlock. It is not a new
dynamical mechanism and it does not choose the melody; it is the compatibility condition for one
smooth Levi-Civita curvature field.

The screen also rotates while it is carried. If that rotation is omitted, the same tidal chord can
appear to change merely because the measuring axes turned. The correct screen-covariant derivative
subtracts that bookkeeping. The remaining change is the contraction of the metric's covariant
curvature derivative.

G228 therefore asks two deliberately separate questions:

1. how many neighboring directions are needed before one metric imposes a genuine cross-direction
   restriction; and
2. whether the screen and Jacobi phase descriptions are exactly the same first-variation geometry
   in different gauges.

Success would make the evaluator more cohesive. It would not supply a law choosing the numerical
curvature history.
