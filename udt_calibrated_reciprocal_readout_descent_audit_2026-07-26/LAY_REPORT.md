# Lay report

This audit asked whether the three global possibilities found previously can
be narrowed using the observed speed-of-light calibration and the physical
meaning of clock versus ruler.

It found an important restriction.

If UDT's two reciprocal channels literally are:

- the physical clock direction; and
- the physical ruler direction,

then one is timelike and the other is spacelike. A physical geometric
symmetry cannot swap them while preserving the metric, because metric
symmetries preserve which directions are timelike and spacelike. Allowing a
positive overall rescaling does not help.

So the elegant `lambda=0` reciprocal swap cannot be ordinary physical frame
transport under that direct clock/ruler interpretation.

There is a mathematical alternative. A tilted or mixed metric can make the
swap a symmetry, but then neither reciprocal channel alone is the physical
clock. The physical clock is a mixture of the two channels. Such a metric can
still be calibrated with measured `c`; the number `c` sets the units but does
not tell us how the channels are oriented.

That leaves four honest possibilities:

- keep the direct founding clock/ruler interpretation and use ordinary
  branch-dependent transport;
- derive a reason the complete metric uses the mixed interpretation;
- retain reciprocal swapping as an internal bookkeeping symmetry rather
  than physical frame motion; or
- discover a complete branch whose holonomy settles the issue differently.

We have not chosen among them. What we have done is prevent two incompatible
ideas—direct causal clock/ruler alignment and physical clock/ruler swapping—
from being used at the same time.
