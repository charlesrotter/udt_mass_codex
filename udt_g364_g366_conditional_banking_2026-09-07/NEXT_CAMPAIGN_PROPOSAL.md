There is real engineering precedent, not a currently certified UDT dataset:
ESA's GOCE instrument used paired accelerometers on three orthogonal arms to
measure gravity gradients. ESA documents both internal calibration and external
calibration using other gravity information; its Level1b products are already
calibrated/validated. This makes data provenance central, not optional.
See [ESA Instruments](https://www.esa.int/Applications/Observing_the_Earth/FutureEO/GOCE/Instruments),
[ESA Calibration](https://www.esa.int/Applications/Observing_the_Earth/FutureEO/GOCE/Calibration),
and [ESA Data products](https://www.esa.int/Applications/Observing_the_Earth/FutureEO/GOCE/Data_products).
Only primary documentation was consulted here; no observations were downloaded,
selected, fitted or tested. GOCE eligibility and suitable unconstrained channel
availability are UNVERIFIED; it is a starting point for an audit, not a selected
physical history, adopted instrument model or asserted ready-to-fit release.

Calibration observations would include independently controlled gain/alignment/
offset checks, clock and baseline metrology, attitude/rotation and nongravitational
controls, plus the designated A records for the scalar. B must be disjoint
records or genuinely unused channels with covariance/exposure accounted for.
Do not force trace-free data processing, fit the scalar in B, or calibrate gains
against the very Einstein relation being tested. Existing gravity-model inputs

need bounded corrections; a nearby apparatus does not make a vacuum hypothesis
