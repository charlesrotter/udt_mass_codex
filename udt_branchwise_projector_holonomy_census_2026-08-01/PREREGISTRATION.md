## Whole question

Across the complete registered post-July metric/coframe branch evidence, which branches actually
supply an intrinsic rank-one projector, its rank-two complement, or a reduced-holonomy subbundle?
For every occurrence, does it exist only locally, remain unique away from degeneracy, transport
covariantly, descend through the registered global completion, and carry nontrivial relative
projector curvature?

This is a branch census, not a search for a particle. It must retain branches with no projector,
constant/trivial projectors, several equally natural projectors, singular crossings, failed global
descent, incomplete completion data, and no-carrier outcomes.

## Branch and object typing

A source group is not a complete branch merely because its name says `complete`, `branch`, `cell`,
or `solution`. It must be classified from its own evidence as one of:

- complete global branch/witness;
- complete bounded off-shell branch/witness;
- local or pointwise family;
- formal/algebraic family;
- conditional carrier-only family;
- incomplete branch/completion data;
- control/counterfamily; or
- irrelevant to this census.

Candidate object classes are frozen in `CANDIDATE_CLASSES.tsv`. A rank-one line in four-dimensional
Lorentzian tangent space is not silently identified with the three-dimensional positive-bundle
projector used by the conditional response theorem. The type conversion itself must be derived.

## Six independent gates

Every candidate is tested separately for:

1. intrinsic definition from the branch's registered metric/coframe data;
2. rank and uniqueness, including every eigenvalue/symmetry degeneracy;
3. smooth local continuation through the full declared branch domain;
4. covariant transport or actual holonomy preservation;
5. global descent through every registered cap, seam, quotient, or transition; and
6. nontrivial relative curvature `Q(DP wedge DP)Q`, kept distinct from ambient curvature.

Passing fewer than six gates is recorded exactly; it is never rounded up to carrier emergence.
