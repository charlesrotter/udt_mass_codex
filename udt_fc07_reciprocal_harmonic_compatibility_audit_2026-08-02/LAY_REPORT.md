# Lay report

We found that three different “measuring marks” run along the same road through the cell:

1. `dphi` is like the road's local slope. After one closed lap, total uphill minus downhill is zero.
2. `theta1` is like an odometer. It records a positive physical distance around the lap.
3. `alpha` is like a lap counter. Topology defines one complete lap as exactly one.

They point along the same road, but they are not interchangeable. Saying “one lap” does not tell us
how many miles the road has, and neither tells us the exact pattern of hills.

That exposes one tempting shortcut. If the local slope were a single constant times the lap
counter, then going around the closed cell would force that constant to be zero—so the road would
have no hills at all. A nontrivial bridge must vary around the cell and balance to zero over the
whole lap.

The angular sector matters cleanly: the physical ruler itself becomes harmonic only when angular
area stays constant. If angular area changes, the harmonic form changes its local strength while
remaining on the same ruler direction.

We also found a real global readout: the harmonic flow through each angular cross-section is
constant, and the clock/ruler/angular factors combine so the local `phi` and angular determinant
cancel. But the value of that flow is not fixed by the current rules.

Density may still be important. With density present, `c` and `G` can form an inverse length of the
right dimensional type. What is still missing is the UDT rule saying that this density scale must
equal a particular geometric response. Without that rule, scanning densities would be turning a
knob that is not yet connected to the machine.

So the advance is a sharper wiring diagram, not closure: we know exactly which three channels must
be joined, why a constant join fails, where angular geometry enters, and why density could provide
the missing scale without yet providing the missing law.
