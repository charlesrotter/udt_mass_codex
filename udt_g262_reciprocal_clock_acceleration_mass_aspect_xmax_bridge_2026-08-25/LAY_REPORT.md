# G262 lay report

The simplification is real.

The UDT metric contains one clock factor. That same factor determines several things without adding
extra mechanisms:

- its value tells how the static clock runs and gives the endpoint redshift ratio;
- its slope tells how much acceleration is required to remain static;
- its value together with areal radius gives the metric's spherical compactness or “mass aspect”;
- slopes and curvature of that mass aspect give the gravitational and angular response.

So time, acceleration, gravity, and the geometric mass aspect are different readings of the same
metric state. They are not independent knobs.

There is also an exact conditional energy statement. If a physical carried energy is represented by
the same transported covector used in the null observer query, its received-to-emitted energy ratio
is the same numerical reciprocal factor as the clock ratio. Toward the working positional asymptote,
that ratio tends to zero in one direction and infinity in reverse.

Two cautions matter. A local object's invariant rest mass has not been shown to change. And the
metric's spherical mass aspect is not yet a normalized physical UDT mass or matter source. At a
zero-lapse end the geometric compactness saturates; it does not diverge.

Finally, this beautiful interlock still describes every positive primary profile. It does not tell
the metric which profile to realize. The remaining bridge is narrower now: connect the geometric
mass/source role back to the same metric by one nonidentity physical law. Renaming the mass aspect
or inserting `X_max` would not close it.
