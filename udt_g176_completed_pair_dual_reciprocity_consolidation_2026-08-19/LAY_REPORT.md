# G176 lay report

The complete metric first builds the whole two-observer comparison, including angular, screen,
mixing, and shift effects. Reciprocity then tells us how to mark the ruler paired with the clock.
That marking is unique.

Other rulers—such as ordinary metric arclength—remain useful measurements, but they are different
questions. They are not competing versions of the UDT reciprocal kernel.

The orchestra remains active. Purely spatial effects change where the marks lie on the reciprocal
tape; clock-side effects change reciprocal depth; shift and orientation information remain in their
own channels.

What remains open is which observer pairs Nature realizes globally, not how to read the scalar once
a completed reciprocal pair has been supplied.
