# Lay report

The earlier work appeared to leave two forks:

- one kind of geometry where the metric could recognize one unique clock direction; and
- another, more symmetric kind where the Hopf circle descended cleanly but seemed to hide the
  clock among two possible directions.

The new result shows that the second fork was not actually blind whenever dilation changes across
the cell.

Within the specific clock/circle plane registered by the construction, think of the two directions
as two rails. As `phi` changes, the metric shortens the clock rail and
lengthens the ruler rail by exactly opposite amounts. That changing pattern is visible in the
metric itself. It identifies the clock rail and ruler rail no matter how we rename or mix the two
coordinates. The result exactly recovers the founded reciprocal pair.

The two earlier certificates can also meet continuously. On the perfectly Hopf-symmetric side,
the opposite stretching identifies the clock. Arbitrarily small symmetry-breaking deformations
exist on which the old curvature-fingerprint method becomes available, and it identifies the same
clock. This is an existence statement, not a claim about every small deformation. There is no jump
to a different time direction along the certified handoff points.

The twist cross-check concerns only the part caused by changing depth; it does not claim that either
rail has no twist of any kind. The Hopf circle's own contact twist generally remains.

There is one honest exception: if `phi` is completely constant, neither rail changes. The new
metric compass then has nothing to read. Other geometric data can identify a natural direction,
but for nonzero twist it is not the founded clock. Constant depth is therefore a genuinely
different or degenerate stratum, not something to hide. If the circle contact curvature is also
switched off, every clock candidate becomes twist-free and neither method chooses one.

Topology also does less than it may first seem. It recognizes which rail closes into a circle, but
it allows the time rail to be tilted by any amount around that circle. And if a special metric has
extra symmetries, it may contain other two-rail choices; this audit does not choose among them.

Could the two useful strata later describe macro and micro regimes? Yes, the geometry now permits
that possibility: they are adjacent parts of one configuration space rather than mutually
exclusive theories. But nothing here assigns the labels. We still lack the equation, dynamics, or
bootstrap closure that says which stratum the universe uses in which regime.
