# G246 lay report

We no longer have to hand the metric a pre-drawn local light path between two supplied observers.

Imagine observer A shining in every possible direction. G245 showed that the metric itself draws
that entire local light cone. G246 now shows that, once observer B's worldline is also supplied, the
metric finds every place where A's cone meets B. Near any ordinary meeting, that automatically
gives one smooth sequence of paired clock readings and the full reciprocal two-observer ribbon.

There is one important distinction. Running the same comparison backward on paper is not the same
as B sending a new signal later. The paper inverse retraces the old relation into the past; a real
future return follows B's future cone and generally pairs different events.

Locally this closes a genuine gap. Globally, several light routes can still connect the same
observers—like going around a cylinder in different windings. The metric supplies all those routes,
but the reciprocal scalar alone does not choose one. The physical metric history and observer
population are also still inputs.
