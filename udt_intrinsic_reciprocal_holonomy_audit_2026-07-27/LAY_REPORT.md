# Lay report

We asked whether the metric's full clock–ruler–screen structure can be carried around the universe
and return with exactly the same orientation, independent of the route.

On these six complete test geometries, the answer is no.  Curvature mixes all six basic kinds of
Lorentz rotation/boost, and one clock–ruler mixing term is exactly nonzero no matter how we choose
the angular weight.  Every one of the 36 closed test routes brought the full structure back changed.

That does **not** break the clock law.  The clock ratio between two endpoints still depends only on
their difference in `phi`.  What fails to be endpoint-only is the larger package containing the
clock, its reciprocal ruler, and the two angular screen directions.

The simplest picture is:

- the clock reading closes by endpoints;
- the full orientation remembers the route;
- route-by-route transport composes perfectly;
- the metric does not yet supply a rule that erases that route memory.

This rules out an attractive shortcut, not the theory.  If UDT ultimately requires a unique global
full frame, its physical on-shell geometry must reduce the holonomy, or it must derive a separate
global identification.  Neither has been selected here.
