## 2. Clock calibration determines both coefficients

Stationarity translates a fixed regular signal branch into another, with equal
coordinate emission and arrival intervals. Equivalently the null Killing energy

    R_AB = dτ_B/dτ_A = sqrt(f_B/f_A),
    delta_AB = -log R_AB = (1/2)log(f_A/f_B).                 (1)

    chi_clock = tanh(delta_AB) = (f_A-f_B)/(f_A+f_B).        (2)

same comparison gives reciprocal R and odd delta/chi. Changing actual observers

Supply three distinct ordered radii 0<r0<r1<r2 in K and two ideal calibrated
clock ratios R_0i. Put q_i=R_0i². They determine a,b by the linear system

    (r_i²-q_i r0²)a + (r_i^-1-q_i r0^-1)b = q_i-1, i=1,2. (3)

For data generated by any positive member of this family its determinant is

    D = [(r1-r0)(r2-r0)(r2-r1)(r0+r1+r2)]
        /[r0 r1 r2 f(r0)] > 0.                             (4)

Its determinant is f0 D. Subtract a column2+b column3 from column1,
then multiply row i by r_i. The determinant of rows [r_i,r_i³,1]
is the displayed Vandermonde product times r0+r1+r2. This proves exact
identifiability within the family for every consistent positive datum, not
only the numerical examples. One ratio alone leaves an affine one-parameter
coefficient family; sufficiently small changes preserve strict positivity.

Known radii/areal labeling, family form and L are independent calibration inputs.

Noisy or inconsistent ratios must be propagated through the actual system and

After (3), another clock ratio, sky direction and proper round-trip time are

## 3. Exact finite-endpoint ray and time evaluator

With dot denoting an affine parameter and common constants suppressed, the null
Killing integrals and null constraint give

    E=f Tdot>0, J=r² phidot, B=J/E>0,
    rdot²=E²[1-B² f/r²],
    |dphi/dr| = B/[r² sqrt(1-B² f/r²)],
    |dT/dr| = 1/[f sqrt(1-B² f/r²)].                       (5)

We study a supplied one-periapse branch: r decreases from r_A to p then increases
to r_B, with p<min(r_A,r_B), f(p)>0, and

    K_p=f(p)/p² = a+1/p²+b/p³,
    H_p(r)=1/p²+b/p³-1/r²-b/r³ >0 for p<r<=max(r_A,r_B),
    H_p'(p)=(2p+3b)/p⁴>0.                                (6)

These inequalities exclude a circular/degenerate turning point and intermediate
barriers. B=1/sqrt(K_p). Define leg integrals

    Phi_R(p,b) = integral_p^R dr/[r² sqrt(H_p(r))],
    T_R(p,a,b) = sqrt(K_p) integral_p^R dr/[f(r) sqrt(H_p(r))]. (7)

For a fixed supplied finite endpoint angular separation Phi, select a regular
root of Phi_rA+Phi_rB=Phi. Existence and uniqueness are hypotheses unless checked
for a stated subcase; no global multiple-image or winding classification follows.
Branch consistency is part of calibration, not an adjustable extra fit parameter.

At reception the apparent source is the direction opposite the arriving ray.
Its acute angle psi_B from the inward radial direction satisfies

    sin(psi_B) = sqrt(f_B)/(r_B sqrt(K_p)).                 (8)

This follows by projecting k on the static orthonormal tetrad, so is a measured
angle for the supplied observer, not a coordinate slope. A radially outgoing
ray has the same acute angle relative to the outward radial axis. At A the
analogous incoming radial leg supplies the corresponding emission angle.

The ideal relay gives total elapsed proper time on the SAME clock at A,

    tau_round = (2L/c_E) sqrt(f_A) [T_rA+T_rB].             (9)

This avoids a remote synchronization offset. A finite relay turnaround would
add its separately calibrated contribution; it cannot be hidden in geometry.
One-way coordinate duration alone is not a two-clock observable without a
supplied synchronization convention.

For quadrature set x=sqrt(1-p/r), u=1-x², and

    J_x=2-x²+(b/p)(3-3x²+x⁴),
    V_x=u²+a p²+(b/p)u³.

Then on 0<=x<=sqrt(1-p/R),

    Phi_R=integral 2/sqrt(J_x) dx,
    T_R=integral 2p sqrt(f(p))/[V_x sqrt(J_x)] dx.          (10)

The square-root turning singularity is removed, with J_0=2+3b/p>0.
Strict positivity controls denominators on the closed leg. No infinite-radius

## 4. A coordinate cancellation does not remove measured dependence

The ENTIRE finite spatial-orbit evaluator Phi_R at fixed p,b,R is independent
of a. Equivalently u=1/r obeys u''+u=-(3b/2)u² with phi derivatives; its
first integral is (u')²=1/B²-a-u²-bu³. Holding B instead of p is a different
comparison and does not have the same cancellation of the boundary data.
At fixed endpoints and selected same orbit branch, p is therefore unchanged
when a varies within the positive domain. Coordinate orbit data alone under
this marking cannot identify a.

Nevertheless write V_R=1/R²+b/R³ and V_p=1/p²+b/p³. Equation (8) gives

    sin²(psi_B)=(a+V_B)/(a+V_p),
    partial_a sin²(psi_B)=(V_p-V_B)/(a+V_p)² >0.           (11)

The inequality is exactly the regular branch condition H_p(r_B)>0. Measured
angle changes strictly with a on any open allowed interval, even while the
coordinate path stays unchanged. The clock and proper-time formulas also
retain a; no universal monotonicity of round-trip time is claimed here.
This is a precise example of information lost by restricting the readout,
not evidence that every frozen slice or approximation fails.
