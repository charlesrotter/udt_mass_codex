# LSB1 — lay decision brief

We now have a concrete virtual experiment in which one geometry must answer
several questions at once. In this supplied family, clock comparisons at three
known locations determine its two adjustable coefficients. Those same settings
then predict another clock comparison, where a signal appears in the receiver's
sky, and how long an outgoing-and-returning signal takes. Each answer does not
get its own adjustment.

One useful lesson is exact: an unchanged path drawn in coordinates can conceal
a changed observed angle. Timing also changes in the finite examples we checked. Looking only at that drawing would
miss information the metric still contains. This supports the roadmap's joint
readouts; it does not prove that every simplification hides important physics.

The finite examples passed, and a fresh reviewer reproduced the predictions by
integrating the geodesics separately. The reviewed result is conditional and
unpromoted. This is a practical step from geometric formulas to linked virtual
measurements, not a new empirical confirmation or a derivation of physical light.

Our “faux light” assumptions are explicit: recognizable probes follow null paths,
ideal clocks tag emission/reception, local frames supply viewing angles, and an
ideal turnaround device supplies the return signal. These assumptions allow
timing and direction work without a photon or Maxwell theory. They do not supply
brightness or an energy law. The scale and apparatus layout are also supplied.

This prepares eclipse-style direction and echo-timing comparisons in principle.
It does not yet attach the geometry to the Sun or predict a distinctive UDT
departure. If GR uses the same geometry and measurement rules, its answers agree.
The native response/assembly question remains separate open work.
