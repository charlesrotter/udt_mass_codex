The useful advance is narrower than deriving a vacuum law: the expansion need
not ask how the response behaves on curvature data that no metric can possess.
We can express the local data in normal metric coordinates, where every allowed
coefficient tuple comes from an actual local Lorentz metric. This is geometric
representation, not a claim that every such metric solves a UDT equation.

Inside an explicitly conditional response class, the lowest-curvature
trace-free term has the already classified Ricci form. Writing
S=Ric-(R/4)g and using supplied weighted slow-variation bounds,

    TF(E) = a S + remainder,
    ||remainder|| <= D epsilon^4 + M epsilon^2 omega(M epsilon^2),
    omega(t) -> 0.

This needs one fixed finite-order, metric-only, unoriented natural symmetric
response, defined on a full neighborhood of flat realizable metric data and
Frechet differentiable at the flat jet. Those are conditional response assumptions, not ordinary
initial data and not all supplied by Local Metric Sufficiency. The curvature
derivative hierarchy is a supplied regime. No physical length is selected.
The coefficient a may be ZERO. Only if a!=0 and DDR is imposed does this give
S/epsilon^2 -> 0. It is not a proof of physical GR recovery or of a convergent
family of field-law solutions.

What is genuinely new relative to GL1: an intrinsic proof eliminating the
unrealizable-ambient-data extension, plus finite-order equivalence of its metric
coefficient bounds and actual curvature-derivative bounds. Curvature-squared
terms in the conversion must be retained. The normal-coordinate machinery is
standard mathematics applied here, not newly invented physics; see
[Jentsch's primary account](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1509.08269v2) and SOURCE_NOTES.md.
