# ICN1 — what the interframe construction gives us

We kept both observers' own clocks ordinary and constructed their actual outward
and return signal comparisons. An ideal immediate relay and regular null paths
are explicit conditions of this calculation.

The two received-tick ratios determine how radar-measured separation changes.
If BOTH matched legs show net redshift, that radar separation must be increasing.
This is an operational timing statement; it does not identify the cause as Hubble
expansion or ordinary recession. One leg can be blue while the product still
indicates increasing radar distance, and the calculation has not independently
separated positional, velocity and gravitational contributions.

There is also a constructive connection to geometry. In a specified longitudinal
case, the ratio of those two clock ratios gives a metric coefficient along the
second clock. Their changes over time, together with free fall, give that
coefficient's first derivatives there. Its ordinary local proper-time clock
behavior remains intact. This is a restricted reconstruction from timing data,
not a general four-dimensional field equation or a law prescribing those data.

Adding a third clock checks the actual routes and relay events. A direct signal
and one relayed through that clock need not arrive together or have the same
clock ratio. We also identify a precise limit of timing alone: supplied geometries
can have identical clock-network records and unchanged clock neighborhoods while
differing in their interiors. These are geometric controls, not proved native UDT
solutions; they do not establish that UDT cannot select a predictive relation.

Your accepted c_E input already converts round-trip clock duration to distance.
G_obs remains available as an input too. Their dimensions alone do not select an
intrinsic length or time. If a justified mass, density or geometric datum becomes
available, they can help attach scale. No such physical source law was inserted
in this calculation, and there is no need to derive either observed constant first.

The gain is an explicit measurement-to-geometry connection and necessary timing
restrictions. The full physical pair assembly and native separation-dependent
law remain open. The near-distance and asymptotic statements are conditional
checks, not established solar precision or a realized cosmological limit.
