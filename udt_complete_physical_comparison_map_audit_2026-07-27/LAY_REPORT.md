# Lay report

We found that the missing comparison may not be one single object.

In the complete stationary geometry, the metric itself can tell two stationary observers their
clock-depth difference by comparing the strength of the same intrinsic time direction at their two
locations. That part depends only on the endpoints.

The angular frame behaves differently. Carrying directions through curved space can depend on which
route is taken. So the natural comparison can contain both:

- one endpoint clock-depth value; and
- a route-dependent record of how the larger frame turns.

This is not a contradiction. At a far-side or cut-locus point, every route can give the same clock
dilation while delivering a differently rotated angular frame.

That means we no longer need to force the entire comparison to be either endpoint-only or
path-dependent. UDT can naturally be a mixture of the two.

The caveat is substantial: this has been derived for a complete stationary configuration, not for
every observer or a dynamically selected universe. We still need the whole theory to decide whether
that kind of configuration is physically selected and whether the clock and angular pieces remain
separate or become more deeply coupled.
