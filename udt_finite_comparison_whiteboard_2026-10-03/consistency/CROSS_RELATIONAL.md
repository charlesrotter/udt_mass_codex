## Judgment and survivor

The conditional asymptotic lemma survives this inspection. It is a concrete
global-geometric alternative to requiring a local response identity, but the
physical reason to impose the extra conformal regularity is unestablished.
Its strongest honest return is a simple-pole diagnostic in the supplied

## Direct reconstruction of the load-bearing algebra

The supplied metric in conformal coordinates is
g=Omega(eta)^(-2)(-deta^2+dx^2). A comoving unit clock has
u=Omega partial_eta. An outgoing null phase theta=eta-x has null gradient;
up to future orientation and a common positive affine normalization, its
frequency measured by that clock is proportional to Omega. Therefore
omega_e/omega_o=Omega_e/Omega_o. With a(0)=1 and the first emission eta_e=0,
the receiver at initial separation L is met at eta_o=L, so p=1/Omega(L).
This independently recovers the clock ratio without assuming the optional
RCD1 response equation. Varying nearby emissions on fixed comoving worldlines
gives the same result from dt_o/dt_e=a_o/a_e.

Since dt=deta/Omega, direct differentiation gives H=-Omega' and
dot H=-Omega Omega''. For the stipulated spatially flat four-metric,
R=6(dot H+2H^2)=12Omega'^2-6Omega Omega''. A C2 simple zero with
Omega'_*=-h<0 therefore gives R->12h^2, and its orthonormal Ricci components
approach diag(-3h^2,3h^2,3h^2,3h^2). The local curvature statement is sound;
no field equation or stress identification is needed.

Taylor's theorem gives Omega=h delta+O(delta^2), hence
p=(h delta)^(-1)+O(1). Integrating proves
t=-(1/h)log(delta/delta_ref)+O(1); the positive reference length only shifts
the time origin. The proposed explicit finite inequality follows from the
same Taylor remainder when a supplied bound |Omega''|<=M actually holds.
Neither C2 regularity alone nor a finite sampled curve supplies a numerical M.

The beta controls also check directly: a_1=exp(ht) has H=h,R=12h^2;
a_2=(1+ht)^2 has H=2h/(1+ht), R=36h^2/(1+ht)^2. They share the finite
conformal range and infinite comoving proper time but have different pole
orders and curvature limits. Thus divergence plus finite range is strictly
weaker than the proposed transversality condition, within the stated sector.

## Potential overclaims and smallest repairs

1. **Keep the sector in the headline.** The geometric completion condition is
   coordinate-independent, but p(L)=1/Omega(L), the simple pole in initial L,
   and H=-Omega' use the specified homogeneous, spatially flat metric,
   normalization and comoving clocks. General observers can contribute endpoint
   Doppler factors and different incidence maps. The proposal mostly preserves
   this restriction; final presentation should consistently say “in this
   homogeneous/comoving sector,” especially next to the pole conclusion.

2. **Read beta2's rejection at its actual strength.** It certainly fails in the
   displayed flat conformal completion and every smooth nonzero conformal gauge
   of that completion. Replacing Omega=x^2 by x makes the same-chart auxiliary
   metric x^-2 times Minkowski, which is degenerate/singular at x=0 and is not
   an admissible smooth gauge repair. A singular change of completion is not
   addressed by that observation alone. The proposal explicitly acknowledges
   this; retain that wording unless a separate invariant argument is supplied.
   A change to a null rather than spacelike boundary would in any case change
   the proposed physical condition. Do not call beta2 globally uncompletable.

3. **Do not identify the label endpoint with X_max.** L_* is the first-arrival
   limit for one emission epoch and comoving preparation. Changing the epoch or
   protocol changes that operational range; it is not already a universal
   native relational endpoint. The proposal's explicit disavowal of the
   equality is necessary and sufficient here. An eventual X_max connection
   requires its own physical assignment, not a relabeling of L_*.

4. **Bump freedom is real.** Smooth support strictly inside x in(0,1) makes all
   initial and endpoint jets equal to beta1, while interior Omega',Omega'' and
   therefore H,R can change. Small epsilon preserves positivity. If a later
   version also imposes strict expansion or received-redshift monotonicity,
   choose epsilon using the bounded bump derivative to preserve Omega'<0;
   those inequalities still do not remove all interior freedom. This survivor
   defeats a unique exponential interior history, but does not prove that
   every arbitrary clock curve can meet the completion condition or a selected
   response equation.

5. **GR fidelity remains separate.** The beta1 geometry is the already supplied
   positive-curvature homogeneous control, so this condition permits behavior
   already compatible with a GR cosmological-term sector. A simple pole is not
   automatically an additional UDT contribution, a current GR-filter pass or
   an empirical discriminator against GR. Ordinary matter/environment behavior
   and a physical source law have not been modeled. Do not phrase regular
   future completion as a recovered local source response.

## What is repeated, and what remains new

The received ratio, p-to-curvature formulas, finite first/echo distinction and
exponential response-parameter blindness are source-owned RCD1/FPC1 results.
The conformal Taylor lemma is elementary mathematics, not a new universal
physical law. The useful proposed connection is the explicit additional global
regularity condition and its operational asymptotic consequence without an
f(R) assumption. The beta2 and bump families disclose exactly the condition's
extra strength and its remaining freedom. That is new enough as a bounded
conditional target, provided it is not sold as a native restriction already
hidden in the working X_max language.
