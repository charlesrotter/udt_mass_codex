# Lay report

The old `mu` really was one instrument in the orchestra—but it was measured in the hallway between
two observers, not in the final two-dimensional sheet on which their clock/ruler comparison is
written.

When we line up the old and new notation carefully, `mu` is one clock-to-one-angular-direction entry
of the modern endpoint mixing transition. So the underlying idea was not lost.

The complete model has four such mixing entries, not one. Many different summaries of those four
entries reduce to the same old `mu` when the other three are switched off. The one-instrument result
therefore cannot tell us which summary—if any—Nature uses for the full orchestra.

There is another separation. The final pair metric records what the chosen observer-pair surface
actually samples. Two different ambient mixings can produce the same final pair metric if the pair
surface tilts correspondingly. That means the old full-arrow `mu` cannot be silently identified with
the final `phi_pair` or `c_eff` result.

So the corrected picture is:

- the mixing channel is genuinely present in the modern machinery;
- the old scalar has a precise restricted address;
- it is not a new term to bolt onto the endpoint formula;
- it is not yet a unique complete invariant;
- and no physical history or regime score was derived here.
