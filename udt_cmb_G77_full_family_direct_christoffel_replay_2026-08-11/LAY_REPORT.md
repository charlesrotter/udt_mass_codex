# G77 lay report

G76 used one mathematical route to send light-like viewing directions through all 591 candidate
geometries. G77 rebuilt that test using the metric's ordinary path geometry directly—effectively
checking the same map with a different engine.

The two engines agree across the entire family. Every one of roughly 1.5 million viewing directions
reached the far sphere, and none of the reconstructed sky patches tore, flipped, or collapsed.

Four difficult cases had been left undecided because the earlier time steps were slightly too
coarse. G77 doubled the resolution twice. Their errors shrank by about four each time, and all four
now pass the registered numerical gate. Their old G76 labels remain in history; G77 adds stronger
evidence rather than rewriting the earlier record.

This tells us the whole-sky mapping machinery is numerically robust under this supplied stationary
query. It still does not tell us which geometry nature uses or what physical CMB pattern results.
