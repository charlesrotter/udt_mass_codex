# Lay report — the orchestra was one machine

We found both a simplification and a wiring mistake.

The old construction had two square, two-by-two pieces. One measured how the clock-distance sheet
leans sideways. The other measured how two neighboring directions on the observer's sky spread
apart. Because they had the same shape, the calculation conditionally treated them as the same
piece.

They are not the same piece. In perfectly flat geometry, the clock-distance sheet can have no
sideways motion at all while the observer's angular view still expands normally with distance.
That exact example catches the mistake immediately.

The repair is simpler than adding another rule. Build one complete observer-centered relation with
four directions: observer time, depth, and two sky angles. The clock-distance reading comes from
the first two directions. Angular magnification and shear come from the other two. All four are
parts of the same metric-built map, so their interaction is already inside the geometry.

This removes several apparent mysteries:

- the observer's question is an input, not something the universe must choose;
- the initial point-screen has a natural zero size and unit opening rate;
- local branches grow from the metric's own map;
- many separate coframe and realization variables are bookkeeping for one combined object.

It does not choose the universe's complete metric history. It also does not supply source
brightness, populations, or weights for multiple observed images. Those remain separate open
questions.

The musical version is: we had mistaken two sections of the score for one instrument. The metric
naturally supplies the whole score; terminal reciprocal depth and angular expansion/shear are
different voices in it.
