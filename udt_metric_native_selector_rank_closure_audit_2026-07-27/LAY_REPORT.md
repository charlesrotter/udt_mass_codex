# Lay report

We found a new piece of the bootstrap picture, but not the complete loop.

Once the metric itself identifies the unique time direction, we can remove that time direction and
look at the three-dimensional “space of its clock paths.” The metric gives this space a definite
shape, volume, distance, and curvature. The twisting of time does not alter that spatial shape; it
appears separately as the curvature of the clock connection.

That means the geometry can now calculate some genuine branchwise global properties from a chosen
`phi` pattern. These are ingredients a future bootstrap law could use; the present theory has not
yet selected them as one side of a bootstrap equation.

The reverse direction is still missing. Knowing one global volume—or any finite list of ordinary
global numbers—does not determine every detail of a smooth `phi` pattern. Infinitely many small
changes can leave those numbers unchanged. We still need a law telling the global geometry how to
act back on the local pattern.

The ordinary diameter of this compact three-space is reached by some pair of points. Your working
`X_max` is an unreachable observer-pair limit, so they are not yet the same object. Deriving their
relationship could make `X_max` a powerful constraint.

Density cannot close the loop yet because the metric now gives us a branchwise volume but still not
a native total mass. The next missing joint is therefore the global-to-local return law—not a better
numerical guess for density.
