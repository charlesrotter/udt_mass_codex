# Lay report — what the orchestra now looks like

The big simplification is that the instruments are not loose pieces anymore.

Once you give the metric one actual observation setup, it produces one complete geometric motion.
Clock/ruler dilation, apparent area, shear, and rotation are different typed readouts of that motion.
We do not need to invent an extra knob that mixes them after the fact.

The SNe test says the rebuilt machine can still play the old, successful middle-regime chord on
both Pantheon+ and DES. It does not yet prove that Nature chose that machine setting, because the
test supplied the old chord's area and brightness rule.

We also found a very revealing trap. In the simple static spherical version, the metric itself does
indeed give both:

```text
pair dilation = phi
apparent angular radius = r.
```

That looks like the missing elegant closure. But if the fitted P1 SNe curve is treated as the
static shape of space all the way back to the observer, curvature blows up at the observer. The
algebra is right; the interpretation is wrong.

The likely lesson is simple: the SNe curve describes what is seen along a history of observation,
not a frozen radial photograph of space. We had taken a melody played through time and tried to
carve it into a stationary instrument.

The next job is therefore not another fit. It is to define the smallest real observing junction:
several observers, a common source event, and the exact geometric transport along each ray. Once
that junction is typed, we can let a regular time-live metric play through it and see what chord it
produces—without showing it the SNe answer first.
