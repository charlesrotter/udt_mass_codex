# G105 lay report

The orchestra was already there. The confusion was about what kind of pattern it can make.

Imagine a perfectly ordinary field of dots seen through a warped lens. The lens may crowd some dots
together and spread others apart. Relative to an ordinary survey map, that crowding has a pattern,
and the pair-counting machinery measures the pattern's autocorrelation. Nothing had to arrange the
original dots periodically.

That is the first UDT route. The complete clock/ruler, angular, mixing, and pair-embedding machinery
determines the map before its area distortion is read. G105 gives an exact all-sector example where
the angular distortion is not uniform.

A stronger kaleidoscope can also make several linked images of the same bead or couple two rays.
That is the separate `H` route. It remains mathematically lawful but is not yet forced by the local
metric equations.

So the desired observational-artifact picture is no longer blocked on `H`. It is conditionally
well-defined through the orchestra's Jacobian. What is still missing is the physical complete
history that tells us the actual distortion, plus a careful account of which part the survey random
catalogue removes. No BAO or CMB pattern has yet been fitted.
