# G153 lay report

The earlier question compared the wrong things. The relational position is like the reading on a
complete observer-pair dial. The pair ruler is like one local tick mark on the metric. A dial reading
of 60 is not supposed to equal the physical size of one tick.

G137 adopted the dial: `rho=X_max tanh(phi_pair)`. G147 explicitly warned that drawing this as the
vector `rho n` was only a convenient local picture, not a spacetime displacement or proper length.
G152 correctly showed what would be required if we nevertheless forced that finite vector to equal
one ruler variation. G153 now shows that this was not the native join.

The metric-native join is the rate at which the dial reading changes per local clock or ruler tick:

\[
d\rho=u(\rho)\theta^0+n(\rho)\theta^1.
\]

Both coefficients come from the complete pair metric once a smooth history and `X_max` realization
are supplied. The angular and mixing orchestra remains inside the response through `phi_pair`,
`T`, `L`, `beta`, and their derivatives. If `X_max` is modulated, its derivative also enters by the
ordinary product rule; the constant-`X_max` expression is only a subcase. No extra loudness
coefficient has been inserted.

This tells us more clearly what a future test toward `X_max` means: follow these derived response
coefficients across one coherent metric history and see whether its finite relational position
approaches the shared asymptote consistently. We have not yet supplied or selected that history,
and we have not calculated `X_max`.
