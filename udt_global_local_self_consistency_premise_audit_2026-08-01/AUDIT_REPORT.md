## What was learned

The phrase “the metric is the theory” cannot do two jobs. It validly rejects imported physics and
requires every eventual closure rule to be metric-native. It does not itself constitute that rule.

Likewise, “complete metric” means no active field, sector, boundary, or join has been omitted from a
supplied problem. It does not mean the problem has a solution, that the solution is unique, or that
one solution is physically realized.

The working bootstrap picture becomes a genuinely additional physical assertion only when it says
that independent global data constrain local/complete configurations and the resulting nonempty
proper solution set agrees with the metric's recomputed readouts. To become operational, that
assertion still needs explicit owner adoption plus a separately tested membership rule, or a future
metric theorem that derives the relation.
