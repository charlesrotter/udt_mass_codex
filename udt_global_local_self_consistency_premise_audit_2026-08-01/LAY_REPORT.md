# Lay report

We found the dividing line between “the metric contains everything” and “the metric chooses one
universe.” Those are not the same statement.

Imagine a complete architectural drawing. “Complete” means every room, pipe, wire, door, and outer
wall is shown. It does not mean the drawing tells us which of several complete buildings must be
built. Likewise, the UDT metric can describe a complete candidate universe and calculate many of
its local and global properties without thereby selecting that candidate as the realized one.

Reciprocity adds an important rule: the selection cannot secretly depend on which observer is
describing the building. But many different selection rules can treat all observers equally.

An observer-respecting yes/no property of complete configurations is enough to reject some
candidates, but it does not yet prove that global data are feeding back; it could be a purely local
rule in disguise. Genuine mutual determination minimally needs two independently adjustable sides:
the candidate configuration and its global data. Whether the pair is allowed must depend on both.
Only then are the global data recomputed from the metric and the two sides required to agree.

That is still much weaker than an action or optimization formula. It permits multiple answers and
says nothing yet about dynamics or stability. But without an explicit rule for which pairs are
allowed, bootstrap remains an architectural picture rather than a calculation.

The global/local bootstrap picture is coherent and may be the right additional UDT principle, but
the current record does not derive it from the metric, finiteness, or Reciprocity. This audit does
not adopt it. Even adoption of that principle-level property would not provide a calculation until
its exact membership rule was derived or separately proposed and tested. A genuinely new theorem
from the same metric premises remains possible; what should stop is repetition of the already
exhausted routes.
