# Lay report — what the no-shortcut reconstruction found

The good news is that your main mental picture was substantially right:

> Once a particular observer-pair comparison and a complete metric are supplied, the reciprocal
> clock/ruler part, angular screen, mixing, and the way the pair sits in the geometry are combined
> first. Only then does the theory read one final clock ratio, `c_eff/c_E`.

So the orchestra is inside the pair relation. It is not a correction pasted onto redshift at the
end.

The shortcut was subtler. Earlier work crushed the whole orchestra into a small three-number Gram
matrix before studying how it changes. That small matrix is enough to reproduce the final snapshot,
but it no longer tells us which instrument produced which part, and it does not tell us how the
instruments evolve. Holding that compressed object fixed and varying only reciprocal depth therefore
did not derive the universe's quiet-middle score. It only described one controlled algebraic test.

The new exact formula keeps five blocks separate:

- the reciprocal/base clock-ruler block;
- the two-dimensional angular screen;
- four mixing components;
- the base part of the observer-pair realization;
- the angular part of that realization.

All five can change the final pair clock ratio in a generic example. At a perfectly unmixed symmetric
point, several angular effects begin only at second order, so they look silent to a first-order test.
That is a symmetry effect, not proof of a physical middle regime.

The `mu` issue was also real. The current complete mixing is a four-component matrix. The older
scalar called `mu` came from a different July ansatz. No derivation currently turns one into the
other, and the complete pair metric allows many equally lawful scalar summaries. Choosing one and
calling it *the* `mu` would be another shortcut.

What we now have is a trustworthy complete **evaluator**: feed it a full metric history and a full
observer-pair history, and it computes the one combined `phi_pair` and `c_eff/c_E` without dropping
the orchestra.

What we still do not have is the musical score: the native rule that determines how those histories
actually change across micro, ordinary, and cosmological regimes. That is the next physics question,
but we can now ask it without confusing a compressed snapshot with the score itself.
