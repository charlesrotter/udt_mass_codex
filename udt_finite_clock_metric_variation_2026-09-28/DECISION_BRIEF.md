# FCV1 — what the derivation tells us

We derived how an actual comparison between separated clocks changes when the
geometry changes. The calculation includes the path between them, the changed
arrival event, both clocks' calibration and the specified observer protocol.
This completes the calculation that the earlier local clock-coefficient
variation left out. Positional dilation remains the founding interpretation
of the geometry behind c_E, light cones and causal timing.

The attempted connection to the field equation did not close. Reversing the
same comparison produces the expected reciprocal answer for every regular
geometry in the calculation. Differentiating that consistency relation therefore
does not tell us which geometry UDT permits. DDR's adopted physical balance
condition remains a separate, stronger statement whose response is unidentified.

There is a concrete reason the measured response cannot simply be named that
local physical response: changing the geometry only BETWEEN two clocks can
change their received tick ratio while leaving all geometry near both clocks
unchanged. The variation of one measurement depends on its whole path and query.
We also proved why its nonzero response cannot directly be represented by a
smooth local tensor integrated over spacetime. This does not rule out local
field equations governing the geometry, or a justified reconstruction using
multiple measurements.

The gain is a usable full variation formula and a specific failed identification,
not a UDT field equation. We did not add an assumption to force a successful
answer. A next attempt needs a defended identification of the physical response
in DDR, or a justified procedure connecting a family of comparisons to it.
Repeating inverse-clock consistency alone will not supply that identification.
No new physical postulate has been proved necessary.
