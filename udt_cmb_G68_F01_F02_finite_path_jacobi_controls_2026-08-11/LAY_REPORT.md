# G68 lay report

We extended the tiny, local “light-bundle” test across a complete stretch of twenty-one deliberately
chosen test geometries.

Imagine drawing a small circle on a transparent sheet at the observer and carrying it through the
geometry to a second sheet. In the round F01 controls, the circle simply grows evenly. In every
mixing-on F02 control, it becomes a very slightly stretched ellipse. How much its total area changes
depends on the entire shape of the mixing between the two sheets—not just the mixing at either end.

All twenty-one test journeys reached the far sheet cleanly. None turned around, collapsed to a
caustic, or left the usable chart. Reversing the direction of mixing reversed the sideways angular
drift exactly, while leaving the underlying stretch related by the expected mirror operation.

The especially useful result is that the finite behavior retains the clean quadratic weak-mixing
pattern found locally: halve weak mixing and the departure from the round result becomes about four
times smaller.

What we now possess is a functioning geometric projection instrument. What we still lack is the
universe telling us which background profile and which remote comparison surface to put into that
instrument, plus the source/state information needed to turn a geometric mode into observed CMB
power. These tests were controls, not candidate universes.
