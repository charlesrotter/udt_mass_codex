## Frame

G199 proved that the two strict radial null directions of the declared primary static-spherical
metric are reversal-related and have no native chiral screen split.  G187 already derived the
finite screen equations for one supplied nonradial ray.  G200 joins those results without adding a
metric component.

At one static source event, a unit spatial ruler `N` in the orbital plane supplies the two future
normalized null directions

\[
\ell_+=U+N,
\qquad
\ell_-=U-N.
\]

The whole question is whether their finite responses differ because the metric applies a different
law to the two directions, or only because the two rays subsequently sample different radii and
therefore different values of the same supplied `phi(r)` history.

## Native hypothesis to test

The primary metric should give one local law:

- the two signs have the same energy and opposite initial radial/angular momenta;
- the local screen tide depends on `L^2`, not the sign of `L`;
- both directions therefore begin with the same screen chord;
- their finite maps may diverge as functions of affine distance because `r_+(lambda)` and
  `r_-(lambda)` traverse different metric regions.

This is regime sampling, not a directional on/off switch.
