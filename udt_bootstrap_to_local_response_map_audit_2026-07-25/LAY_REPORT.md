# Lay report

The bootstrap idea can be broader than “the universe has the right density.”
It may say that the universe closes consistently only when its total energy,
density, curvature, boundary geometry, maximum observer separation, and other
global properties agree with one another.

Your chicken-and-egg description captures the heart of it: matter and energy
shape the universe; the resulting universe sets the conditions under which
matter can exist; and a physically allowed universe must make those two sides
agree. That is a genuine increase in conceptual clarity.

We found the exact mathematical shape such a rule would need. Think of those
global properties as an orchestra. There must be one arrow saying how the
state of the whole orchestra constrains each instrument, and another saying
how all the instruments together create the sound of the whole orchestra.
Closure occurs when both descriptions agree. Differentiating both arrows would
tell us how changing the whole universe pushes on a small local piece of
geometry and how that local change feeds back globally.

What we have is the wiring diagram for that orchestra. What we do not yet have
is the score. UDT has not yet told us exactly which global quantities belong in
the rule, how the whole constrains the parts, how the parts recompute the whole,
or how strongly each part contributes.

In your broad sense, this is optimization: tuning the entire orchestra until
it is mutually in tune. Mathematically it is a coupled closure or fixed point;
it does not need one score to minimize. A narrower minimize/maximize rule would
need a derived objective, and we do not yet have one.

Density alone is not enough. Changing the shape of the angular metric without
changing its local volume is invisible to the volume part of density. Native
mass, energy, curvature, or another global channel could still respond to that
shape change. So this is not a failure of bootstrap; it shows why bootstrap
probably has to be a coordinated global rule rather than one density number.

We also proved that merely finding a self-consistent universe does not reveal
the rule that selected it. Different rules can have the same final solution
but react differently when the geometry is nudged. That missing reaction law
is precisely what the complete action has lacked.

The next task is therefore not to guess an action or tune a density. It is to
inventory the global quantities the complete metric genuinely defines and see
whether Reciprocity, the finite cell, and bootstrap force a relationship among
them. If they do, that relationship may be the missing joint between the global
universe and local matter geometry.
