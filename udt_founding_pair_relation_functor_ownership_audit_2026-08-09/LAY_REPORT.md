# Lay report — what “ordered observer comparison” really gives us

The founding idea gives us a perfect rule for reading a tape **after the comparison is set up**.
It says how the clock side and ruler side change reciprocally, how reversing A and B reverses the
answer, and how matched comparisons add.

What it does not say, in the old written derivation, is exactly how A lays that tape through the
complete geometry to B. Two observers can be the same two observers while we pair different moments
on their clocks or evolve A's ruler in different ways. `c_E` keeps every tape in the same real
units, but it does not choose which of those tapes is the physical one.

The good news is that this is now a much smaller gap than “we need a new equation for phi.” If an
observer comparison is understood operationally to include the paired events, A's clock and ruler
germ, and the branch being used, then the metric fills in the local geometry. The reciprocal
formula then tells us where B lies on that complete, orchestra-modulated tape. That is a legitimate
conditional interpretation of the founding phrase, not a new force or mechanism.

What remains is mainly a consistency joint: when A compares with B and B compares with C, we need a
rule proving that B hands over the same calibrated tape state rather than silently rebuilding it
differently. We also need honest branch handling where geometry supplies several paths.

So the result is neither “everything was already derived” nor “we are missing phi.” It is:

```text
the reciprocal ruler is founded;
the complete local tape can be built once the full comparison query is declared;
the universal rule for matching those tapes across many observers is still open.
```
