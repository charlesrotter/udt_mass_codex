# G150 lay report

G149 showed that one real metric configuration can play the whole first-order chord. G150 asks
whether the instrument itself forces those notes to have a fixed relative volume.

The answer is no—not at first order, and not if every smooth regular measuring relation is allowed.
We constructed a smooth local pair relation that can supply any requested combination of four readouts:
depth change, radial frame tilt, and two sideways screen turns. The construction works at any finite
pair depth.

That means another large survey of first-order examples would be circular. The metric tells us how
to assemble and read the chord, but local first-order consistency alone does not compose the melody.

This does not mean UDT has no melody, and other first-order quantities remain. It tells us that
these four volumes are not locally selected. Any further restraint must enter through a smaller
physical query class, the next pair-frame jet and metric curvature/Jacobi structure, or global
completion/dynamics. The next move is one minimal curvature/next-jet join, not another first-order atlas.
