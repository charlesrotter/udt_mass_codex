# G281 lay report

The short answer is: **the old supernova work was a mixture, not one thing.**

UDT genuinely gives a redshift from reciprocal `phi`. It also genuinely tells us how to calculate
the apparent area of a light bundle once a complete metric history and viewing path are supplied.
Those parts survived the audit.

What UDT has not yet supplied is the unique curve telling us how areal/optical distance changes
with `phi` throughout the universe. Older work quietly filled that gap with a polynomial profile,
P1, or a curve reconstructed from the SNe themselves. One early headline fit also used the wrong
number of redshift factors in the luminosity formula.

So:

- the old spectacular fit was scaffolded and cannot count as a prediction;
- P1 was a useful fitted distance profile, not part of the native kernel;
- the recent two-dataset reconstruction and DES holdout are honest empirical evidence, but still
  not a native prediction;
- the present reciprocal kernel remains native and was not retuned by the recent calibration.

The missing piece is now narrow: derive the metric-owned relation between reciprocal depth and the
light bundle's apparent area. Then freeze it before looking at SNe and test both data sets. That is
the point where SNe can become a prediction test rather than a way of drawing the curve.
