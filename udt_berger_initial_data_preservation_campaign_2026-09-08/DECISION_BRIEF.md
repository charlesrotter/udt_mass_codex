## What this adds

The useful distinction is between preserving a geometric family of circles,
preserving a metric relationship on their quotient, and preserving the selected
direction under more general lawful initial data. These are different questions.
The campaign does not transfer the older conditional carrier's finite-box
stability to the metric, or introduce another action to make that transfer work.

1. **BI1: the homogeneous initial-data class is broader than the old recipe.**
   On a fixed nonround Berger geometry, the original vacuum constraints allow
   arbitrary horizontal shear within one explicit quadratic constraint. Pure
   trace and horizontal isotropy are not necessities. Every such homogeneous
   datum initially leaves both the Ricci line and its orthogonal projector
   stationary. For general smooth data those are distinct conditions.
2. **BI2: circles and direct metric descent have different requirements.**
   The entire lawful homogeneous class has an actual local vacuum development
   preserving the original isolated line, the same closed Hopf circles and the
   declared normalized integral's magnitude1. But the unaveraged horizontal
   metric descends directly to their circle quotient on a time interval iff
   the initial horizontal shear is zero. With shear, this stronger relationship
   fails immediately although the circles persist. Another supplied quotient
   metric is not ruled out. The original spectral branch must be continued;
   later metrics can have three simple eigenlines.
3. **BI3: local lawful spatial variation can move the selected direction.**
   Actual analytic initial fields on an open patch can match a pure-trace
   control in value and first spatial derivatives at one point, yet give a
   nonzero initial normal change of the Ricci line. All four nonlinear vacuum
   constraints hold throughout that patch. This is stronger than proposing a
   formal jet, but weaker than constructing global data on the whole S3.
