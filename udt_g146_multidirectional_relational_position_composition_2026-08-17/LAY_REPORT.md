# G146 lay report

The straight-line part of the music is settled: when observers separate along one direction, their
bounded positions combine by the reciprocal Mobius rule.

But that does not tell us what happens when one separation points east and the next points north.
We found two mathematically clean position projections extending the same straight-line rule into
three dimensions. They agree perfectly whenever all observers lie on one line, but they give
different answers for a right-angle turn.

That means the radial melody alone does not write the full chord.

There is also an encouraging structural clue. In one exact control, combining two differently
directed position changes automatically leaves a rotation behind. UDT already has a separate
angular/screen-transport instrument. A bare position arrow also cannot reverse a composed
right-angle turn correctly without keeping additional frame information.

The promising next question is therefore whether the two-dimensional directional screen around a
position can be identified, by the complete metric itself, with UDT's path-transport screen. Only
after that common carrier is derived can we test whether the two rotations are actually the same.

If they match by derivation, position and angular transport become one multidirectional reciprocal
law. If they do not, we have learned that the scalar position law and path angular memory remain
distinct instruments.

Nothing here chooses a universe, a proper-distance profile, or a value of `X_max`. It identifies a
smaller and more geometric missing joint.
