# G226 lay report

The pieces have clicked together more tightly.

Along each supplied light-like relation, the metric carries four pieces of angular information:
two describing where a tiny neighboring ray is on the observer's screen, and two describing which
way it is moving. Keeping all four matters. At a focus, the first two can collapse even though the
full four-part state remains perfectly reversible.

The surprising exact result is that the observers' clock ratio tells us how the natural area of
this four-part state is rescaled. Its reciprocal is precisely the ruler carry already found in
G224. So the clock/ruler kernel and the angular/Jacobi machinery are not separate additions; they
are two faces of one matrix law.

At the middle observer, the metric's least-turning screen comparison rotates both position and
direction together. The choice of screen axes cancels out. What remains is genuine route memory:
different sequences of sky directions or different curved edges can leave different rotations,
focusing, and shear.

What G226 does **not** do is choose which chain is physically realized. It assembles the metric's
answer correctly for a supplied chain. It adds no fitted coefficient, `X_max`, source model, or
light-transfer assumption.
