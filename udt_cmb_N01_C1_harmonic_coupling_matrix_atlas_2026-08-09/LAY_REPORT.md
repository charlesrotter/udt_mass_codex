# Lay report

We took the simplest full rotating angular geometry and asked which notes in its angular orchestra
actually interact.

When rotation/mixing is off, every spherical note remains independent. For rotating labels, two
angular pieces are separately mixed but cancel in exactly the right way; for the nonrotating label,
one piece vanishes and the other is already diagonal. Looking at only one piece in the rotating case
would falsely suggest mixing. This is a concrete example of the “orchestra” idea—the complete
combination matters.

As mixing turns on, notes of the same north/south symmetry begin to couple. The first step couples
nearest same-symmetry neighbors. In the finite atlas, stronger mixing passes the interaction through
more neighbors. The exact complete-basis operators have no finite cutoff in that coupling. The
rotation label still survives because this chosen control has an axis; a general angular geometry
need not keep it.

We have not computed the resonant notes themselves. We now have the correct wiring diagram for that
future calculation, including the part that changes as one moves through the radial geometry.
