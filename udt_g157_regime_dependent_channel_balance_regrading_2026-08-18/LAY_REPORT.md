# G157 lay report

We were partly asking the orchestra to behave like a marching band.

The actual metric mathematics does not require every instrument to maintain the same relative
volume. It says something subtler: when one observer comparison is followed by another, the whole
score must combine consistently. Common scale and reciprocal depth add, while reciprocal depth
changes how the shift channel accumulates.

That permits these clock/ruler/shift instruments to be loud in one regime, quiet in another, and loud again elsewhere,
provided all of those values belong to one coherent metric history. No extra selector is needed
merely to make the ratios equal—because equal ratios were never required.

What remains missing is not permission for the volumes to change. It is the equation or legitimate
data that tells us what score the realized history actually plays, plus a physical carry when two
query sheets are genuinely unrelated.

So the mathematics was mostly healthier than our repeated “which one gets selected?” language.
This is a conceptual correction, not a demolition of the recent derivations.

One honest boundary remains: this proof covers the three-channel triangular core. The screen and
mixing instruments are live upstream, but their complete composition score still needs its own
bounded derivation.
