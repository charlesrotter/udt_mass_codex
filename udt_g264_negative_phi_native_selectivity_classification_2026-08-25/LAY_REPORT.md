# G264 lay report

Simply being on the negative side of `phi` does not force the metric into trouble. We found a whole
smooth family that dips as deeply negative as we like, then returns to the quiet metric at large
distance. Every finite member stays geometrically regular.

But if `phi` keeps falling without bound, the *rate* at which it falls matters. There is a precise
threshold. Below it the far end becomes geometrically quieter; above it curvature and the force
felt by stationary observers blow up. Right at the threshold they settle to finite nonzero values.

The striking simplification is that this threshold case is not new scaffolding. It is exactly a
family we had already derived from the metric: the one where the two angular tidal channels cancel.

So negative regions are not selected merely by being negative. They become more informative when
we ask how the entire metric approaches a negative extreme. That narrows the possibilities, but it
does not yet say that Nature chooses the threshold family or that it is `X_max`.
