# G264 map — native selectivity of negative-phi regions

Date: 2026-08-25
Question type: `METRIC_LED`

## Whole question

On the complete primary static-spherical metric

\[
ds^2=-f(r)c_E^2dt^2+\frac{dr^2}{f(r)}+r^2d\Omega^2,
\qquad f=e^{-2\phi}>0,
\]

does the sign condition `phi<0`, equivalently `f>1`, natively remove more candidate metric
profiles than `phi>0`? Or does selectivity arise only from the magnitude and radial jets of a
supplied profile through curvature, center, asymptotic, or completeness behavior?

The task separates four logically different gates:

1. local Lorentzian regularity at finite `phi`;
2. smooth areal-center regularity;
3. asymptotic flatness and completeness of the static spatial slice;
4. asymptotic curvature and normalized-frame response classes.

It characterizes, but does not discard, every class. Spatial completeness, asymptotic flatness,
bounded curvature, and monotonicity are reported as conditional properties, not silently promoted
to founding physical laws.

## Exact witnesses

Test both:

- a smooth negative bump family
  `f=1+epsilon (r/L)^2 exp(-(r/L)^2)`, with `epsilon,L>0`;
- an asymptotic growth family `f~C r^alpha`, with `C>0` and `alpha>0`.

The first can refute sign-only selection. The second can reveal derivative/growth thresholds without
turning them into a history law.
