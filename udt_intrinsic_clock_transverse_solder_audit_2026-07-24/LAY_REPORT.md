# Lay report

We found a real connection, but it is not yet the connection we need.

The metric naturally pairs the length-and-time plane with the perpendicular
area plane. Think of two sides of the same oriented four-dimensional sheet:
the metric knows which area is the counterpart of the other.

But area alone forgets how much clock dilation occurred. The reciprocal
clock transformation expands one channel exactly as much as it contracts
the other, so its total area never changes. The attractive Hodge relation
therefore cannot carry the actual clock-depth information.

To connect the clock law to detailed angular focusing, the metric would
first have to pick one direction in the otherwise rotatable screen. It has
not done that. If such a direction is supplied and remains consistent along
the path, the math gives a very sharp condition: its transverse curvature
must be negative and equal in magnitude to the square of the clock-dilation
rate.

That condition is interesting because it is not invented—it falls directly
out of asking whether the two exact transport laws are the same kind of
transformation.

Neither current major branch passes it. The complete round branch has
positive transverse curvature and no clock dilation. WR-L has clock
dilation and a genuine local relationship between clock and angular size,
but its transverse curvature is also positive, so their full transport
rules are different.

In short: the metric has shown us what a genuine solder would have to look
like. It has not yet shown us a complete universe branch that contains one.
The honest clock-plus-angular object remains the two exact rules travelling
side by side.
