# Lay report — what the screen rotation can and cannot do

The free angular rotation is harmless to the reciprocal clock/distance number, once the supplied
clock/ruler pair uses the declared common calibration.

Imagine that observers carry a clock, a ruler, and a round compass face. The metric does not mark a
preferred zero-degree point on the compass face. We have now checked the whole rotation of that
face. Turning it changes how angular components are written, including visible mixing components,
but it does not change:

- the clock stretching;
- the clock/ruler area stretching;
- their reciprocal imbalance; or
- the final clock-versus-ruler number read from the supplied pair geometry.

The arbitrary rotation also cancels exactly when observer A hands the geometry to B and B hands it
to C. It does not erase the route they took; different paths may still have real holonomy.

The important limitation is equally clean. This angular handoff is an isometry: it preserves all
lengths and areas. It can carry a dilation amount that was already present, but it cannot create
that amount. We have removed the screen-orientation ambiguity from the scalar problem; we have not
yet derived what fixes the physical amount of positional dilation. The cold reviewer also caught
an important bookkeeping point: comparing the final determinant formula with the reciprocal number
requires stating how the starting clock and ruler were normalized. The result survives because the
audited pair uses that normalization; it is now explicit instead of hidden.
