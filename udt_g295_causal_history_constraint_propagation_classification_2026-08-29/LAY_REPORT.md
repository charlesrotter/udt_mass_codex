# G295 lay report

The hole is smaller than it looked.

We had been speaking as though UDT needed two additional machines:

1. a rule tying the whole observer network together; and
2. another rule moving it forward without breaking causality.

G295 shows that these can be two views of one underlying spacetime rule. When physicists cut a
complete spacetime into successive “moments,” one part of that rule appears as constraints on each
moment and another part appears as evolution. The cut creates the two-part description; Nature does
not necessarily contain two separate mechanisms.

There are three important limits to that simplification:

- We still do not know the actual rule.
- Merely forcing the whole network to agree instantaneously can transmit an off-cone response and
  is therefore not acceptable.
- A rule normally defines a family of possible universes. Starting or boundary conditions then
  identify one member. It was too strong to expect the founding algebra by itself to print the one
  complete history of our universe.

The cleaner target is now:

> one metric-native rule defining allowable complete relational spacetimes, whose observable
> responses remain inside the metric's causal cones.

When written using a time slicing, that one rule must both preserve its constraints and update the
complete state—including angular and screen information. The scalar reciprocal depth by itself is
not enough.

This does not solve the field equation. It does tell us more precisely what we are looking for and
removes the unnecessary picture of two bolted-on mechanisms or a universal global “now.”
