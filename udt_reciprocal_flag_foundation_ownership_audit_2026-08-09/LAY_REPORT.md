# Lay report — what the flag audit found

The new formula is useful, but it is not the whole missing rule.

Think of UDT as starting with two matched calibration channels: a clock channel and a ruler channel.
The founding postulates already say that when one shrinks by a factor, the other grows by the
inverse factor. They also say that exchanging the two abstract roles reverses the sign of the
dilation. That part is solid.

The new flag formula tells us how to read one number from a **supplied** complete comparison. It
watches how a chosen clock direction and its clock–ruler plane change together. It composes exactly,
and it can see some angular mixing. In its carefully limited mathematical class, it is the unique
balanced clock-versus-ruler readout.

But the metric has not yet told us which clock direction, which ruler direction, and which complete
comparison map nature uses for every pair of observers. Ordinary geometry transport cannot solve
that: it carries rulers without changing their calibrated lengths, so it reports zero UDT dilation.
We can manufacture other transports that do change lengths, but there are infinitely many, so
choosing one would be inventing the missing law.

The clean new picture is:

```text
founded UDT gives the clock/ruler calibration grammar;
the flag formula gives a very good conditional meter;
the missing joint tells the complete metric how to realize that grammar for an actual observer pair.
```

This is narrower than where we started. We are no longer searching for an arbitrary scalar hidden
inside every four-by-four matrix. We are looking for the metric-native solder between UDT's abstract
reciprocal calibration pair and the complete observer-pair geometry. It can be pair-relative, so it
does not introduce a preferred observer throughout the universe.

The existing `X_max` and pure reciprocal `c_eff` ideas are not overturned. They remain required
reductions or gates for whatever complete realization eventually survives.
