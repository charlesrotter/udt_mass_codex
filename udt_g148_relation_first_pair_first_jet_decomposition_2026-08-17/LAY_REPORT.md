# G148 lay report

For this calculation we provisionally use the position ball as a way to draw the completed pair
relation. That lets us proceed without deciding whether some independently existing carrier also
exists; it does not settle that larger question.

Inside that working representation, the covariant identity has a simple three-part chord when the
relation changes:

1. the observers move farther apart or closer together along their ruler direction;
2. their direction can turn sideways across the metric's own two-dimensional screen;
3. the observer's instantaneous notion of “space right now” can tilt as the clock frame changes.

These are not three corrections added to a radial formula. They are the exact three orthogonal
pieces of one metric-derived vector change.

The representation's coefficient functions require no fitted volume controls:

- radial position change carries `sech(phi)^2`;
- sideways turning and frame tilt carry `tanh(phi)`;
- reciprocal clock/ruler dilation carries `exp(-phi)` and `exp(+phi)`.

Near `phi=0`, the sideways coefficient tends to zero. Near either asymptotic end, the radial
coefficient tends to zero, the directional coefficient tends to unit magnitude, and reciprocal
dilation becomes extreme. Those are coefficient limits, not yet physical loudness: the multiplying
first jets could vanish, remain finite, or grow.

But it is not yet the whole symphony. The actual notes—how quickly `phi` changes, how fast the ruler
turns, and how the rest space tilts—come from the complete time-live metric history. G148 tells us
how the metric combines them, not which numerical history Nature realizes.
