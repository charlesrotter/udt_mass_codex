## Argument rechecked in this context

Let P and H hold and put `k = F(1)`.

1. Repeated addition gives `F(n)=n*k` for positive integers. Zero and negative
   integers follow from the admitted zero value and oddness (also consequences
   of H). More generally, `F(n*t)=n*F(t)` for positive integer `n` and real `t`.
2. For any integer `m` and positive integer `n`, apply this identity at `t=m/n`:
   `n*F(m/n)=F(m)=m*k`. Thus `F(q)=q*k` for every rational `q`.
3. For any real `x`, choose rational `q_j -> x`. Continuity gives
   `F(x)=lim F(q_j)=lim k*q_j=k*x`. This step uses the admitted continuity;
   the conclusion is not asserted for arbitrary discontinuous additive functions.
4. Conversely, for every real `k`, `F(x)=k*x` is continuous, odd, zero at zero,
   and satisfies H for all real `x,y`. Thus the classification is exact within P
   and H, with no restriction on `k`. Zero and negative slopes remain allowed;
   no normalization, positivity, injectivity or monotonic increase is supplied.

Under P alone, `F(x)=x^3` is continuous, odd and zero at zero. It violates H at
`x=y=1`, because `F(2)=8` while `F(1)+F(1)=2`, and is not of the form `k*x` on
all of R. Hence H imposes a proper restriction relative to P. This counterexample
refutes an inference of universal linearity from P; it does not refute H as an
additional assumption or provide empirical evidence against its physical use.
