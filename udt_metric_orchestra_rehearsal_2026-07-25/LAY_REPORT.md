# Lay readout

Yes—we have enough identified geometric instruments to put them on one stage
and hear whether they interact.  In this bounded rehearsal, they do.

The simplest measurement, scalar curvature, is like placing one microphone in
the hall.  It hears the clock/ruler field, angular size, angular shape, and two
connection curls, but many contributions cancel in that single mixed signal.
That made earlier work look more disconnected than the metric actually is.

When we retain all ten components of the Ricci curvature tensor in the declared
coframe chart—more like using ten directional microphones—the hidden ensemble
appears.  The clock/ruler field talks directly to angular area and shape.
Angular area and shape talk directly to the four connection components.  Taken
together, all eight instruments belong to one connected response network in
this chart and local regime.  This was reproduced by a second calculation that
did not use the symbolic derivation code.

That is a real advance, but it is not yet the finished symphony.  We have found
the metric's wiring diagram, not the law that chooses what each instrument
plays.  Nothing in this audit selects the universe's profiles, matter, density,
boundary behavior, action, or a bootstrap fixed point.  It also does not prove
that the seven coframe-completion directions beyond `phi` are physical fields.

The clean next question is no longer “do these sectors interact?” They do,
within the tested complete-coframe domain.  The next metric-led question is
whether the *nonlinear* Cartan curvature and its geometric identities preserve
this connected ensemble throughout a finite cell, or impose exact relations
that reduce its freedom.  That should be mapped before any density tuning or
GPU solution search.
