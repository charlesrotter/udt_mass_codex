# G145 lay report

We were treating “the network of observer relationships” and “the universe’s metric history” as if
they might be two separate machines needing a coupling between them. They are not separate once the
network is complete.

Think of a building reconstructed from enough overlapping architectural scans. A few scans leave
hidden rooms. A sufficiently rich, mutually consistent set reconstructs the whole building. At that
point there is no second building behind the scans waiting to be selected: the complete scan network
already is a faithful description of it.

UDT now has the same result for its regular observer-pair geometry. The orchestra forms each full
pair response. Enough pair directions recover the complete metric, and genuine overlaps provide
their own translations. Reciprocal position and causality say how those relations must fit together.

But these rules do not choose the notes. Many different smooth reciprocal profiles fit together
perfectly. We exhibited two with opposite curvature signs at the same marked observer, and the
ambiguity survives when all angular and mixing instruments are active.

So this is a simplification, not another failure: no second mechanism is needed to translate a
complete set of pair pullbacks into the metric they encode. But the observer-query atlas,
calibrations, numerical values, and physical realization are still supplied or open. What remains
is to learn what supplies those actual values—possibly global completion/initial data,
observations, or a still-undiscovered metric-native law. The present calculation does not decide
which.
