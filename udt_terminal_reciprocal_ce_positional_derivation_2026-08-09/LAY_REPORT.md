# Lay report — what the derivation says

The new idea was right in one important sense: we had been looking at the problem backward.

Imagine observer A lays out a clock-and-ruler grid. The clock side is converted into distance units
using ordinary measured `c_E`. The **whole metric** then deforms that grid on the way to observer B.
Angular geometry, twisting, mixing, and the large-scale background are allowed to affect the grid.

At B, the grid has three separate features:

1. an overall enlargement or shrinkage;
2. a sideways slant; and
3. an imbalance between the clock direction and ruler direction.

That third feature is the candidate UDT positional dilation. Once the grid and its physical
calibration are fixed, there is one and only one reciprocal log-imbalance in this decomposition:
compare the ruler stretch with the clock stretch logarithmically. That number is the pair
coordinate proposed for relational `phi_AB`.

So this result supports a cleaner picture than a mysterious fluid painted onto space: `phi_AB` can
be the final score of how a supplied complete A-to-B pair geometry has unbalanced the reciprocal
clock/ruler pair. Proving that the metric itself selects that physical pair geometry remains open.

This also explains the “orchestra.” The angular sector does not play a separate tune that gets
added to radial `phi` afterward. All instruments alter the full A-to-B grid first; the reciprocal
`c_E` rule reads one final `phi` from the result.

What is still missing is not the algebraic formula for reading a fixed tape. We now have that.
What remains is the universal rule telling us exactly which A-to-B comparison surface—the physical
tape—and calibration the theory uses in every dynamical and global situation. Locally, the metric
can build conditional candidate tapes once observer histories and event pairing are supplied.
Globally, several tapes can coexist at a cut locus or in complicated topology.

In one sentence:

> We derived the reciprocal markings on a supplied calibrated ruler; we have not yet derived the
> universal rule that constructs and lays the physical ruler through every possible universe.
