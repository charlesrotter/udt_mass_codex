# G123 lay report

For the common-event question we deliberately asked, we found the direct observer-to-observer
object, and it is simpler than another hidden path.

Each observer builds a complete measurement chart: clock label, distance along a ray, and two sky
angles. Wherever A's chart and B's chart describe the same event, their direct relation is the
dictionary translating A's four labels into B's four labels.

On an ordinary patch that dictionary is a clean four-dimensional map. It reverses and composes
exactly. Its Jacobian can mix clock/ruler and angular entries, so the orchestra is already inside
the complete observer relation. Nothing is bolted onto a scalar afterward.

Where several regular rays reach the same event, the dictionary has several local branches. At a
vertex or caustic it can cease to be a smooth dictionary at all; there we retain the whole
incidence set without pretending G123 has classified its singular shape.

One distinction remains. The dictionary tells us how the event labels and their small changes
correspond. It does not automatically say that the two observers' light bundles have the same
directional slope/history at that event. That extra “beam momentum” is Jacobi phase. Matching it is
a source-boundary compatibility question, not part of common-event incidence alone.

So the direct map for this declared common-event query now exists without inventing a path. This
does not prove that co-presence universally means same-event incidence. The next job is to use this
chart transition to derive the finite-radius live relationship among pair depth, frequency depth,
shift, and the angular/phase channels—while keeping those readings distinct.
