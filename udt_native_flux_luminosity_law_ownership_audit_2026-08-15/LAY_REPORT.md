# Lay report — the brightness joint

We now know more precisely what the metric does and does not provide.

A fresh adversarial review independently rebuilt the key arithmetic and agreed with this boundary.

Imagine a source shining through a complicated moving system of lenses. The rebuilt UDT metric can
already tell us:

1. how the source clock compares with the observer clock;
2. how a narrow beam is stretched, squeezed, rotated, and focused; and
3. how the beam looks when the same geometric relation is read in reverse.

Those three pieces account for three powers of the redshift factor in the brightness calculation.
The angular orchestra and mixing are not outside corrections: they are already inside the beam-area
map.

One piece remains: what quantity is carried in that beam and conserved—or changed—between the two
observers. The metric supplies the road and the changing width of the road. It does not yet say
whether the cargo is conserved or how the receiving observer assigns energy to each unit of cargo.

If UDT eventually says that the carried amount is conserved and that its measured energy changes in
the same inverse ratio as frequency, then the familiar result follows exactly:

```text
d_L = (1+z)^2 d_A.
```

Nothing in the rebuilt geometry contradicts that relation. But the old claim that the metric alone
already forced it relied on an electromagnetic/current derivation that the current repository has
correctly demoted.

The gain is that the uncertainty is now one small, sharply typed joint: a radiative transfer product.
It is not an arbitrary missing cosmological profile and it is not another angular correction.

The next physics question is therefore concrete: does the complete coframe derive a conserved
radiative measure and its energy readout? If yes, the SNe luminosity law closes. If no, that
conservation/readout rule must be declared as a minimal observational or source premise before SNe
can be called a full UDT validation.
