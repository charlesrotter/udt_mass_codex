# Lay report

We asked whether the reciprocal clock/ruler rule can be carried consistently around an entire
finite universe.

The answer has two layers.

If we remember the observer, ruler direction, and path used for each comparison, every setting of
the unresolved sideways response `lambda` works consistently. Different paths may rotate the ruler
differently, but the bookkeeping knows they are different paths. Nothing breaks.

If instead we demand one answer at the destination that is completely independent of which path was
taken, the two complete `S3` examples become much stricter. Their geometry rotates spatial
directions in every possible way. The only reciprocal rule unchanged by all those rotations is
`lambda=1`: one behavior for time and the opposite behavior equally for all three spatial
directions.

That is interesting, but not yet a derivation of `lambda=1`. UDT began as a comparison between
observer frames, so it may naturally keep the path and observer labels. We have not proved that the
founding rule demands a single path-independent object at every location.

We also recovered the mirror special case `lambda=-1`: it singles out the ruler direction and treats
everything else oppositely. It is mathematically symmetric in one sense, but the complete `S3`
geometry does not preserve a ruler direction around loops, so it fails the path-independent test.

The practical gain is that the remaining question is no longer vague. We must decide from UDT’s
founding observer reciprocity whether comparisons are inherently path-labelled or must collapse to
one endpoint value. That conceptual ruling determines whether the new `lambda=1` result is merely a
conditional clue or an actual selector.
