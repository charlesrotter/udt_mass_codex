# Lay report — what the three ladders really mean

The old calculation was like studying three sets of notes on one circular slice through an
instrument. The corrected atlas accurately measured those notes on that slice. But adding the rest
of the instrument changes the acoustics: the extra angular direction changes the volume through
which the wave moves, and the radial and angular motions become coupled.

In the cleanest spherical version with rotation/mixing turned on, `m=-1,0,+1` remain honest labels
for three rotation classes. They are not three notes of one triplet. Each class contains a full
two-dimensional family of radial-and-polar shapes. With mixing turned off, ordinary spherical
multiplets return, but a multiplet is all the `m` values belonging to the same `ell`; the old
equal-index rows do not establish that common `ell`.

The broader UDT angular screen can also deform in ways that remove the rotational symmetry
altogether. Then `m` is not even a universal label. Current UDT structure has not selected the
spherical/axial version over those alternatives.

The metric can sort modes into symmetry drawers when a symmetry exists. It does not tell us how
much physical power to put in each drawer. That second operation needs a state, source, response,
or population law that UDT has not yet derived.

So the useful result is a clean boundary: the three ladders were not meaningless, but they were
slice-specific. We now know exactly what must replace them before any honest FD2 profile adjustment:
a selected complete angular geometry and its full coupled mode families, or a family-wide
characterization that openly carries the nonselection.
