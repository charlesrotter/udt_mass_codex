# Lay report

We found that the complete UDT-shaped metric can contain the clock and ruler structure in one piece,
without borrowing the missing part from a different example.

The test metric lives smoothly over a complete three-sphere. We gave it an uneven but globally
well-behaved `phi` pattern. Three curvature measurements act like three independent coordinates:
any symmetry of the metric must leave all three unchanged, so it cannot secretly slide, rotate, or
mix through space. The only surviving continuous symmetry is translation along the time direction.

That makes the time direction intrinsic rather than something selected by the coordinate label
`t`. Its changing metric length gives the observer-pair clock depth, and the metric's twist picks the
matching spatial ruler direction. Both occur in the same complete geometry.

The important limit is that we proved **availability**, not **choice**. We chose one legal smooth
pattern to show the metric can do this. We have not yet found the UDT law that says the universe must
choose that pattern, or any particular pattern. That selection/closure question is now the honest
next seam.
