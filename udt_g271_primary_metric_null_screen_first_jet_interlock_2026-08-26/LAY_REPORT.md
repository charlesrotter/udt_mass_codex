# G271 lay report

The new result is a genuine simplification.

Imagine the metric's reciprocal gradient as one push. A light ray can meet that push head-on or at
an angle. The head-on part becomes the familiar direct depth/redshift change. The sideways part
becomes the transported screen mismatch `W` that lowers the mutual-clock response below the ideal
`sech(delta)` ceiling.

So these are not two unrelated instruments needing separate volume knobs. Locally they are two
directions of the same metric effect:

```text
head-on component^2 + sideways component^2 = total reciprocal-gradient strength^2
```

For a radial ray the sideways part is exactly zero, which explains why the earlier radial kernel
gave `sech(delta)` exactly. Around a quiet region where the metric gradient is nearly zero, both
parts are quiet. For a nonradial ray in a changing metric, the sideways part turns on automatically.

What remains unfinished is the accumulated finite-distance result. To obtain that, the same metric
law must be integrated along a supplied light path through a supplied profile. G271 has found the
native local generator; it has not selected the universe, distance law, or `X_max`.
