# Lay report

The metric did give us more pieces—but it did not label one of them “use this as the law.”

The simplest picture is a compass. Before this audit we definitely had one arrow: `dphi`, the
local change in the already-known observer-to-observer clock depth. Once the complete stationary
metric is opened up, it also supplies a clock direction, a ruler direction, an angular-gradient
direction, and—when orientation is kept—the sideways angular direction. In a generic region those
four directions fill the whole four-dimensional local space.

That is real structure. It also explains why repeatedly searching for “the next obvious one-form”
could not close the theory: there is not just one candidate sitting there.

We proved this more strongly than a simple counting argument. There is a whole family of rules that
all reproduce the known clock-depth law when observers stay on one intrinsic clock/ruler leaf, but
differ when the comparison moves through the angular geometry. Every member composes correctly.
There is also a whole endpoint-only family, so demanding path independence does not remove the
ambiguity.

In the orchestra metaphor: the metric has identified the instruments and shown that they can play
together. It has not yet supplied the score telling us how loudly each instrument contributes to
the physical observer comparison.

The next missing object is therefore narrower and clearer. The smallest thing that could fix the
mixture is an explicit rule saying what a physical observer comparison actually measures. A full
on-shell consistency equation or a global completion/period condition could also fix it, but would
be a larger solution. The local stationary metric alone cannot do that job.
