## Source-first proof

Write P=log p and H(P)=log Q(exp P). Since every record tends to (1,1),
Q(1)=1 and continuity makes this logarithm legitimate near 0. The reviewed
IEC hand extraction gives

    P = -T L^2/2 + O(L^4),
    log q = 3P + 4P^2 + d4 L^4 + O(L^6),
    d4 = 2|V|^2 + <V,W>,

with T=g(R(n,U)U,n), V=R(n,U)U-Tn and W=R(n,U)n-TU.
These are source results, not re-derived geometric coefficients in this review.

If R is nonzero anywhere, some timelike electric Jacobi operator is nonzero.
Otherwise J_U X=R(X,U)U vanishes for every timelike U and every X: its quadratic
polynomial dependence extends the equality to every U. Polarization gives
R(X,Y)Z+R(X,Z)Y=0; antisymmetry and the first Bianchi identity then give
3R(X,Y)Z=0. Thus all electric operators zero would imply R=0.

At a nonzero electric operator, its restriction to U-perp is Euclidean
self-adjoint. Choose a unit eigenvector n with nonzero eigenvalue T. Then V=0,
d4=0 and P has nonzero quadratic coefficient. In
H(P)=h1 P+h2 P^2+o(P^2), comparison forces h1=3 and h2=4. This fixes two
derivatives of the common H, not its exact whole function from a Taylor jet.

For every other event/frame/direction, the same Taylor expansion now gives
d4=0, including T=0 directions: if T=0, P=O(L^4), so the H-3P contribution
is O(L^8) and the conclusion follows directly. Equivalently the exact smooth
Taylor remainder is o(L^4) uniformly only along each fixed preparation; no
uniform family bound is required.

Opposite physical directions have d4(n)+d4(-n)=4|V|^2. Hence V=0 for every
unit spatial n and every future U. Every vector of U-perp is therefore an
eigenvector of the linear self-adjoint J_U, forcing J_U=lambda(U) I there.
One direction with d4=0 would not suffice, and a sixth-order test is unnecessary.

Here is an algebraic proof of the remaining all-frame step, without a Lorentzian
symmetric-space classification. Extend from normalized future timelike U to
all timelike U by scaling and U->-U. Tracing gives lambda_U=Ric(U,U)/3 for
the unnormalized operator on U-perp. Consequently, for every timelike U,

    g(U,U) R(X,U)U
       = Ric(U,U)/3 * (g(U,U)X-g(X,U)U).

Both sides are polynomial in U, so the identity holds for null U as well.
For a nonzero null U and X with g(X,U)!=0 it says Ric(U,U)=0. A symmetric
bilinear form S that vanishes on this Lorentz null cone is c g: in an
orthonormal basis evaluate S(e0+v,e0+v)=0 for every unit spatial v and also -v.
The odd part kills S0i; the even part makes the spatial block -S00 I. Hence
Ric=c g. Substitution in the timelike identity gives

    R(X,U)U = (c/3)(g(U,U)X-g(X,U)U).

Extend polynomially to every U, subtract the constant-curvature tensor, and
apply the same polarization/Bianchi argument. This proves R=K(g wedge g) with
K=c/3 at each event, including points whose curvature might initially vanish.
Since nabla R=0 and nabla g=0, scalar curvature 12K is parallel; connectedness
makes K constant. This handles flat and degenerate sections without dividing
by T on those sections.
