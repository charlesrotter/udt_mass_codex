# G81 in lay language

G80 showed that, for one straight radial line of sight, running the geometric comparison backward
returned the same lensing information in the mathematically required reversed form.

G81 made that test harder in two ways. First, one ray travelled through all three spatial directions,
so the angular and mixing parts of the metric genuinely participated. Second, we deliberately used
different rotated coordinate grids at the two ends, like turning the graph paper independently at
the source and receiver.

The answer transformed exactly as one geometric object should. The nonradial case developed real
cross-coupling between its two screen directions, so this was not secretly the old diagonal radial
test. A separately written calculation reconstructed the same result by firing nearby rays instead
of integrating the production Jacobi equation.

What improved is confidence that the reverse-pair relation is a genuine screen-covariant geometric
law, not an artifact of one radial direction or one convenient screen basis. What did not change is
the physical-selection problem: this test does not tell us which cosmic profile, endpoint, source,
or CMB observable nature uses.
