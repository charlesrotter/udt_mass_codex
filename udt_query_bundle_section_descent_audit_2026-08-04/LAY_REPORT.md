# Lay report

We found that the missing piece is not simply “choose one clock-and-ruler plane everywhere before
anything else can work.”

Think of the metric as a landscape and an observer pair as a movable viewing rig. Three kinds of
statement are possible:

- Some facts belong to the landscape itself: its metric and ordinary curvature. Moving the rig
  does not change them.
- Some rules belong to how a rig asks questions of the landscape. UDT's reciprocal clock/ruler
  comparison composes consistently for each properly labelled rig and path. It does not need one
  master rig bolted down everywhere.
- Some proposed physical structures—one screen, one angular mixing response, or one projected
  boundary direction—really do change when the rig changes. To call one of them a physical field,
  UDT must say which rig/reduction is realized, derive it from a particular metric branch, or supply
  another native rule.

The exact calculation makes this visible. Two equally valid observer/ruler pairs at the same point
give different reciprocal planes, different screen readouts, and different projected curvature,
even though the underlying metric has not changed. The reciprocal comparison law still composes
correctly for both.

So we removed a false all-or-nothing blockage. A native law could live directly on spacetime, or it
could be a genuinely observer-pair law. Only laws that treat the projected screen/plane as physical
need a selected or derived section.

The remaining “table leg” is a precise ownership decision already implicit in the theory: where
does UDT's native law live, and is the reciprocal plane a question observers ask, a physical field,
or something a completed metric branch derives? The audit does not answer that physics question;
it tells us exactly where the answer is needed.
