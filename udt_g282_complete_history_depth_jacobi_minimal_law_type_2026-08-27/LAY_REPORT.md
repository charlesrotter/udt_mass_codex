# G282 lay report

We have narrowed the missing piece considerably.

UDT's native kernel tells us how one completed observer pair dilates once its reciprocal state is
known. The complete metric also tells us how a bundle of neighboring light paths changes area once
the full metric history is known.

Those are connected parts of one metric, but one pair by itself does not determine the neighboring
bundle. G282 proved this with an exact example: two spacetimes can look identical along the central
light path—including the local metric and its first change—while nearby paths spread differently.
That means they produce different apparent areas and brightnesses.

So the remaining gap is not “add a luminosity correction” and not “fit another curve.” It is a rule
for how neighboring observer relations belong to one evolving metric. In the orchestra analogy,
we know how to read one chord very well; the missing score tells adjacent players how their notes
must change together.

The good news is that this is much narrower than an unspecified missing universe. The rule could be
written as a curvature law, an equivalent frame/connection law, or a global consistency law across
neighboring relations. G282 does not yet tell us which form is native, and it deliberately imports
none of them.

The reciprocal redshift, completed-pair kernel, angular interlock, and quiet GR regime all remain
intact.
