## 2. Full-metric same-ray witness

Consider the regular Brinkmann metric

\[
ds^2=-2\,du\,dv+dx^2+dy^2+A(x^2-y^2)\,du^2,
\qquad A>0.
\]

Compare it with the flat member obtained by setting \(A=0\), along the central null ray
\(x=y=0\). On that ray:

- the two metric matrices are identical;
- every first coordinate derivative of the metric agrees and vanishes;
- every Christoffel symbol agrees and vanishes;
- therefore the central affine transport and all data determined by the metric one-jet agree.

The transverse curvature does not agree. With the registered sign convention,

\[
R_{uxux}=-A,
\qquad
R_{uyuy}=+A.
\]

The trace is zero, so a scalar focusing trace alone would also miss the separator. The neighboring
screen directions nevertheless feel equal and opposite tidal curvature.

Write \(A=q^2\). For vertex data

\[
D_x(0)=D_y(0)=0,
\qquad
D_x'(0)=D_y'(0)=1,
\]

the exact screen-Jacobi solutions are

\[
D_x(u)=\frac{\sinh(qu)}{q},
\qquad
D_y(u)=\frac{\sin(qu)}{q}.
\]

Their area determinant is

\[
\det D(u)=\frac{\sin(qu)\sinh(qu)}{q^2}
=u^2-\frac{q^4u^6}{90}+O(u^9),
\]

whereas the flat determinant is exactly \(u^2\). Thus the central pair/path data through first
metric order do not determine the neighboring-ray area.

This is not a source or dynamical model. It is an exact local counterexample inside the complete
Lorentz-metric arena.

## 3. Primary-spherical witness

Let

\[
\phi_A(s)=s^2,
\qquad
\phi_B(s)=s^2+s^4,
\qquad s>0.
\]

At reciprocal depth \(\phi=2\),

\[
s_A=\sqrt 2,
\qquad
s_B=1.
\]

Both profiles therefore produce the same reciprocal state at their respective endpoints, while
placing that state at different areal positions. The primary metric form tells us how to evaluate
either profile; it does not turn a depth value into one unique areal location.

## 4. What the two witnesses prove together

The two failures are complementary:

1. endpoint/projective depth does not fix areal position even in the primary spherical family;
2. a complete central null relation through first metric order does not fix transverse Jacobi area
   in the complete metric arena.

Consequently, a law that closes a physical history must have value content for a *family of
neighboring relations*. It must constrain the optical tidal curvature or an equivalent object,
rather than only normalizing or composing one completed pair.

A conventional second-order metric PDE is one possible representation, but G282 does not derive
