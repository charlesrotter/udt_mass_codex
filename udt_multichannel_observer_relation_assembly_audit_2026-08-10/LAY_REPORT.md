# Lay report — what the orchestra audit found

The observer comparison is not naturally one dial.

Once a complete comparison surface is supplied, its metric gives three independent local readings:

- how the whole clock-and-ruler scale changes together;
- how the clock and ruler change oppositely—the familiar reciprocal `phi` reading; and
- how the clock direction leans into the ruler direction—the shift.

The angular sector contributes a fourth kind of information: how the transverse frame rotates as
the comparison follows a path. That is not another ordinary number. It behaves like an arrow or a
rotation and can remember which path was taken.

The important simplification is that the earlier forest of possible one-forms does not have to be
treated as a forest of new physical instruments. Most are alternate ways of combining or sampling
the established geometry. Without a measurement rule fixing their coefficients, they remain
possible microphone settings rather than new channels.

The metric also admits clean situations in which only one channel is active: pure reciprocal
dilation, common-scale change, pairing shift, or angular return. More general configurations can
activate several together. That makes regime-dependent behavior geometrically plausible, but the
audit has not yet identified those situations with microscopic, terrestrial, or cosmological
physics.

`c_E` tunes the ordinary clock/ruler measurement. `G_obs` and electron mass remain possible later
calibrations. None of them conducts the orchestra or chooses the score in this audit.
