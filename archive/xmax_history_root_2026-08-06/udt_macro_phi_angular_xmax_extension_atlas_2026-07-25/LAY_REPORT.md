# Lay readout

Your intuition was substantially right, with one important distinction.

The clock rule itself stays simple: `phi` still controls the reciprocal clock
and ruler pair exponentially. The angular part of the metric does not rewrite
that rule.

What the angular sector can change is how that clock depth becomes a physical
separation between observers:

1. If the depth direction and the angular geometry are perfectly aligned,
   the angular sector does not alter the local distance-per-unit-depth. But it
   can still make the whole finite cell wider, narrower, or directionally
   uneven, changing the largest observer-pair distance.
2. If the depth surfaces tilt or vary through the angular directions, the
   angular geometry enters the local distance conversion itself. Different
   directions on the same clock-depth surface can then have different physical
   separations.

So the macro picture is not “the angular sector changes what `phi` means.” It
is “the angular sector can modulate how the founded `phi` clock law is embedded
in the complete universe.” That gives a clean mathematical route to small
directional variations near a large-distance limit without changing the basic
clock law.

What is still missing is the global rule that chooses one complete angular
extension, one finite-cell completion, and one observer-pair comparison. Until
that is derived, the metric shows the available modulation mechanism but does
not calculate a unique `Xmax` or a CMB pattern.
