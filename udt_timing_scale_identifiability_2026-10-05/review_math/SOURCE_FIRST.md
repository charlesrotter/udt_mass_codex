## Derivative theorem and its hypotheses

Use receiver proper length ell and x=1/R. On CPR1's strict limiting actual
incidence, b(x),t_e(x) are smooth at x=0 by the smooth finite inverse-radius
integrals and a nonzero incidence Jacobian. Write w(x)=x v=
[sqrt(H^2+(E^2-1)x^2+2m x^3)]. Then

    dx/dell=-x w(x),                 w(0)=H,
    A=x a0(x),                      a0(0)=S_*/H>0,
    Z=z(x)/x,                       z(0)=H(1-Omega b_*)/[sqrt(h) S_*]>0.

Here a0,z are smooth along the actual incidence; in particular b is not frozen
between emissions. Direct differentiation gives

    d log Z/dell=w(x)[1-x z'(x)/z(x)]=H+O(x),
    lim d log Z/dtau_seconds=c_E H.

This does not require b_*=0, a source size, an angular-area endpoint1/H, or a
noncaustic optical size inverse. The strict ray/incidence hypotheses do remain.
Derivative control is essential: Z=CR[1+sin(R^2)/R] has Z/(CR)->1 but does not
have the asserted drift limit under dR/dell~HR. It is excluded by the actual
smooth x-tail, not by the leading equivalence alone.

The existing product Z(tau_e,*-tau_e)->1/(c_E H) offers another ideal timing
inversion if calibrated source proper timestamps and their limiting endpoint
are available. It does not make the endpoint finite in receiver proper time.

## What the records must mean

An ideal function Z(tau_o) with calibrated receiver seconds identifies H in
this conditional family. Received frequency nu_o=nu_e/Z gives the same rate as
-lim dlog(nu_o)/dtau_o if nu_e is an unknown positive constant. More generally,
positive C1 nu_e extending to a finite nonzero endpoint has

    dlog(nu_e)/dtau_o=(dlog(nu_e)/dtau_e)/Z ->0.

Thus an unknown constant amplitude is harmless; arbitrary source variability
is not. For example nu_e proportional to (tau_e,*-tau_e)^p alters the asymptotic
received log drift by the factor p+1. Without a restriction on intrinsic cadence,
one can prescribe nu_e(tau_e) to fit any positive received-frequency record
along a given regular arrival map. Ordinary source proper clocks do not by
themselves specify the frequency of emitted radiation or encoded pulses.

The finite emission endpoint is a genuine sampling obstruction. Fixed positive
source tick spacing gives only finitely many ticks in every finite source-time
window before tau_e,*. In general its last pre-endpoint tick arrives at finite
receiver time. An infinite ideal late tail of instantaneous clock ratios or
arbitrarily fine source timestamps is an additional record assumption, not a
consequence of a periodic ordinary source. No finite noise/precision/cadence
bound, required observation baseline or numerical error estimator is proved by
the limit. A finite sampled time series does not establish its own asymptote.

If receiver recorded time has unknown multiplicative calibration kappa,
t_read=kappa tau_seconds, the recovered drift is c_E H/kappa; an additive
origin shift is harmless. Identifiability therefore needs the stated receiver
clock calibration and physical branch attachment, not just a time label.

## Angular information and the exact scale ambiguity

A radial orthonormal receiver vector in outgoing coordinates is
n=(-1/(E+v),E,0,0), orthogonal to u_o. The propagation direction has components

    sin(alpha)=b/(R A),
    cos(alpha)=[s/(E+v)-v b^2/(R^2(1+s))]/A.

These use a declared receiver radial frame; the incoming sky direction is the
negative of the propagation direction. They tend to

    sin(alpha_*)=H b_*/S_*, cos(alpha_*)=1/S_*,
    alpha_*=atan(H b_*).

The limiting angle fixes only Hb_* without an independently fixed b_*. It does
not itself determine H. Full dimensionless angular curves also retain the
homothety when times and all source lengths are co-scaled. Timed angular changes
can carry scale if their particular asymptotic order/coefficient and reference
frame are supplied, but a universal first-order angular-decay inversion has not
been assumed here: cancellation of a leading coefficient must be checked.

For lambda>0 the coordinate-identified homothety is
(m,a,R,b,t,ell)->lambda(m,a,R,b,t,ell), (H,Omega)->(H,Omega)/lambda,
with E,h,s and actual incidence angles unchanged. Z is unchanged at matched
events; J and source-screen sizes both scale by lambda, so observed image
angles are unchanged. But dlogZ/dtau_seconds scales by lambda^-1. Calibrated
timing is exactly a nonzero-weight datum of central R16; its ability to break
this family scale does not contradict the dimensionless-record degeneracy.
This is a conditional geometric comparison, not an adopted native UDT symmetry.

c_E converts a measured time to length and the measured drift to H; it does
not set the measured drift. c_E and G_obs alone retain R16's unit-covariance
obstruction. G_obs cannot add a geometric-mass relation m=G_obs M/c_E^2 without
an independent physical interface. Even if supplied, co-scaled M and m preserve
the scale ambiguity unless an independent mass or other dimensional datum is
held fixed. Recovering H alone does not identify m,a,E,b_* or the whole metric,
and the matched GR experiment retains exactly the same mathematical records.
