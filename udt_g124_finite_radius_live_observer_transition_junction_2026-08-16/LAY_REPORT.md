# G124 lay report

The new result says the finite-radius “music” is simpler than we thought.

The observer relation already has two scale readings:

- `phi_pair` tells how unequal the clock and ruler channels are;
- `kappa_pair` tells how their common measuring scale changes while affine ray distance is converted
  into physical spherical area.

The measured frequency comparison is exactly

```text
frequency depth = reciprocal imbalance - common expansion + source-clock motion.
```

So the angular/optical contribution was not another instrument that needed to be attached to
`phi`. It was `kappa`, an instrument already present in the complete pair metric. The source-clock
term is also not a fit coefficient; it exists only when the query supplies the source clock.

If the observer actively carries a changing sky instrument, that motion first changes both the
terminal clock reading and the source-relative clock reading. The two changes cancel only in their
properly matched final combination. We have not turned the angular sector off or erased it after
the fact.

This explains G116’s earlier local formula and extends it to any finite regular radius in the
declared central-spherical class. It also isolates a misleading infinity: when areal radius stops
being a good coordinate, `phi` and `kappa` share a divergent chart term that cancels. The turning
point alone proves neither that the actual frequency stays finite nor that it diverges. That must
be checked in the regular affine chart with the supplied clocks. The initial observer vertex and a
later spherical caustic also both have zero areal radius, but they are different strata: the first
owns normalized initial data, while the later caustic can retain nontrivial Jacobi-phase information.

What this does not do is choose the universe’s history. The metric history still determines how
loud `phi`, `kappa`, and source motion are at each distance. But the score connecting those
instruments is now exact and contains no adjustable mixture coefficient.
