# Lay report

The metric now gives us four pieces of one local measuring frame without borrowing them from
different models:

1. a clock direction;
2. a ruler direction;
3. the two-dimensional “viewing screen” perpendicular to both; and
4. the exact comparison of clock rates between two stationary observers.

That closes a real gap.  Previously we had a complete screen in one geometry and an interesting
clock in another.  Now one complete metric has both.

The surprise is what happens when we try to send the viewing direction straight along the ruler.
If the depth field changes sideways across the screen, that direction bends into the screen.  On
this twisted three-sphere, forbidding that bending everywhere would force the depth field to be
constant—erasing the very clock dilation we wanted.

In plain language: the clock sector and angular sector cannot remain independent when the universe
has nontrivial dilation.  The metric itself makes them interact during transport.  That looks more
like the sought “orchestra” than another invented mechanism.

It does not yet give the finished physics.  We still need to follow actual rays through the metric
and calculate how their image area grows.  That accumulated image area is what supernova brightness
uses.  It is not the same as the size of the local viewing screen at one point.

The next bounded task is therefore to follow this unavoidable mixing along actual metric geodesics,
compute the full image/Jacobi transport, and see whether any of the already registered branches
naturally produces the WR-L/SNe angular law.  No parameter should be fitted to make that happen.
