# G234 lay report

The good news is that the missing item is smaller and clearer than “which universe do we choose?”

In the spherical version of the UDT metric, the reciprocal kernel is already the rule that tells
two supplied observers how clocks, rulers, and the completed pair respond at a given depth. What
is not yet fixed is one curve: how reciprocal depth changes as the observers' areal separation
changes.

We had been describing three possible ways to close that gap. The whiteboard shows there are only
two real kinds:

1. the metric may obey an invariant condition that reduces the possible curves to a much smaller
   family;
2. the complete network of observer relations may obey a genuinely global harmony condition that
   ties distant parts of the curve together.

Turning on time, angular shape, mixing, shift, and volume is essential for the complete orchestra,
but it is not a third way of choosing the music. Those instruments add more possible notes unless
the metric itself makes them obey one of the two kinds of conditions above.

We already know several useful restrictions—quiet middle, smooth center, completeness, causality,
and overlap consistency. They make the allowed music more disciplined, but none yet determines the
melody. A very strong constant-curvature condition would do so, but UDT has not derived that
condition; adopting it would be scaffolding.

So the next step is not another higher-derivative calculation. It is to name one genuinely native
condition that throws out an otherwise valid comparison metric for a reason unrelated to the
answer we hope to see. Until such a condition is named, more instruments or more anchors can
describe and calibrate a supplied score, but cannot compose it.
