# OEV1 lay return

We now have usable observational constraints and a checked way to calculate
received ticking from a supplied geometry. We have not selected UDT's geometry.

The observation review compared three routes. Megamasers are the best immediate
distance lead in this limited comparison: their distance estimates do not start
from a Hubble redshift-distance rule. They still depend on a model of the source
disk, gravity and motion. Six published records are preserved with those
assumptions and separate uncertainty intervals. No cosmology or UDT curve was fit.
Redshift drift and supernova timing remain useful for different questions;
their measured quantities and source assumptions are not interchangeable.

The numerical work checked14 comparisons at three accuracy settings. It solves
the original clock/signal geometry on existing controls and compares received
frequency with the derivative of actual arrival times. A separate high-precision
implementation checked the saved results and caught deliberate corruptions.
This finite workload took seconds and did not justify a GPU campaign.

The next physical connection must be a concrete astronomical comparison:
one declared geometry with actual source/observer histories, distance and
spectral readouts, source/motion assumptions and uncertainties. It can then be
confronted with the preserved observations. Native evolution also needs an
admitted equation or constraint class and its initial/boundary data. More runs
of the same controls do not provide that relationship.

Stage4A is complete;4B returned the expressly permitted narrowed observable
constraints instead of a native-candidate likelihood. Stage5A is complete at
its supplied-control scope; the5B native-evolution gate remains open. No new
physical premise, preferred scale, kernel response curve or CANON was adopted.
Final reviews/checks and actual commit/remote verification own operational closure.
