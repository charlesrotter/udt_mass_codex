# G167 lay report

The main simplification is that we do not need to build a separate orchestra and attach it to the
UDT metric.

The primary metric already contains the clock, radial ruler, and two angular directions. When two
observers make a comparison, their comparison surface can cut through all four directions. The
metric simply measures that complete cut. If the comparison tilts or turns through the angular
directions, those directions change the one final clock/ruler ratio automatically. They can even
create a cross-term—the pair's version of a shift—although the underlying metric is diagonal.

In the orchestra metaphor, the metric already contains the instruments. The observer pair
determines which instruments are being played by specifying its direction and motion through the
metric. We had partly mistaken the most general possible list of instruments and controls for a
set of independent physical histories that UDT had to choose among.

There is an important limit. A perfectly central radial comparison does not lean into the angular
directions, so its scalar reciprocal depth is the original pure `phi` result. Its angular behavior
still matters for image size, focusing, and screen transport, but it is a separate output channel.
Angular modulation of the scalar depth appears for nonradial or rotating comparisons, or if a
future derivation shows that the ambient UDT metric itself must become nonspherical or mixed.

So this is a real narrowing:

- the complete pair calculation inside the primary spherical metric is now algebraically assembled;
- no extra scalar `mu` or after-the-fact correction is needed there;
- motion of the pair through the metric gives a live response without pretending that we derived a
  time-evolving universe;
- what remains is deciding/deriving the physical observer-pair realization and determining whether
  UDT needs a genuinely broader ambient metric outside this macro slice.

It is not yet an observational prediction or a complete UDT cosmology, but it removes another large
piece of scaffolding.
