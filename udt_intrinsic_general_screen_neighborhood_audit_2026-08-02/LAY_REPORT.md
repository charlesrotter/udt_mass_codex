# Lay report — what changed when the whole angular screen was allowed to play

The old exact example had a hidden restriction: the angular part of the metric was allowed to grow
or shrink only in lockstep with the depth field. It was like tying the volume knob of the angular
orchestra to the clock knob. Because the two knobs always moved together, their relative motion was
zero.

We untied them without adding new physics. We used the complete angular metric: one area knob and two
independent shape/shear knobs. Then we asked whether the full metric could still recognize its own
clock/ruler pair, rather than relying on labels we had supplied by hand.

It could, in explicit smooth whole-cell examples. The metric's curvature uniquely identified the
clock direction; the clock's twist identified the ruler direction. With that pair recovered, the
angular area could change independently of depth, producing a real nonzero geometric two-form. Both
shear knobs could be active at the same time.

So the useful advance is:

> the reciprocal backbone and a genuinely independent angular response can coexist inside the
> complete metric.

That removes one previous false obstruction. It does **not** tell us which angular pattern nature
chooses, what equation makes it evolve, or how it becomes matter. We found that the joint can exist;
we have not yet found the rule that uses it.

The strongest next geometric clue is the direction naturally defined by this new two-form wherever
it is nonzero. That direction might provide another intrinsic piece of the metric's structure, but
it must be mapped without assuming that it is a particle carrier or desired section.
