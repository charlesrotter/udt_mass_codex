# Lay report — what the metric owns at the carried/rebuilt seam

We found that the two endpoint structures are genuinely different:

- one is the clock/ruler/screen apparatus carried from observer A along a path;
- the other is the apparatus observer B reconstructs locally from B's metric.

The metric can always tell us the whole set of ways these two apparatuses line up on the six regular
branches we tested. It does **not** choose one unique way to rotate the two-dimensional screen while
lining them up. Every screen rotation leaves the clock, ruler, screen plane, and metric unchanged.

The cold review found that this is not a defect in the bridge. The whole rotating family is the
correct mathematical bridge. If observer A hands its apparatus to B and B hands it to C, the
otherwise-arbitrary screen rotation cancels between the two handoffs when the bookkeeping is done
consistently. We verified that cancellation exactly with two independent calculations.

So the projector-level “missing middle map” has dissolved into gauge bookkeeping. The free rotation
is like choosing where zero degrees lies on a circular dial: useful for coordinates, but not needed
to pass the clock/ruler/screen geometry from observer to observer.

What remains unknown is whether the *number* we ultimately read as reciprocal clock/distance depth,
and the calibration density that helps construct it, are also unchanged by that free rotation. The
next bounded audit tests exactly that. If they are unchanged, no preferred screen direction is
needed. If they are not, the missing information is not a rotation selector in the metric; it is
additional pair/calibration data.
