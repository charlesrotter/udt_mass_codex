# G149 lay report

We stopped moving the instruments by hand and built one small, smooth piece of an actual orchestra.

In G148, the formula said that the separation relation has three first-order voices: its depth can
change, its ruler direction can turn sideways, and its observer rest frame can tilt. But the test
that showed the complete metric and pair-query blocks were active used an abstract slider called
`lambda`.

G149 replaces that slider with one genuine local spacetime and one smooth two-observer measuring
surface. The metric itself now supplies the connection that says how directions change. The same
geometry produces all three voices, and when they are recombined they reproduce the direct change
of the pair-position vector exactly.

Every registered spacetime-gradient block and every pair-clock-direction query block was audible in
this witness. That is an important joining result: the formula is not merely an after-the-fact sum
of separate effects. The sideways `sigma`-direction first jet was not tested here.

What it does **not** yet tell us is which complete spacetime history Nature uses, how those voices
vary over cosmic distance, or whether their physical volumes follow the hoped-for loud--quiet--loud
pattern. We have assembled and tested one chord from one coherent instrument configuration. The
next useful step is to vary a smooth family of such histories without fitting observations and see
which parts of the chord are identities, which are genuine free data, and whether any simple
metric-native regime structure appears.
