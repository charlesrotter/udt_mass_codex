# Lay report

We asked whether the whole UDT clock–ruler–angular structure could be made globally rigid—carried
around curved space without remembering the route.

The metric gives a surprisingly simple answer.  The angular `S3` geometry constantly rotates a
singled-out ruler into the other two spatial directions.  There are only two ways to stop that:

1. destroy the angular coupling, which leaves the regular `S3` family; or
2. declare all three spatial directions equivalent.

The second option is mathematically regular.  It gives ordinary round `S3` space with a separate
time direction.  But it also forces `phi` constant, removes the twist that selected the ruler, and
makes the clock comparison trivial.  So it preserves “time versus space,” not UDT's nontrivial
clock-versus-reciprocal-ruler structure.

This suggests that requiring one global rigid UDT frame may be the wrong demand.  The local frames
and scalar clock law remain sound; the full orientation naturally remembers its route through
curvature.  The next question is whether the original observer-frame reciprocity ever required more
than that.
