# Lay report — the missing table leg became smaller

We did not find the complete missing table leg, but we found that most of its upper framework was
already implicit in the metric.

Imagine every observer carrying a clock direction, a ruler direction, and seven adjustable ways
that those two directions can interact with the angular part of the metric. Previously those seven
adjustments looked like chart-dependent knobs. We have now shown that they form one genuine
seven-dimensional geometric object. Changing the coordinate frame merely changes how its seven
components are written; it does not erase any of them.

The metric also gives us a mathematically clean way to carry this object along any chosen path.
So we now possess:

- the object being carried;
- the rule for changing its local coordinates; and
- the mathematical machinery for carrying it along a path.

What we still lack is the universe's rule choosing which observer/ruler pair and path are physical,
and how the seven instantaneous adjustments grow into one finite complete transformation.

The last point matters. Two machines can begin with exactly the same initial motion but bend onto
different trajectories one moment later. Algebraically, two coframe lifts have the same first
metric change and different second metric changes. The first derivative therefore does not tell us
the whole finite law.

So the new picture is:

> UDT already supplies the rack, the seven adjustment channels, and a mathematical conveyor for
> moving them. The missing piece is the native gearing that integrates those channels into the
> finite reciprocal transformation—and the rule saying which conveyor route represents an actual
> observer comparison.

This is genuine narrowing, not closure. It also explains why repeated local selector searches
stalled: they were trying to choose a finished instrument before the finite gearing law had been
derived.
