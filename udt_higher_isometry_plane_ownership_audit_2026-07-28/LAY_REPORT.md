# Lay report

We had found a reliable clock-and-ruler compass inside one chosen pair of symmetry directions. The
new question was whether the entire metric chooses that pair when the geometry contains more than
one possible circular ruler.

The firm answer is: the metric does not always choose one.

Think of the metric as a building containing one straight time rail and two circular staircases.
We tested every way of pairing a time-like rail with a staircase. Only the original pairing keeps
the exact reciprocal area if the building's dilation, staircase tilt, and staircase width are all
allowed to vary independently across the entire family of buildings. That tells us which pairing
is most structurally robust.

It does not yet prove what happens in a typical single building. In one fixed building those three
features vary together along one direction, and their effects can cancel. The first draft missed
that distinction; the adversarial review caught it.

The metric also permits a special, perfectly smooth building in which the two staircases are
geometrically equivalent. We constructed one with changing dilation—not merely a featureless
constant case. Both circular directions are globally free, and both make exactly the same
reciprocal ruler partnership with the clock. The metric cannot choose between them there.

Topology does not break the tie. A smooth toric `S3` has exactly two unoriented free circle choices,
analogous to a right-handed and a left-handed Hopf winding.

We also learned that looking at all three symmetry directions at once is not equivalent to testing
each two-direction partnership. The full three-way response mixes the directions, while the
pair-by-pair family identity still singles out the registered partnership. That is a useful
structural fact, not a contradiction.

So we found:

- across the whole freely varied family, the registered plane is the uniquely robust one;
- at least one exact smooth nonconstant-depth geometry has two equivalent reciprocal planes;
- at constant depth, the dilation part of the compass goes silent.

We have not proved that a typical fixed geometry has only one plane, nor completed every response
degeneracy. What remains missing is a native reason the realized universe occupies one branch
rather than another. We have not shown that the alternatives are macro and micro regimes, or that
one produces matter.
