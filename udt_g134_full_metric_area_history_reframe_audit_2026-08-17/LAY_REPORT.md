# G134 lay report

The area rule really does simplify the picture, but in a precise way.

Imagine every observer pair reports not only the size of its own little tile, but also how its tile
leans and overlaps every other tile. The complete collection is no longer a loose pile. If the
numbers are mutually consistent, they assemble into exactly one four-dimensional metric, including
its scale. Most arbitrary piles cannot do that.

That is the advance: the orchestra's complete chord relationships are another way of writing the
whole instrument—not an extra effect attached afterward.

But the rule still does not compose the symphony. Any regular Lorentz metric automatically has a
lawful area structure. The rule can tell whether supplied observer relations belong to one coherent
score and can reconstruct that score exactly. It does not yet tell us why Nature chose those
particular notes rather than another equally coherent score.

So the physical-history problem is narrower now. We do not need a selector *after* a complete
observer-area network has been assigned. The actual missing item, if it is missing, is upstream: a
native condition determining how that one complete metric/area field changes and closes globally.
