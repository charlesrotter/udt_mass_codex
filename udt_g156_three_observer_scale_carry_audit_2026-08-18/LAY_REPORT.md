# G156 lay report

We found an important piece that was already hiding inside the metric.

Every valid observer-pair surface comes with a natural little “scale ruler.” Mathematically it is
the square root of the surface's area measure. The number called \(\kappa\) is the logarithm of that
ruler in whatever calibrated coordinates we use. So we do not need to invent a new object to carry
common scale.

If we are also given a lawful way to carry one observer's pair-plane into another's, the change in
this scale ruler is simply the determinant of the complete comparison. For three observers, those
scale changes multiply exactly when the full comparisons compose.

There is one crucial limitation. Checking only the determinant is like checking whether three
shipping boxes have the right volumes. The boxes can have matching volumes while their contents
are twisted or sheared differently. So common-scale closure is a necessary check, but it does not
close the whole orchestra.

The good news is that the “missing scale carrier” was not missing. The remaining problems are more
specific: what physical comparison connects genuinely different observer-query sheets, and what
law determines how the metric's common scale changes across the universe? This audit derives
neither of those, but it removes one false missing part from the list.
