# G128 lay report

G127 showed, at one instant and one place, that the same curved metric can look perfectly round to
a radial ray but uneven to a tilted ray. G128 asks whether that was only a local snapshot.

It was not. We let the rays actually travel through four supplied spherical geometries. We kept
the reciprocal field, time dependence, common scale, and radial shift active where each test
history supplied them. Along the way the metric itself continually changed how the two transverse
directions focused.

The flat control stayed perfectly round. Every radial ray stayed round. Every nonflat family gave
tilted rays a small but finite difference between their two screen directions. A completely
separate neighboring-ray calculation reproduced the result.

In the orchestra metaphor: we did not add an angular instrument or volume knob after the music
was played. Different viewing directions heard different chords because one supplied metric was
already evolving all of the relevant spherical-base instruments together.

This is a real simplification, but still a bounded one. We have shown that the local same-history
bridge survives finite, time-live propagation on the declared controls. We have not yet shown
which complete history Nature uses, what happens in the full nonspherical coframe, or what an
observational sky must look like.
