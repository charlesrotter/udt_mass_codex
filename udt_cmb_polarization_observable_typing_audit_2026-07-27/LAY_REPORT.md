# Lay report

The CMB polarization really can become a useful guidepost—but it cannot choose the missing UDT
geometry for us yet.

The metric already knows how to define a two-dimensional viewing screen and how geometric arrows on
that screen would rotate while they are carried along a supplied path. But a geometric arrow is not
automatically polarized light. UDT still needs to identify the physical thing being polarized and
derive how matter creates it.

There is also an important difference between a sky map and a power spectrum. A power spectrum adds
together all patterns of the same angular size. It remembers how much pattern exists at each size,
but forgets much of where it was and how it was oriented. We constructed two different directional
maps that give exactly the same spectrum. That means a direction-dependent UDT effect could be
invisible—or ambiguous—in the ordinary spectrum.

The most informative future tests would therefore be:

1. whether polarization rotates differently in different directions;
2. whether normally separate angular directions become correlated;
3. whether full, unaveraged polarization correlations depend on the actual pair, path, and screen
   orientation.

The familiar `EE`, `TE`, `BB`, `TB`, and `EB` spectra remain valuable later, but their detailed
peaks and amplitudes mix geometry with how the radiation was produced, foregrounds, lensing, and
instrument calibration. Matching them now would invite us to tune one of the unresolved metric
extensions toward the answer.

So the answer is: **not too far out, but downstream**. CMB polarization gives us an excellent future
falsification contract. It does not replace the present task of deriving the complete angular/
mixing extension and physical observer/path/screen domain from UDT itself.
