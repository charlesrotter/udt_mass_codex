# G162 lay report

The last step found that we had been asking one hidden knob to do two different jobs.

Imagine two orchestras playing the same chord. One orchestra may be turned slightly relative to the
other. That relative turn matters if you track how individual players or directions line up. But it
does not change the chord you hear.

The pair metric is the chord. Its clock/ruler imbalance, common volume, shift, conditional
`c_eff/c_E`, and bounded position are unchanged by the leftover Lorentz turn. So the scalar
reciprocal kernel does not need us to choose that turn. This removes a false missing piece.

The turn is not meaningless. If a physical route or overlap is supplied, it records frame memory:
how the individual clock/ruler directions were carried. Normal-plane rotation and lensing/Jacobi
channels are other instruments again and remain separately typed.

What remains open is more basic: which complete metric history Nature realizes, which observer
query or route is physical, and how the history reaches its global or `X_max` completion. G162 did
not solve those questions. It made them smaller by proving that one previously suspected unknown
does not belong inside the scalar kernel.
