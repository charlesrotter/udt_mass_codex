# Lay report

We took the exact supernova curve developed using Pantheon+, locked every part of its shape, and
played it against a different recording: the five-year Dark Energy Survey supernova release.

The curve stayed broadly on pitch. DES would nudge its one shape number from about `1.056` to about
`1.015`, but the difference is only roughly `1.6 sigma`. That is ordinary dataset-to-dataset
movement, not a contradiction.

There is an important wrinkle: the fit is almost *too* good. The scatter is smaller than one would
normally expect from a simple count of the data points and the published covariance. Because these
are heavily processed supernova measurements, the familiar chi-square ruler is only approximate
here. We have not derived which part of the release processing explains the low scatter, so the
honest label is “the Pantheon curve survives, with a low-scatter warning,” not “UDT wins.”

This substantially reduces the worry that months of Pantheon work simply trained us to one
catalogue. It does not make P1 metric-derived, determine the size of the universe, or validate the
complete UDT orchestra.

A fresh outside reviewer rebuilt the calculation from the sealed public data, confirmed all central
numbers and the correct covariance handling, and accepted exactly this limited conclusion.
