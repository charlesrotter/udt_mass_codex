# Lay report

The map found work to do, but almost none of it is honest computer-scanning work yet.

Imagine seven partially assembled instruments. One has a single unresolved adjustment that can be
measured on a workbench: the F01 lambda-Schur sign. That is the one small CPU-ready check. It would
tell us whether one already-defined part adds another unstable direction. It would not certify the
whole instrument because a different wall-germ adjustment remains loose.

Several other instruments are missing pieces of their design drawings. F02 and F07 need one actual
assembled configuration, not merely compatible diagrams. F04 needs the native rule telling it how
to evolve through physical time and what its real boundary is. F05 has a closure shape but no native
rule for how that shape responds to disturbances. Running GPUs on those objects now would require us
to draw in the missing mechanisms ourselves.

So “blocked” means “queued for the missing derivation before computation.” It does not mean rejected.
Five families remain in active derivation queues. F03 stays useful as a control, while F06 remains a
precise example of a configuration that cannot exist under its current premises.

The practical result is encouragingly small: no extensive GPU survey is indicated, one narrow CPU
check can be designed later, and the remaining work has been organized by the exact missing piece
rather than by the hope of finding a particle.
