## Purpose

Before a broad stability or solution scan, attempt to fill the upstream mathematical objects missing
from the five active development families. “Sweep” means complete coverage of the registered gaps,
not a shared solver, shared action, or search for favorable outcomes.

This proposal must receive its own preregistration and source freeze. It is not executed by the
present survivor-map audit.

## Dependency order

Ask whether the metric, finite-cell structure, Reciprocity, and current bootstrap hypothesis derive
or forbid one common nonzero static/time/angular live on-shell field, one native whole equation, one
compatible differentiable boundary, and its tangent/variation domain.

This group comes first because it is upstream of any stronger F02 stability statement and of the
formal F07 module assembly. A shared zero mode or formal embedding is not a witness.

### Q02 — F01 germ ownership

Independently of the lambda-Schur CPU candidate, determine whether deeper N4, period, holonomy, seal,
or complete-cell structure fixes the second wall germ. Explore every metric-admissible branch; do not
invent a germ pin to complete a preferred certificate.

The separately preregistrable lambda-Schur sign check may proceed as a bounded CPU tile, but neither
its sign nor the germ result may be promoted to whole-chain, time-live, or physical stability.

### Q03 — F05 response and variation domain

Ask whether the metric supplies a native response and perturbation domain for the ring/completion
family. The existing period and mass-exclusion identities do not constitute a stability functional.
No P4 or Hopfion operator may be transferred into this family.

### Q04 — F04 native time and physical boundary

Only after the upstream response/ownership work, ask whether UDT derives a physical time-response law
and physical finite-cell carrier boundary for the conditional Hopfion. The supplied round `S2`,
`L2+L4`, computational box, and static operator remain explicit premises rather than native answers.

## Required products

Each group must return:

1. an exact object/type statement for the missing cell;
2. a premise and source ledger;
3. all admissible branches, including negative and nonunique branches;
4. a constructive witness or a scoped obstruction/countermodel;
5. a dependency update stating which later stability questions become well-defined;
6. an explicit `CPU_READY`, `GPU_READY`, or still-blocked ruling with certification requirements.

## Fail-closed rules

- Do not import a common action, response, carrier, time equation, or boundary.
- Do not identify formal compatibility with a realized solution.
- Do not use numerical convergence to define a missing continuum object.
- Do not discard a family because its derivation remains open.
- Do not merge the four groups merely because they share words such as stability or boundary.
- Regrade every premise-scoped negative if its controlling premise changes.

## Maximum conclusion

The sweep may enlarge the set of honestly stability-testable families. It may also determine that
some cells remain underdetermined. It cannot itself derive a particle spectrum, matter, mass,
bootstrap selection, or common stability operator. GPU work begins only for a family whose native
equation, domain, boundary, residual gate, CPU anchors, and independent validation route all close.
