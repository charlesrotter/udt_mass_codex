# CBR1 decision brief — complete the diagnostic branch

The simulated measurement test works within its declared scope. Starting with
actual calculated clock exchanges, it reconstructs enough curvature information
to test a necessary part of the candidate response equation. It recovers the
positive control, rejects an unrelated curve at withheld points, and preserves
the warning that scalar agreement can conceal failure of the full equation.

This was the next step already recommended after CMF1. Charles's atomic-clock
question was a paraphrase, not an instruction to adopt atomic anomalies as a
goal. No hardware experiment, empirical anomaly or physical scale was sought.

What changed: we have moved from an ideal measurement formula to a working finite
numerical diagnostic, with saved readings and independent reconstructions.
What remains: these are supplied comparison geometries. The positive case was
created using the candidate equation, so recovering it is a test of the measuring
procedure, not evidence that nature or UDT selects that equation.

The main practical lesson is precision cost. Taking smaller measurement steps
can reduce one geometric approximation while magnifying reading errors. In the
fixed synthetic design, errors as small as 1e-12 in log tick ratios already
leave the candidate slope unresolved under the conservative bound. This is not
an atomic-clock specification: units, physical preparation and usable scale
have not been established.

The recommended discussion is now about the physical connection, not a larger
version of this benchmark. RCD1 audited the missing implication; PSC1 found
that the weak scalar response permits other nonlinear completions; CMF1 and
CBR1 supplied its independent readout and numerical test. None supplies a new
reason for identifying this particular response with UDT's positional geometry.
Repeating those calculations with more favorable curves would not add one.

Retain this diagnostic as a tool. Before another research dispatch, identify a
specific UDT implication or independently justified observation that could
distinguish the candidate from the known surviving alternatives, and check its
novelty against RCD1/PSC1. The acceptable alternative is to leave the response
unselected and return to a different native geometry route. The present result
does not prove that an extra founding postulate is necessary.

No physical adoption is requested. No successor is launched. Resources used
were short CPU runs and two separate review contexts; no GPU or long solve.
The exact equations, test thresholds, exposed tuning, review limitations and
saved evidence are in the fixed CBR1 package and the central development.
