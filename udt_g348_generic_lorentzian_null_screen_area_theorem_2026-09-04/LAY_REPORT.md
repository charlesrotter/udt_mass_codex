## What we learned

The recent sky-area behavior was not a lucky feature of the one Taub/Kasner spacetime we had been
using. It follows from the geometry of any smooth spacetime metric along any supplied regular light-
cone path.

The metric itself supplies a two-dimensional transverse “screen” along the path and tells nearby
paths how to move across that screen. The complete position-and-direction transport remains
reversible and internally consistent for arbitrary curvature and arbitrary ordinary endpoint
observers.

## The important new qualification

In a general spacetime, neighboring paths can focus together at a conjugate point—a caustic. At
that point the measured infinitesimal cross-sectional area becomes zero. This does **not** mean the
spacetime or the complete transport has broken. It means only that a particular endpoint chart,
which tries to recover initial direction from final position, can no longer be inverted there.

With two transverse directions there are two possibilities:

- one direction focuses: the signed area changes orientation across the crossing;
- both directions focus: the area has a double zero and the orientation does not reverse.

The metric's conserved reciprocity relation forces those crossing orders. Higher-order ambiguous
crossings do not survive on a regular null path.

## What this does not do

This remains infinitesimal causal geometry. It does not yet say how much light a source emits, how
a finite beam carries energy, what a detector records, or which path and observers Nature
populates. It supplies no observational distance, universe history, matter, scale, or `X_max`.
The kernel, metric, angular cancellation, and adopted response equation were not changed.

Fresh external review independently rebuilt the hard parts and accepted this bounded result
without mathematical repair. It did not turn the result into a theory of light or a selected
universe.
