# G249 lay report

The clock and angular instruments really do interlock, but they do different jobs.

Redshift tells us a ratio: how one observer's clock compares with another's. The angular sector
tells us how a bundle of neighboring directions spreads into an area. The metric combines those
outputs exactly through `r/A`.

What `c_E` supplies is the exchange rate between seconds and metres. An exchange rate does not tell
you how much money is in the account. In the same way, `c_E` converts a clock interval into a ruler
interval, but redshift provides only a ratio of clock intervals—not one absolute interval.

The exact calculation found that one can enlarge the whole metric by a factor `ell` while leaving
the complete dimensionless redshift story and angular shape unchanged. The physical angular area
then grows by `ell^2`. So the interlock can hear the scale, but does not choose its numerical value.

There is a substantial simplification: after the full dimensionless metric history and branch are
known, the angular response is not another freely fitted orchestra function. The metric's Jacobi
equation fixes its normalized evolution. Only one absolute scale anchor remains in this bounded
family. One reliable absolute area, time, or length datum could calibrate it; the remaining data
would then be tests rather than additional fit coefficients.

Also, one redshift value is not enough to predict angular response. The response hears how the
metric is changing around that depth and along the whole ray. Where depth turns around or several
routes reach the same observer, the answer remains branch-labelled rather than a single curve.
