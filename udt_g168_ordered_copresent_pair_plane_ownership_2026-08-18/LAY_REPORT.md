# G168 lay report

The local mathematics is simpler than a path-selection law, but the cold review exposed one honest
remaining premise.

To evaluate a local pair with a metric, you must give it two directions: which way the observer's
clock points and which way the other observer lies. That tiny local object is the **pair germ**. It
is the first infinitesimal arrow of the relationship, not a route drawn through spacetime.

Once those two directions are supplied, the UDT metric does all the rest. It removes any clock component
from the separation direction, produces the physical ruler direction, and therefore fixes the
two-dimensional clock/ruler plane. If the separation points partly around the sphere rather than
only radially, those angular components automatically enter the same pullback before the reciprocal
kernel is read. Nothing is bolted on afterward.

What the current foundation cannot yet do is turn the names “observer A” and “observer B” into that local arrow.
Names are not a measurement. Even two fixed observer worldlines can be joined by different smooth
comparison surfaces with different initial directions. But UDT does not need to choose a whole
surface to evaluate a local pair: the supplied local arrow is enough.

So the simplification is:

```text
supplied local pair germ -> clock and separation directions
metric -> ruler + pair plane + angular participation
pair pullback -> one reciprocal response
```

The local pair-plane calculation is closed conditionally. What remains is not a path: it is the
physical ownership statement that completed UDT relations really supply these germs, followed by
the global question of which germs coexist and how their frames are carried between locations.
