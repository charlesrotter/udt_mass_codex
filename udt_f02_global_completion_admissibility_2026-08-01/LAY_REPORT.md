# Lay report

We tried to take the small F02 “mass-bearing” pattern from one open strip and close the strip into
every kind of loop or smooth finite cell already on the books.

Three things happened.

First, simply joining several ordinary strips does not help. One might try to give one strip an
uphill slope and the next an equal downhill slope. The total change would cancel, but the two
pieces hit the join with incompatible “tension.” Enforcing a smooth ordinary join under one common
rule makes the same tension pass through every piece, and then a closed loop forces every slope to
zero. One can manufacture cancellation by changing the rule itself between pieces, including
flipping the sign of the energy. But the project has not derived a lawful global join for that
construction, and it no longer lies in the locally positive sector.

Second, a smooth cap is even stricter. The angular circles have to close gently, like the rounded
tip of a drum rather than a cut edge. Their first slopes must be zero at the cap. F02’s profiles are
straight lines, so a zero slope at one cap means zero slope everywhere. That removes the nonzero
F02 quantity on the smooth capped branches.

Third, there is a possible mathematical escape: join charts with a flip, so “uphill” in one chart
is correctly read as “downhill” in the next. The algebra of the flip works. But the repository has
not yet derived the full rule telling all metric pieces and their derivatives how to cross that
join. Calling it a solution would fill in missing physics by hand.

So the honest answer is: the ordinary loops and smooth caps are ruled out for this affine F02
pattern; the open strip remains only local; twisted and mixed joins are genuinely undecided. This
narrows the problem substantially, but it is not yet stable matter or physical mass.
