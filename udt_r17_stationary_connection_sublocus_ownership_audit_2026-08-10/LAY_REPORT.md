# Lay report

The connection behaves like a small compass carried through the angular part of the R17 geometry.
The question was whether some stationary versions of the geometry make that compass especially
simple.

They do, but only on sharply defined special settings:

- If `phi` varies around the universe, the compass cannot be flattened onto the two-sphere beneath
  the Hopf fibers. Even the apparently special `lambda=-1` case fails globally once the whole
  compact `S3` is enforced.
- If `phi` is constant, the remaining possibilities form a clean one-number family. At isolated
  values the compass is completely flat. At a larger discrete set it rotates by a whole number of
  turns around each fiber, so an abstract quotient can be made.
- The stricter demand that it be exactly the ordinary tangent compass of the underlying Hopf
  two-sphere has no regular solution when the R17 twist is nonzero. Its formal solution sits at the
  point where the spatial slice degenerates.
- On the complete cell there is no half-way holonomy: the compass either has no net curvature at
  all or generates the full circle of rotations.

Most importantly, no choice of those special settings is supplied by the current authority and
core R17 sources that were frozen into this audit. They supply metric configurations and
identities, not an on-shell field equation. A broader repository census points the same way, but
the external reviewer correctly required us to keep that broader statement as supporting local
evidence rather than an independently closed repo-wide theorem. The six actual R17 witnesses all
live in the generic full-rotation class.

So we found real structure, but not a hidden selector. The correct lesson is not to force R17 onto
the elegant flat subset. The generic path-dependent angular carry is part of the geometry that a
later physical law must account for.
